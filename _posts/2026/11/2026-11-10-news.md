<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Our security forecast for 2027</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">By Chris Betz, CISO, Google Cloud</span></p></div>
<div class="block-paragraph_with_image"><div class="article-module h-c-page">
  <div class="h-c-grid uni-paragraph-wrap">

      <p>Today, we’re publishing insights on the cybersecurity trends and challenges we anticipate in 2027, including agentic threats, changing attack tactics, and the new security defense approaches required.</p><p>This year we’ve witnessed a fundamental change in cybersecurity. When the year began, only a few organizations were using LLMs and agents to find and fix vulnerabilities. Today, it’s happening at scale across enterprises.</p><p>Organizations expected a reality with agentic hacking where scale, speed and sophistication would outpace human-only response; today, we’re seeing it in practice. Organizations predicted software development at an unheard of pace; today, we’re securing software delivered in hours and days instead of weeks and months.</p><p>We have passed many milestones, and the world won’t go back — and we are all digging in to fully understand the new reality.</p><p>The year ahead promises to be challenging, exciting, innovative and will outpace 2026. In the year ahead, enterprise software will change as agility and speed provided by agentic development shifts us from a collection of static applications to a live fabric of autonomous agents that can manage pricing, negotiate supplier contracts, allocate capital, and reroute supply chains in real time.</p><p>To make this vision a reality, many organizations are rushing to build agents. It’s on us as an industry to ensure the development and operations models are designed to be trustworthy and safe.</p><p>The risks in 2027 won’t be just server outages or stolen databases. As the agentic operations world takes off, we also need to be concerned about the data and technology foundation that agents rely on.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">

        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">When an agent makes a consequential decision, you must be able to reconstruct the entire story: what inputs came in, how the agent reasoned through them, which tools it called, what parameters it passed, and why policy allowed it. Without that trace, you can&#x27;t debug it, you can&#x27;t validate its correctness, and you can&#x27;t trust it. And if you can&#x27;t trust it, you can&#x27;t scale it.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Karthik Narain</strong><br />

</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Security must evolve to protect the balance sheet through decision integrity. Every consequential action taken by an autonomous system requires a complete, tamper-proof decision trace, recording and tracking: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Inputs entered into the system.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">How the model reasoned through its decisions.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Which tools the model called, what parameters it passed, and why policy allowed it.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Collecting this data will help ensure that your teams can reconstruct, validate, and audit every automated decision before scaling.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Making the alert queue disappear, from chasing tickets to setting doctrine</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For decades, security operations centers (SOCs) have suffered from crippling alert fatigue — too many alerts compounded by legacy security tools that hand human analysts half-finished investigations. When autonomous threats can probe a network and move laterally in minutes, human defenders can’t manually stitch together logs across eight different consoles or approve individual defensive actions at microsecond speed.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6
      h-c-grid__col--offset-2 h-c-grid__col-m--offset-3 h-c-grid__col-l--offset-3">
      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">2027 is the year analysts stop chasing alerts and start shaping outcomes.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Ramya Chitrakar</strong><br />
                
                
                  vice-president, Engineering, Google Cloud Security
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">That broken alert queue disappears in 2027 by flipping the operational model. The alert becomes the final output of an investigation. Autonomous defensive agents will handle the data enrichment, correlation, and triage automatically, surfacing a verified conclusion rather than a raw ticket. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of chasing alerts at a keyboard, human analysts move up the stack to set strategic doctrine, define risk appetite, and verify automated actions built for rapid explainability — where any defensive decision can be understood by a human in 60 seconds and reversed in 30.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Moving Beyond Zero: Transaction-level trust and first-class agent identities</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As enterprises deploy thousands of custom agents across multicloud environments, flat trust architectures, shared human service accounts, and blanket administrator tokens are turning internal workflows into high-leverage entry points for prompt injection and lateral movement. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Traditional Zero Trust is too rigid and broad to govern autonomous software acting on its own.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6
      h-c-grid__col--offset-2 h-c-grid__col-m--offset-3 h-c-grid__col-l--offset-3">
      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">We need to extend the Zero Trust concept, not just at the network layer and the machine layer, but now down to the transaction layer.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Heather Adkins</strong><br />
                
                
                  vice-president, Security Engineering, Google
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The year ahead demands an architectural shift to </span><a href="https://blog.google/security/going-beyond-zero-a-new-paradigm-for-enterprise-security/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Beyond Zero</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> so we can extend verification down to every individual transaction, network packet, and point-in-time authorization. Every autonomous agent should be treated as a first-class identity with its own verifiable credentials, strict least-privileged scope, and open communication standards. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When backed by real-time autonomous reasoning, we can dynamically allow a transaction on Tuesday and block the exact same request on Wednesday if conditions look anomalous.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Closing the loop between live runtime and defect-free code</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The traditional divide between writing software and securing it is breaking down, while adversaries </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/responding-to-core-php-vulnerabilities-cve-2026-109618-cve-2026-109619"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">actively target the open-source libraries</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and external tools that coding assistants pull into codebases. Developers can no longer file a ticket and wait weeks for a manual security review, yet deploying hasty, unvalidated automated patches in production is just as dangerous to business continuity as the vulnerability itself.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6
      h-c-grid__col--offset-2 h-c-grid__col-m--offset-3 h-c-grid__col-l--offset-3">
      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">Adversaries aren&#x27;t just attacking model math and algorithms. They&#x27;re actually poisoning the open source software, supply chain, MCP tools, and external libraries that agentic coding assistants pull automatically into code bases.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Sandra Joyce</strong><br />
                
                
                  vice-president, Google Threat Intelligence
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In 2027, security becomes a closed-loop, continuous system embedded directly into the software delivery pipeline. By placing agentic reasoning inside developer tools, we can perform threat modeling and catch configuration errors side-by-side as code is written. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">It’s also crucial that we link live production environments directly back to source code so that defenders can trace a real-world exposure to the exact line of code, validate whether it is genuinely exploitable, and deliver a pre-tested fix at machine speed without breaking production.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Turning internal ground truth into a decisive defender advantage</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Concerns persist around attackers gaining the upper hand in cybersecurity, and many fear autonomous capabilities will only widen that gap in 2027. Adversaries are already compressing six months of reconnaissance into hours, and exploiting flaws before vendors even know they exist. Compounding the challenge for defenders, no single model can discover or patch every vulnerability across code and cloud on its own.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6
      h-c-grid__col--offset-2 h-c-grid__col-m--offset-3 h-c-grid__col-l--offset-3">
      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">The defender&#x27;s advantage is that attackers operate with fragmented external visibility and guesswork. Defenders own the complete ground truth, identity privilege graphs, internal network architecture, code lineage, and real-time runtime activity.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Sandra Joyce</strong><br />
                
                
                  vice-president, Google Threat Intelligence
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In the year ahead, defenders will use autonomous technology to capitalize on the one thing an outside attacker will never have: complete internal ground truth. While adversaries operate with fragmented external visibility and guesswork, defenders own the full picture of their identity privilege graphs, internal network architecture, code lineage, and live runtime activity. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By feeding that unified context into multi-model ensembles — such as pairing lightweight models for continuous scanning with deep reasoning models that cross-validate findings and eliminate false positives — organizations can spot and remediate toxic combinations of permissions and configurations that look harmless in isolation, before an external attacker can ever piece them together.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Countering asymmetric threat speed and the new extortion playbook</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Not every adversary will embrace machine speed the same way in 2027. While state-sponsored espionage actors often avoid loud, hyper-fast automated scripts that risk triggering alarms and blowing their long-term stealth, cybercriminals tend to go all-in on autonomous velocity. </span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6
      h-c-grid__col--offset-2 h-c-grid__col-m--offset-3 h-c-grid__col-l--offset-3">
      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">What we&#x27;re seeing right now is a variety of adversaries that are exploiting vulnerabilities before the vendors even know about it.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Charles Carmakal</strong><br />
                
                
                  CTO, Mandiant Consulting
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As endpoint defenses get better at blocking traditional file-encrypting ransomware, criminal crews are shifting to rapid, high-volume data theft and extortion — specifically targeting proprietary model weights, custom agent skills, prompt libraries, and cloud GPU compute (“</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/llmjacking-monetizing-compromised-cloud-models?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">LLMJacking</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">”).</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Preparation here means eliminating security silos. It’s a critical failure point to treat models, agent permissions, software supply chains, and cloud infrastructure as separate domains. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead, we want to unite them under a single, intelligence-led governance framework that allows defenders to lock down high-value autonomous assets and shut down extortion paths before data can leave the environment.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Red-teaming the agentic enterprise before adversaries attack</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In the rush to capture productivity gains in the year ahead, many enterprises are deploying custom agents directly into core business workflows without testing how those systems behave under adversarial pressure. That mistake leaves guardrails vulnerable to bypasses, scanner blinding, and indirect prompt injection.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6

      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">One of the things that the team&#x27;s been doing over the past few years is doing a lot of red team exercises… with those agentic solutions, so that we have the opportunity to identify and fix the issues before an adversary has the ability to do that against victim organizations.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Charles Carmakal</strong><br />
                
                
                  CTO, Mandiant Consulting
                
              </span>
            
          </cite>
        
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

</div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Continuous, proactive adversarial red-teaming must be a mandatory prerequisite before any autonomous workflow touches production. Systematically attempting to break into agentic systems, bypass safety controls, and manipulate model behavior prior to deployment is crucial to securing the agentic era.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Critically, model safety engineering should combine the results of those tests with frontline threat intelligence on novel attack prompts and evasion techniques. Defenders can then harden classifiers and neutralize new attack techniques before adversaries can use them against real victims.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Orchestrating specialized agent teams to prevent agent sprawl</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Relying on a single generalist agent to defend an enterprise will fail in 2027 because deep technical skills do not transfer across domains. For example, an agent built to reverse-engineer malware is not the agent that should reason about an identity blast radius. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As departments across the business rapidly spin up uncoordinated, standalone agents without centralized oversight, agent sprawl is emerging as the </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/transform/these-4-ai-governance-tips-help-counter-shadow-agents"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">next iteration of shadow IT</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, threatening to derail enterprise agent deployments through inadequate governance.</span></p></div>
<div class="block-pull_quote"><div class="uni-pull-quote h-c-page">
  <section class="h-c-grid">
    <div class="uni-pull-quote__wrapper h-c-grid__col h-c-grid__col--8 h-c-grid__col-m--6 h-c-grid__col-l--6

      <div class="uni-pull-quote__inner-wrapper h-c-copy h-c-copy">
        <q class="uni-pull-quote__text">Technological adoption cannot outpace architectural governance… We have to prioritize machine speed defense. Security cannot remain a manual checkpoint that slows innovation down.</q>

        
          <cite class="uni-pull-quote__author">
            
            
              <span class="uni-pull-quote__author-meta">
                
                  <strong class="h-u-font-weight-medium">Sandra Joyce</strong><br />

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Just as a well-coordinated human team beats a lone generalist, the year ahead belongs to orchestrated networks of specialized hunter, decoder, and responder agents, each operating with its own domain depth, memory, and dedicated tools. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Succeeding at scale requires building the governance and orchestration layer first. The key here will be to establish graduated autonomy, shared state, clean handoffs between specialized agents, and a central coordinator that knows precisely when to act autonomously and when to escalate to a human.</span></p>

<div class="block-paragraph"><p>Throughout my career in cybersecurity and infrastructure protection — both in government and now at Google Cloud — I have consistently seen that friction between new and existing technology is often a barrier to progress. Organizations globally are moving beyond integrating AI and LLMs, and are now utilizing AI to power autonomous, agentic workflows. Nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 are already using Gemini Enterprise to build, scale and govern agents. But as AI systems begin to plan, execute multi-step processes, and work alongside human teams, leaders are confronting this same tension between cutting-edge and legacy technology.</p><p>To understand how existing technology environments shape an organization’s ability to operationalize AI, Google Cloud partnered with Hypothesis Group to survey 1,250 enterprise IT and business decision-makers across the United States. ### The open AI-native stack on Kubernetes: From open models to distributed inference and agents

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/the-open-ai-native-stack-on-kubernetes-from-open-models-to-distributed-inference-and-agents/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/the-open-ai-native-stack-on-kubernetes-from-open-models-to-distributed-inference-and-agents/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 10:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-10 12:10:37

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Over the past decade, Kubernetes established itself as the standard for orchestrating microservices and containerized applications. Today, we're witnessing the next major architectural shift in distributed computing: </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">AI workloads moving from isolated research prototypes into large-scale, multi-tenant production fleets</strong>. <span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this post, we'll walk through the open-source stack that powers this shift and show you the sample manifests that you can try on your own Kubernetes clusters. You can also join us at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026 to see these projects in action through live demos and technical sessions.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For modern engineering teams, production AI is rapidly moving beyond generic calls to closed, black-box APIs. Organizations are realizing that their long-term competitive advantage lies in domain specialization. Platform engineers, ML researchers, and AI builders want the autonomy to run high-performing </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">open-weight models</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (such as the </span><a href="https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemma</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> family). They want to adapt them to proprietary enterprise datasets by using </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">reinforcement learning (RL)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and serve them with predictable latency, high throughput, and maximum hardware efficiency.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Whether it's scientific initiatives training domain models like </span><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/dolphingemma/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">DolphinGemma</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (an LLM helping scientists study how dolphins communicate) on acoustic data, legal platforms evaluating complex contracts, or engineering teams building autonomous coding agents, the objective is the same: </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">achieving frontier-level task performance at a fraction of the serving cost and latency.</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Yet, scaling RL post-training, stateful distributed inference, and autonomous agent execution on traditional container infrastructure introduces substantial hurdles:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Accelerator underutilization in RL</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: When you alternate between training and rollout generation, expensive GPUs and TPUs remain idle for most of the training run.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Cache-blind load balancing</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: When you use standard Kubernetes round-robin and least-connections routing, multi-turn agent calls are scattered across arbitrary pods and wipe out key-value (KV) cache locality.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Framework fragmentation</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: When you operate parallel, siloed compute clusters (such as bespoke </span><a href="https://www.ray.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Ray</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or </span><a href="https://slurm.schedmd.com/overview.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Slurm</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> setups) alongside Kubernetes, you fragment capacity and multiply maintenance toil.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agent security in multi-tenant clusters:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> When you execute untrusted, model-generated shell commands and scripts in shared environments, you expose clusters to sandbox escapes and privilege escalation.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">At </span><a href="https://rsvp.withgoogle.com/events/google-cloud-at-kubecon-north-america-2026?pli=1" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Google Cloud is addressing these challenges by introducing and expanding a unified, open-source AI-native stack across Kubernetes and </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE)</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> based on these components:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="http://openrl.dev" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">OpenRL</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (CNCF Sandbox):</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> A Kubernetes-native post training service that simplifies RL post-training and maximizes accelerator utilization.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://llm-d.ai/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">llm-d 1.0</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (CNCF Sandbox):</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> An open distributed inference control plane that provides prefix-cache-aware routing, disaggregated serving, and multi-tier KV cache management.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/add-on/ray-on-gke/concepts/overview"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Ray on GKE with KubeRay</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (CNCF Sandbox)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: A framework that provides enterprise lifecycle management, elastic autoscaling, and </span><a href="https://kueue.sigs.k8s.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Kueue</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">-driven scheduling for distributed Ray compute and data processing.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://ate.dev/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Substrate</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (CNCF Sandbox)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: An open orchestration runtime that provides lifecycle management and execution targets for multi-turn AI agent workflows.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://gvisor.dev/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">gVisor</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (CNCF Sandbox)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: A kernel-level container sandbox that delivers strong system call isolation when you execute untrusted agent code in multi-tenant clusters.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://pytorch.org/blog/introducing-torchtpu/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TorchTPU</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: A backend that provides first-class, native PyTorch support across Cloud TPUs and GPUs inside upstream </span><a href="https://vllm.ai/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">vLLM</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and </span><a href="https://docs.sglang.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">SGLang</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Bridge reinforcement learning and Kubernetes with OpenRL</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has become standard practice, domain excellence especially in multi-step reasoning, mathematical proofing, and autonomous tool use requires RL.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Historically, it's been difficult to bridge the world of ML experimentation with production Kubernetes fleets. ML researchers want to iterate rapidly in Python and PyTorch by using standard reward functions, while platform teams need multi-tenant governance, quota enforcement, and predictable scheduling.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To eliminate this friction, Google created </span><a href="https://openrl.dev" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">OpenRL</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and contributed it to the </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">CNCF Sandbox</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Architectural highlights of OpenRL</span></h3>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Clean 4-primitive Python abstraction</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Researchers write standard Python training code that's centered around four core primitives (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">forward_backward</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">optim_step</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">sample</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, and </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">save_state</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">). OpenRL translates these primitives into Kubernetes scheduling workflows without requiring developers to learn Kubernetes internals or manage infrastructure code.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Cooperative accelerator time-slicing</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: OpenRL interleaves training steps and rollout inferences on the same physical accelerators. This approach avoids the need to dedicate separate, static clusters to compute-bound training and latency-sensitive rollout generation.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Delta-weight synchronization</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Shipping full multi-gigabyte model weights over cluster networking after every optimization step creates massive network saturation. OpenRL transmits only parameter deltas to rollout workers, eliminating rollout cold starts.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Zero-config dynamic resource allocation (</strong><a href="https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/resource-management/dynamic-resource-allocation/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">DRA</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: OpenRL automatically profiles model context lengths, batch dimensions, and LoRA versus full fine-tuning configurations. It then dynamically bin-packs workloads across a heterogenous accelerator fleet by using native Kubernetes DRA claims.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Distributed inference at scale with llm-d 1.0</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">After you post-train a model, serving it serving it under interactive, bursty agent traffic presents a new set of distributed systems challenges. Standard ingress controllers treat LLM traffic as stateless HTTP requests, scattering sequential turns across different replicas and constantly rebuilding KV cache.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud, Red Hat, IBM Research, CoreWeave, and NVIDIA co-founded the project, with contributions from over 500 engineers across 25+ organizations. Today, </span><a href="https://llm-d.ai" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">llm-d 1.0</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> is the CNCF Sandbox distributed inference control plane that transforms standalone inference engines (like vLLM and SGLang) into a coordinated, cluster-wide serving fabric.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Core capabilities of llm-d 1.0</span></h3>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Prefix-cache-aware routing (</strong><a href="https://llm-d.ai/docs/dev/architecture/router/epp" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Endpoint Picker</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: The Endpoint Picker integrates into the </span><a href="https://gateway-api-inference-extension.sigs.k8s.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Kubernetes Gateway API Inference Extension,</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and GKE manages it natively through </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-gke-inference-gateway"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE Inference Gateway</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. The Endpoint Picker evaluates real-time KV cache residency across worker pods. In production deployments, it's </span><a href="https://llm-d.ai/blog/llm-d-v1.0-state-of-the-art-inference-ready-for-day-2" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">doubled KV cache hit rates</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> from 35% to 70% and </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/llm-d-1-0-an-open-source-stack-for-distributed-inference"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">cut median time-to-first-token (TTFT)</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> by 70-85% on multi-turn agent workloads.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">3-Tier KV cache offload</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: When accelerator high-bandwidth memory (HBM) nears capacity during long-context interactions, llm-d cascades KV cache blocks from </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">HBM → host CPU RAM → local NVMe SSD / Google Cloud Managed Lustre / Cloud Storage.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> This offload prevents expensive context recalculations.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">EPD Disaggregation with </strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">DisaggregatedSet</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Decouples </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Encoding, Prefill, and Decode (EPD)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> onto specialized hardware pools that are connected by high-speed interconnects. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">DisaggregatedSet is built </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">built on Kubernetes </span><a href="https://lws.sigs.k8s.io/stable/docs/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">LeaderWorkerSet (LWS)</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. It enables independent horizontal scaling and coordinated, zero-downtime rolling upgrades.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Native TorchTPU upstream support</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: PyTorch models run natively on Google Cloud TPUs inside upstream vLLM and SGLang through TorchTPU. Developers get day-zero parity across TPU v5e, v6e, and v7x alongside NVIDIA GPUs without the need to maintain private forks or rewrite pipelines.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Latency-tolerant async processing &amp; flow control</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: The </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">llm-d-async-processor component</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> pulls background, latency-tolerant tasks from Pub/Sub or Redis into cluster slack capacity. It applies strict traffic prioritization so background processing never degrades interactive user experiences.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Fleet observability with Prism</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Track cluster-wide throughput, inter-token latency (ITL), TTFT, and cache hit ratios by using </span><a href="https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d-prism" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Prism</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, the llm-d open-source benchmarking and performance dashboard.</span></p>
</li>
</ol>

<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strong isolation for AI agents: Agent Substrate &amp; gVisor</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As multi-turn AI agents advance from conversational assistants to autonomous systems that generate code, execute shell commands, and orchestrate external APIs running untrusted, model-generated code in shared clusters introduces serious security risks.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud is contributing </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Substrate</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and continuing core contributions to </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">gVisor</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> in the CNCF Sandbox:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Kernel-level sandboxing</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: gVisor provides a user-space kernel that intercepts and virtualizes system calls, which helps ensure that untrusted agent code can't compromise the host kernel or access neighboring tenant workloads.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Sub-second ephemeral execution</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Sandboxes spin up in milliseconds per agent turn, providing isolated execution environments for both RL rollout generation in OpenRL and live production agent workflows.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">4 月の Google Cloud Next で、同世代の 2 つの新しい特化型 TPU を</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/infrastructure/eighth-generation-tpu-agentic-era"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">紹介</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">しました。大規模トレーニング用の 8t とリアルタイム推論用の 8i です。TPU 8t と 8i は、前世代の TPU と比較して、それぞれ 最大 2.7 倍優れたトレーニング パフォーマンスと最大 80% 向上した推論の価格性能比を提供します。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">本日、その取り組みをさらに一歩進め、第 8 世代 TPU ポートフォリオを拡張します。大規模なコンテキスト ウィンドウとラックあたりで非常に高いコンピューティング密度を実現する TPU 8i Max と、安全で機密性の高い推論を実現する TPU 8tx です。Google Cloud が 1 年間に 4 つの TPU システムを導入したのは今回が初めてです。</span></p></div>

<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">チップを中心にコンピュータを構築する </strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">本日、2 つの新しい TPU システムを発表します。新しいチップと新しいシステムの間には重要な違いがあります。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しいチップの設計は、物理法則と複雑で長期にわたる製造ライフサイクルを伴う複数年のプロセスにおいて、シリコン ウェハー上に数十億個の微細なトランジスタを配置することを意味します。一方、システムは、チップの周りにコンピューティング、ネットワーキング、ストレージをどのように集約するかに関係しています。新たなお客様のニーズや研究のユースケースに迅速に対応するために、それらをチップと一緒にどのようにパッケージ化するかがこれに含まれます。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">10 年以上前に最初の TPU を設計したとき、私たちは AI ソフトウェアがシリコン製造よりもはるかに速く進化することを理解していました。そこで、システムのバランスとモジュール性に根本的なアプローチを取り、 </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/ai-infrastructure?hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AI Hypercomputer</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> の一部である構成要素として TPU を設計しました。これにより、エンジニアはパッケージング、高速メモリ、光ネットワーキング、およびそれらの周囲のセキュリティを速やかに最適化できます。このアプローチにより、専用スーパーコンピュータを数年ではなく数か月で提供できます。 </span></p></div>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">セキュアでハイコンテキストな AI に特化 </strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">第 8 世代 TPU ポートフォリオの各システムは、 AI ライフサイクルの異なる部分をターゲットにしています。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">TPU 8t と TPU 8i は、この世代のチップのベースラインを確立しました。 </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">TPU 8t は、大規模な基盤モデル向けのトレーニングの原動力となるように設計しており、最大化された「グッドプット」（消費エネルギー 1 ワットあたりに提供する有用なインテリジェンス）を備えています。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">TPU 8i は推論エンジンであり、メモリの壁を取り払い、チップ間通信の遅延を半分までに削減するように設計しました。 </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">現在、企業が自律型エージェントを本番環境に導入する中、ハードウェア レベルのデータ セキュリティと数百万トークンのコンテキスト スケールという 2 つの差し迫った新たな課題を解決するために、これらの基盤システムに 2 つの新しい構成を作成しました。 </span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">TPU 8tx</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">第 8 世代の 3 番目のシステムである TPU 8tx は、安全で機密性の高い推論に最適化しており、小規模なトレーニングにも適しています。これは、最も機密性の高いデータとともにフロンティア モデルや独自のモデル、自律型エージェントを実行する、規制対象の企業や政府機関向けに構築しました。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">通常、データは保存時やネットワーク送信時には暗号化されていますが、コンピュータで処理する間はメモリ上で復号する必要があります。TPU 8t と同じシリコン上に構築し、カスタムの AMD CPU ホストと組み合わせた TPU 8tx は、セキュリティをハードウェア レイヤーに直接組み込み、現在使用中であっても機密データとモデルの重みを保護および分離した状態に保ちます。そして、リアルタイム推論用に作成したのと同じ低レイテンシの Boardfly ネットワークを採用することで、 TPU 8tx はマルチステップ エージェントを動かし続け、パフォーマンスの低下をほぼゼロに抑えながら、TPU 8t と比較してレイテンシを最大 2 倍低減します。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">トレーニング グレードのコンピューティングと高速な推論ネットワークを組み合わせることで、組織は単一のシステム上でモデルを同時に実行およびカスタマイズできます。TPU 8tx により、チームは別々のクラスター間で機密データを移行する必要なく、プライベート データを使用したモデルのファインチューニング、大規模モデルから小規模モデルへの蒸留、強化学習によるエージェントのトレーニングを行うことができます。 </span></p></div>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">TPU 8i Max は、コンピューティングとメモリを連動してスケーリングさせることで、 TPU 8i の 2 倍のコンピューティング、2 倍の SRAM、2 倍の HBM 帯域幅を提供するため、プロセッサに必要なデータが絶えず供給され、大規模なワークロードが停滞することはありません。さらに、TPU 8i Max は既存のラック占有面積内に 2 倍のシリコンを収容するため、データセンターは 1 平方フィートあたりおよび 1 ワットあたりで大幅に多くのコンピューティング パワーを導入できます。 </span></p></div>

<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">すべての開発者に TPU を開放 </strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">10 年以上前に最初の TPU を提供開始した当時、TPU の処理能力を必要とするワークロードを実行している企業はごくわずかでした。ユースケースは限られていたため、エンジニアは最適化したフレームワークと手作業で調整したコードを使用していました。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">年月が経ち、 AI が進歩するにつれて、より多くの企業がアクセラレータを利用し始めました。現在、 AI の普及により、幅広い企業やユースケースにおいて TPU は非常に優れた選択肢となる段階に達しています。その結果、私たちは TPU を汎用ハードウェアと同じくらい簡単に利用できるように、導入の障壁を取り除くことに取り組んできました。 </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">本日、こうした投資の成果を以下の主要な分野でお客様にお届けします。 </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">すべての PyTorch 開発者に TPU 開発を開放。コードの書き換えは不要：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 先月、 </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/topics/ai-infrastructure/torchtpu-is-now-open-source-run-native-pytorch-on-tpus?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TorchTPU</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> をオープンソース化しました。これにより、 PyTorch コードと Hugging Face モデルが TPU 上でネイティブで実行できるようになり、 vLLM や SGLang などの主要なオープンソース推論エンジン向けの、すぐに使える TPU バックエンドも提供します。さらに、ハードウェアの最適化を容易にするために、新しいオープンソースの </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/pytorch-on-tpu#tpu-agent-suite"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TPU agent suite</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> （現在プレビュー版）は、 Claude Code 、 Cursor 、 Google Antigravity などのコーディング ツールに直接接続し、数分での TPU オンボーディングの自動化、ボトルネックの診断、自然言語によるカスタム カーネルの自動チューニングを行います。本番環境のデプロイメント向けに、新しいオープンソースの </span><a href="https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/11/supercharging-xla-inference-xla-serving-engine.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">XLA Serving Engine</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を使用すると、 Python でモデルをトレーニングし、 TPU 、 GPU 、 CPU 全体の低レイテンシな C++ ランタイムで直接サービングできます。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">InferenceX および AgentX ベンチマークにおける優れた本番環境のパフォーマンス：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 本番環境で本当に重要なのは、理論上のチップのピーク速度ではなく、実際の AI ワークロードでの結果です。現実的なエージェンティック コーディングとロングコンテキスト作業を検証する、業界標準の InferenceX および AgentX </span><a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpu-inferencex-full-steam?utm_source=post-email-title&amp;publication_id=6349492&amp;post_id=213967688&amp;utm_campaign=email-post-title&amp;isFreemail=false&amp;r=2j6jwc&amp;triedRedirect=true" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ベンチマーク</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">において、 TPU 上のネイティブな vLLM-TorchTPU ソフトウェア スタック は優れた価格性能比を達成しました。</span><a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpu-strikes-the-king" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">新たな評価結果</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">は、高いモデル精度を維持しつつ、こうした利点が幅広いフロンティア オープン モデルやオープンソースのサービング スタック全体に適用できることを示しています。同じハードウェア上での今後のソフトウェア最適化により、これらの結果はさらに向上する見込みです。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">自律型エージェントのアイドル コンピューティングの無駄を排除：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> AI エージェントはステートフルであるため、データベースの検索、外部のソフトウェア ツール、または人間のフィードバックを待つ間、数秒から数時間、頻繁に一時停止します。これらの一時停止中にアクセラレータの電力効率を高めるため、新しい </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/topics/ai-infrastructure/agent-compute-engine-deep-dive?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Compute Engine</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> ソフトウェアは、アイドル状態のエージェント セッションを自動的に一時停止し、ミリ秒単位で使用中のメモリをチップから移動させ、運用のインフラストラクチャ コストを削減します。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">モデル ステート レイヤー (TPU Sync) でメモリの壁を突破：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> チップを中心にコンピュータを構築することは、ソフトウェアでメモリ、ストレージ、ネットワーキングを統合することも意味します。エージェントが数百万トークンのコンテキストと継続的な強化学習（RL）ループ全体で推論するにつれて、メモリはすぐにボトルネックになります。 TPU クラスターのモデル ステート レイヤーである </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/introducing-tpu-sync-fast-kv-cache-and-weight-transfer-on-tpu?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TPU Sync</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> をオープンソース化します。TPU Sync は、チップの HBM、ホストの DRAM、共有ストレージ、およびデータセンター ネットワークを 1 つの共同設計されたメモリ階層に統合します。これにより、ミリ秒単位で大規模な KV キャッシュをチップ外に自動的に階層化し、分離されたプレフィル ノードとデコード ノード間でコンテキストをストリーミングし、RL の重みを同期して、KV 転送レイテンシと Time to First Token (TTFT) を 90% 以上削減します。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">単一のコントローラーから TPU 全体で分散ワークロードをスケーリング：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) で</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/cloud-pathways-a-distributed-ml-runtime-is-ga?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">一般提供</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を開始した </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/workloads/pathways-on-cloud/pathways-intro?hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud Pathways</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> は、 Gemini の基盤となる Google DeepMind ランタイムをすべての Google Cloud のお客様に提供します。単一コントローラー アーキテクチャと</span><a href="https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/pathways-utils" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">pathwaysutils</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を使用すると、事前トレーニング、強化学習、マルチホスト推論のために、複数のスライスにまたがる大規模な TPU システムを、1 つのローカル マシンであるかのようにオーケストレーションできます。Cloud Pathways は、コロケーションした Python サイドカー、1 分未満のスライスのホットスワップ、エラスティックなリシャーディングにより、ハードウェアの中断が発生しても実行時間の長いジョブを動かし続けます。 </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE 動的スライシングによるカスタム TPU トポロジのオンデマンドな結合：</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 一般提供を開始した </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/dynamic-slicing"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE 動的</span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">スライシング</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">は、光回線スイッチ（OCS）テクノロジーを使用して、物理ハードウェアのプロビジョニングと論理スライスの形成を分離します。 TPU キューブをオンザフライでより大きなトポロジに動的に組み立てたり（スーパースライシング）、より小さな分離されたワークロードに分割したり（サブスライシング）することで、このアップデートは柔軟性に欠ける手作業でのノードプール再作成サイクルを不要にします。このトポロジの俊敏性により、ジョブの起動時間を最大 5 分の 1 に短縮し、障害復旧を最大 4.5 倍に加速し、すべての TPU にわたってフリート全体の稼働率とワークロードのグッドプットを最大限に高めます。 </span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="block-paragraph"><p>Throughout my career in cybersecurity and infrastructure protection — both in government and now at Google Cloud — I have consistently seen that friction between new and existing technology is often a barrier to progress. A new piece of software or service may work wonderfully on its own but then it’s placed within a complex, interconnected IT environment and lost in the operational inertia.</p><p>Today, enterprise technology is experiencing a historic transformation. Organizations globally are moving beyond integrating AI and LLMs, and are now utilizing AI to power autonomous, agentic workflows. Nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 are already using Gemini Enterprise to build, scale and govern agents. But as AI systems begin to plan, execute multi-step processes, and work alongside human teams, leaders are confronting this same tension between cutting-edge and legacy technology.</p><p>To understand how existing technology environments shape an organization’s ability to operationalize AI, Google Cloud partnered with Hypothesis Group to survey 1,250 enterprise IT and business decision-makers across the United States. The <a href="https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/the_innovation_paradox2026.pdf" target="_blank">findings</a> reveal a critical paradox: the legacy software systems enterprises rely on to deliver technology to their employees are simultaneously functioning as an innovation tax, actively holding back the AI transformation leaders are trying to achieve.</p></div>

      <h2><b>Legacy IT’s operational toll</b></h2><p>In our study, we defined “legacy” technology as software running locally on computers and servers rather than natively in the cloud. Across industries, decision-makers report that maintaining this footprint exerts a constant friction on their AI ambitions.</p><p>The consequences to AI are not theoretical. Nearly half (49%) of respondents report that relying on on-premises software has actively delayed, reduced in scope, paused or canceled AI or other technology initiatives.</p><p>Digging deeper, routine patch application and maintenance emerged as a primary operational bottleneck. As AI-enabled vulnerability discovery accelerates, the rate at which flaws are surfaced in legacy codebases has outpaced what human security teams can realistically address.</p><p>This creates a zero-sum game between routine maintenance of legacy systems and AI modernization. Instead of directing engineer cycles toward AI modernization, IT teams are spending their time and resources on maintaining legacy systems.</p>

<h2 class="public-DraftStyleDefault-block public-DraftStyleDefault-ltr"><span>Enterprise monoculture</span></h2>
<div class="Draftail-block--unstyled">
<div class="public-DraftStyleDefault-block public-DraftStyleDefault-ltr"><span>Why do organizations continue to rely on legacy environments that constrain their modernization? As our research shows, it is not necessarily architectural preference — but the result of vendor lock-in.</span></div>

In our study, 39% of decision-makers report that reliance on legacy software and cloud providers forces them to navigate complex licensing rules or contracts, or limited negotiating flexibility.</span></div>

<div class="public-DraftStyleDefault-block public-DraftStyleDefault-ltr"><span>Even more concerning is the systemic operational risk created by single-vendor concentration. Concentrating operating systems, productivity applications, cloud hosting and emerging AI models within a single vendor's monoculture creates an immense single point of failure. An autonomous AI agent is only as secure as the operating system and network it traverses. When that underlying stack is vulnerable, organizations are not deploying resilient intelligence; they are expanding their attack surface.</span></div>

<div class="block-paragraph"><h2>Accelerate agentic technology</h2><p>Delivering this level of autonomous agility across the enterprise requires modern technology built to support the agentic era. Here are a few ideas to get there:</p><ul><li><b>Demand architectural and provider choice:</b> AI and cloud must never be held hostage by the legacy software running local servers, email or desktop operating systems. Organizations need the commercial and architectural freedom to select best-of-breed IT on its own merits.</li><li><b>Commit to secure-by-design foundations:</b> The industry must move past the broken cycle of releasing insecure software and patching it after the fact.
