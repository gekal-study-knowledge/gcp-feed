<hr />
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Executive Summary </span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As threat actors </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">increasingly move beyond basic prompting</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to deploying autonomous multi-agent </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/artex-and-friends-enter-agentic-offensive-frameworks"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">frameworks</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, automated vulnerability exploitation pipelines, and alternative monetization schemes like </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/llmjacking-monetizing-compromised-cloud-models/?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">LLMJacking</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, the adversarial AI landscape has reached a critical inflection point. In this update, Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) and Mandiant examine how adversaries across the cyber crime, espionage, and information operations spectrum are operationalizing generative AI to compress attack timelines, scale operations, and execute multi-stage intrusions at machine speed.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Key trends over the last quarter include:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Shift to Autonomous Workflows and Multi-Agent Frameworks:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Adversary tradecraft has continued to transition beyond manual prompting to deploying autonomous multi-agent offensive frameworks—such as </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/artex-and-friends-enter-agentic-offensive-frameworks"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ARTEX</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, and PentAGI—to execute multi-stage intrusions at greater operational velocity.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Automated Vulnerability Research and Exploitation:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Threat actors are increasingly leveraging LLMs and agentic frameworks to fully automate the reverse engineering of patches via differential analysis, allowing them to rapidly weaponize n-day vulnerabilities and scale exploitation pipelines against internet-facing servers without human intervention.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Monetization and Infrastructure Abuse</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Financially motivated threat actors continue to profit from </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/llmjacking-monetizing-compromised-cloud-models"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">LLMJacking</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> by stealing LLM credentials and cloud resources from compromised enterprise environments and reselling access to high-performance AI models through underground proxy services (e.g., LLM Mixers).</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLM Integration in Information Operations (IO): </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">State-sponsored groups, commercial Influence-as-a-Service (IaaS) vendors, and inauthentic political monetization networks now routinely use LLMs in their operations to produce synthetic media at scale, create localized personas, and automate cross-platform distribution.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Targeted Espionage and Supply Chain Evolution:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Cyber espionage groups are leveraging LLMs for target research, automated data analysis, and novel supply chain attack vectors—such as manipulating autonomous coding assistant configurations to drop backdoors during local execution.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Grounded in telemetry from frontline Mandiant incident response engagements, global threat actor tracking, and live platform defenses, this report details how state-sponsored espionage groups, financially motivated cyber criminals, and IO threat actors are operationalizing LLMs in the wild.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">At Google, we are committed to developing AI boldly and responsibly. Our multifaceted defense strategy integrates proactive model-level safeguards, specialized threat intelligence, and targeted containment protocols to protect our customers and infrastructure. We continuously harden our models against misuse, mitigate malicious activity through</span><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/combatting-ai-scams/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;"> proactive disruption</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> of bad actor projects and accounts, and use our autonomous </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/security/ai-threat-defense?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google AI Threat Defense</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> architecture and </span><a href="https://blog.google/security/going-beyond-zero-a-new-paradigm-for-enterprise-security/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">BeyondZero</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to operationalize security, continuous monitoring, and responding at machine speed across enterprise environments.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Multi-Agent Frameworks in Cyber Intrusions</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While previous milestones documented in our </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">quarterly research</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> highlighted nascent adversary interest in agentic capabilities, GTIG and Mandiant have now </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/artex-and-friends-enter-agentic-offensive-frameworks"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">observed</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> multiple threat actors abusing open source, agentic penetration-testing (pentesting) frameworks to automate discrete phases and specific operational tasks during live intrusions.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Threat actor adoption of these frameworks represents a paradigm shift towards intrusions occurring at machine speed, with the automated chaining of complex attack paths scaling operations significantly. While the mechanics of the intrusions remain the same, such as using compromised credentials and targeting unpatched systems, the speed and scale at which frameworks can exploit these known weaknesses introduces a new defensive challenge. </span></p></div>

<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">CAIRN:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> an open source, multi-agent pentesting framework that implements an OODA (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act) loop where specialized agents collaborate via a shared "blackboard" to autonomously navigate from an initial parameter (e.g., a target IP address) toward an operational objective. </li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">HexStrike AI</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: an open source offensive Model Context Protocol (MCP)-integrated harness that enables LLM clients to directly connect to over 150 pre-installed offensive security tools. Operating through a dual-component architecture, the framework wraps standard utilities across reconnaissance, web application testing, exploitation, and binary analysis into callable functions coordinated by more than 12 specialized autonomous sub-agents.</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </strong></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">PentAGI</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: an open source autonomous offensive framework designed to lower technical barriers for entry-level operators running complex campaigns. Between late August and early October 2026, GTIG tracked a threat actor using a customized attack orchestration platform built off of the open source version of PentAGI used to conduct systematic reconnaissance and vulnerability scanning against a wide range of organizations in Asia. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We have observed several threat actors separately experimenting with the use of these frameworks and harnesses, including financially motivated, as well as suspected espionage threat actors with a PRC nexus. These observations additionally extend to the use of other tools and services, such as ZeroHollow, an inference service used for exploit development and LLM routing, and Agent Zero, an open source agent framework which features autonomous code execution and dynamic tool generation.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Given the low barrier to entry, ease of integrating new exploits, and massive scaling potential, we believe it is highly likely that threat actors will continue to use these frameworks in campaigns targeting organizations globally. Concurrently, as capabilities evolve, we suspect these operations will continue to move further along the autonomous scale, approaching fully agentic intrusions. Notably, while these frameworks are capable of significantly compressing attack timelines to machine speed and achieving operational objectives, they currently trade stealth for velocity. Unrestricted autonomous execution generates forensic traffic, relies on recognizable identifiers, and leaves behind distinct code-level traces; while these artifacts increase the likelihood of detection and mitigation, these tools remain highly capable of achieving operational objectives. As the sophistication of these tools and underlying LLMs increases, we anticipate not only reduced human involvement in these operations but also increased stealth and sophistication of the activity.</span></p></div>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Use of Agents in Vulnerability Research and Development </span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The integration of autonomous LLM agents into vulnerability research and software security workflows has shifted from theoretical experimentation to active operational deployment. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Adversaries are leveraging multi-agent systems and LLM-driven pipelines to automate labor-intensive phases of vulnerability analysis, such as differential patch examination, dynamic protocol fuzzing, and proof-of-concept (POC) weaponization. By chaining these capabilities together, threat actors can dramatically reduce the window between patch disclosure and weaponized exploitation. Threat actors may find it more efficient to use LLMs to rapidly weaponize patched n-day vulnerabilities, as they can focus research on a finite set of source materials: comparing source code versions (including from pre-release or preview channels), and examining release notes, security research, and POC code related to specific flaws. In contrast, searching for zero-day vulnerabilities requires casting a wider net. LLMs are transforming how vulnerabilities are discovered and exploited across public and enterprise infrastructure, shrinking the patch window for organizations. Simultaneously, the lower barrier to entry has fueled a wave of LLM-generated exploits that are claimed to work but don't, creating additional verification work for defenders.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For instance, in September 2026, GTIG </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">analyzed</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> vulnerability statistics to assess how LLMs are shaping the threat landscape. We found that LLMs are transforming not only the speed of vulnerability discovery and exploitation, but also the types and risk profiles of the flaws being uncovered. Interestingly, while overall disclosures and exploits nearly doubled between January and August 2026, zero-day exploits rose only marginally from a 2025 average of 8 per month to 11 per month in 2026—before spiking to 22 in the month of August.</span></p></div>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Full Integration of LLMs into Information Operations</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </strong></h3>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Commercial Development of Turnkey, LLM-Enabled Influence Operations</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In August 2026, GTIG investigated threat activity leveraging the Gemini API alongside tools from multiple AI providers to build an end-to-end IaaS platform designed to target public sentiment on regional political topics, primarily across Europe and the Middle East. The platform was engineered to automate the entire IO campaign lifecycle, including persona creation, multimodal media generation, automated content distribution, and interactive posting across platforms. Identified prompts reflected an operational focus on emulating organic grassroots engagement (i.e., “astroturfing”) while evading platform detection rules.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Identified prompts instructed models to adopt localized personas specific to targeted geography, mimicking regional dialects and slang, while incorporating informal phrasing and intentional typos to avoid sounding like an automated tool. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Prompts supporting media generation introduced deliberate camera flaws simulating realistic photos typically captured by smartphone cameras (e.g., making images appear grainy or incorporating uneven lighting changes) and omitted background text to avoid synthetic image markers.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To evade social media platform defenses, operators routed traffic through residential proxies and preserved saved login cookies. This setup was designed to allow automated tools to maintain active, authenticated access continuously, avoiding security checkpoints like CAPTCHAs and login re-verification.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GTIG also detected a separate commercial contractor leveraging the Gemini API—as part of a modular, multi-provider AI architecture—during the development of a turnkey IaaS platform engineered to manage over 100,000 accounts across major global platforms. The platform is architected to combine automated agents, anti-detection and device identifier spoofing tools, and cloud phone farms to operate thousands of inauthentic personas across platforms. Observed prompts indicated the actor’s plan to market the tool to both the actor’s domestic government bodies and foreign clients for influence campaign execution.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To manage large volumes of social media accounts, the platform was designed to run virtual smartphones in cloud environments—disguising each virtual device as a legitimate mobile device—and route web traffic through proxies to bypass automated bot detection and rate limits.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The architecture additionally includes automated fake account creation using disposable email addresses and overseas virtual SMS numbers. To avoid further suspicion, newly-created accounts were configured to undergo an automated aging period, leveraging LLMs to simulate realistic user behavior (e.g., how long a user lingers on a post, scrolling, and interacting with non-campaign content) to establish a history of normal activity before pushing targeted messaging.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h4><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">Tactical LLM Adoption: Demographic Microtargeting and Content Scaling</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Alongside the development of IaaS software suites, threat actors continue to integrate commercial LLM tools into baseline content workflows to overcome linguistic barriers, customize messaging, and scale output. Using commercial models to produce commentary, synthetic personas, and stylized animations at the content layer allows IO actors to improve the credibility and demographic resonance of their campaigns, while enabling IPM networks to mass-produce political clickbait for advertising revenue.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLM-Enabled Quality Enhancement in Long-Running IaaS Campaign:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Since our last report, GTIG observed a long-running IaaS provider tracked since 2019 leveraging LLMs to elevate the baseline quality and volume of its influence operations. Utilizing a proprietary software suite designed to manage thousands of synthetic profiles across major platforms, the provider targeted political discourse in multiple countries. Operators integrated LLM models to produce more convincing virtual personas, as well as academic-style commentary and media focused on political, religious, and civil rights topics. The resulting content was subsequently promoted and amplified across platforms using fabricated Western personas. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Demographic Targeting via Synthetic Content: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We have observed threat actors using LLM-generated content to customize context for specific regions and audiences, for example by using synthetic influencer personas to promote policy narratives targeting specific demographics. </li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLM-Augmented Inauthentic Political Monetization (IPM):</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GTIG also tracks a growing trend of IPM actors using LLMs to mass-produce political content. Although their thematic focus, quality, and volume can resemble state-backed IO campaigns, these non-ideological operations exploit societal polarization solely to maximize engagement, click-through rates, and advertising revenue. GTIG has observed IPM operators prompting commercial LLMs to identify lucrative political topics, trending keywords, and high-Cost Per Mile (CPM) niches for ad monetization, while promoting the resulting content via synthetic news anchors that occasionally spoof the likenesses of real journalists and commentators to manufacture credibility.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Threat Actors Abusing and Monetizing Access to AI Tools</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While compromised credentials have long served as the fundamental bedrock for initial access, privilege escalation, and lateral movement during intrusions, adversaries are increasingly repurposing stolen credentials and compute resources to power unauthorized LLM tooling and autonomous operational pipelines. As access costs, token rate limits, and geographic restrictions present persistent operational hurdles for threat actors attempting to scale LLM capabilities, adversaries are actively harvesting session tokens and platform credentials directly from victim endpoints and continuous integration environments. In tandem with this shift toward hijacking valid credentials for LLMs, underground cybercrime forums and illicit marketplaces have rapidly matured to meet this demand—transitioning beyond generic identity dumps to commodifying verified frontier AI accounts, tokenized API quotas, and targeted infostealer configurations as turnkey utilities for downstream exploitation.</span></p>

<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Monetizing LLMJacking through LLM Mixers</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Threat actors frequently monetize LLMJacking intrusions by selling access to compromised compute and LLM resources. In addition to the rise of LLMJacking, GTIG is tracking the proliferation of “LLM Mixers”—illicit storefronts that provide buyers with unauthorized access to LLM services and underlying proxy infrastructure. This infrastructure routes buyer traffic through a pool of stolen credentials and compute instances to ensure reliable access, while also offering the ability to spread query volume across multiple victim environments to reduce the likelihood of detection. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The LLM Mixer ecosystem mirrors the broader cybercrime marketplace, where specialized threat groups handle discrete phases of the supply chain. While LLM Mixer operators maintain infrastructure and customer fulfillment, for example, operators may partner with separate threat actors who establish initial access to compromised environments, purchase compromised credentials from infostealer operators, or recruit partners to systematically register free trial accounts. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GTIG analyzed numerous LLMJacking vendor advertisements on Russian- and Chinese-language</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">underground marketplaces, and found that most services offer access to multiple models, including frontier and jailbroken models that advertise support of malicious cyber activity. Advertised pricing varies significantly across individual mixer platforms and based on the models and types of credentials offered; for example, free trial credentials were much cheaper than enterprise. We observed prices per million tokens range from $0.10 USD to $25 USD. One vendor, APIMart, applies a 5x price increase on both input and output once a prompt exceeds 100,000 tokens. Rather than pricing per million tokens, some vendors such as JetNight offer monthly subscriptions at differing prices based on the type of model offered.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Threat Actors Continue Using AI for Productivity Gains</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Since our last </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">report</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, GTIG has continued to observe threat actors leveraging LLMs to augment various phases of the attack lifecycle. However, widespread adoption of LLM tooling also introduces significant operational drawbacks. The forensically noisy nature of many LLM tool processes can degrade OPSEC and compromise stealth, creating a detrimental impact on espionage campaigns. In addition, LLMs can reduce operational reliability, as adversaries must contend with model limitations, including the hallucination of non-existent details or even targets. Consequently, we anticipate that actors who specialize in stealthy operations designed to maintain long-term persistence are less likely to rely on forensically noisy tools within a targeted environment, though they may employ them selectively for speed and scale, depending on operational priorities. Conversely, we assess with high confidence that actors less concerned with operational stealth, including many cyber crime actors, will continue incorporating autonomous offensive tools into their operations, given that the increased reach of their campaigns will likely compensate for any decreases in success rates. </span></p>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Government-backed threat actors</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">State-sponsored threat actors continue to leverage LLMs in their respective operations, reinforcing baseline assessments communicated in our previous reports. For example, we continue to see CALANQUE ION, an Iranian government-backed actor previously tracked as </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/untangling-iran-apt42-operations?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">APT42</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, leverage LLMs—including Gemini—to augment reconnaissance and targeted social engineering. We also see PRC-nexus threat actors leveraging LLMs for reconnaissance and research. In addition to this new baseline of threat actors leveraging LLMs, we observed the following activity: </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In Q3 2026, PRC-nexus cyber espionage actors used LLM tools and models for reconnaissance, front-end scanning, researching targets across various sectors and geographies, and weaponizing exploits. </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We have also observed threat actors using LLMs to conduct post-compromise data analysis. This includes PRC contractor LEAKY CASTLE, which overlaps with </span><a href="https://www.wsj.com/tech/how-a-chinese-hacking-firm-tapped-ai-to-supercharge-cyber-spying-b577c846" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">public reporting</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> about data leaked from a contractor using LLMs to analyze, translate, and understand exfiltrated data, including deriving insights into organizational structures and adding them into finished intelligence reports to sell to customers.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In a new trend, we have observed PRC-nexus actors abusing LLM platforms and file-hosting backends for staging, dead drop C2s, and exfiltration. <p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We also continue to see threat actors from the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea (DPRK) using LLM tools to support DPRK IT worker activity, such as generating resumes and preparing for job interviews at Western organizations. DPRK threat actors are also abusing generative tools for operational scaling and exploiting native agent execution hooks as enterprise access vectors.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We observed the use of public facing tools to generate responses during job screenings, allowing them to sound authentic while posing as Western IT workers to secure remote employment.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While we continue to see DPRK actors leveraging LLM-powered tools to generate lure content and assist with coding, over the course of 2026 we’ve observed an evolution of tactics. In multiple intrusions, a suspected MIDNIGHT NEPTUNE threat cluster combined LLM-enabled social engineering and abused organizations' AI tools as a supply chain attack vector. </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In recent intrusions targeting cryptocurrency organizations in North America and Europe, the threat actor exploited LLM software architectures as a lateral execution surface. For initial access, the actor created fraudulent recruiter profiles on professional networking platforms whose profile imagery contained SynthID watermarks, confirming use of LLMs to synthesize deceptive professional identities. These personas directed targets to fraudulent meeting platforms featuring "ClickFix" social engineering lures, which prompted targets to run diagnostic fix scripts, which executed likely LLM-authored staging scripts via ClickFix lures. Following initial compromise, the actor pivoted into internal code repositories to execute a supply chain vector internal to the organization: modifying </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">.claude/settings.json</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> configuration files so that when users started up Claude Code, it would automatically trigger the download of the SOMBERMEME backdoor. To maintain stealth, suspected MIDNIGHT NEPTUNE further masked its lateral movement by generating GitHub Personal Access Tokens (PATs) named after Microsoft Copilot, underscoring how threat actors are simultaneously abusing generative tools for operational scaling and exploiting native AI agent execution hooks as enterprise access vectors.</span></p>

<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLM Toolkits Offer Turnkey Cyber Crime Solutions</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In developments in the last quarter, in addition to the use of offensive frameworks in financially motivated activity, we observed cybercrime actors leverage LLMs to scale tooling for clients; for example, Phishing-as-a-Service (PhaaS) operators using LLMs to create tools for low-sophisticated adversaries. The Jieyou phishing service, an active Chinese-language PhaaS platform first observed in March 2026, is primarily used by cybercriminals to deploy and manage highly convincing phishing campaigns, with a particular focus on e-commerce fraud. The service introduced an LLM chatbot assistant in its June 2026 backend update. This chatbot allows threat actors to easily configure phishing panels and modify existing templates using natural language commands, significantly lowering the technical barrier for deploying campaigns. The backend chatbot is model-agnostic and integrates with several frontier and open source platforms via APIs. The evolution of the Jieyou platform highlights a growing industry trend where PhaaS providers leverage LLMs as a modular force multiplier. Rather than manually editing payloads, low-skilled affiliates can now utilize natural language through the chatbot to dynamically customize pretext lures and phishing interfaces on the fly.</span></p>

<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Proactive Model and Platform Defenses</span></h4>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">※この投稿は米国時間 2026 年 10 月 29 日に、Google Cloud blog に</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/using-vpc-flow-logs-to-troubleshoot-gke-workloads?e=48754805&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">投稿</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">されたものの抄訳です。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Kubernetes 環境を効果的に運用するうえで、ネットワーク トラフィックを可視化することは不可欠です。サービスの速度が低下したり、接続が失われたりした場合、誰が誰と通信しているのかを把握することで、問題を迅速に絞り込むことができます。標準的なクラウド ネットワークでは、フローログが IP アドレス、ポート、プロトコル間の接続を記録することで、その可視性を実現します。しかし、Kubernetes では Pod は一時的なものです。Pod の IP アドレスは、Pod のスケーリング、クラッシュ、ノード間の移動に伴って絶えず変化します。そのため、10.4.2.18 が 10.4.8.44 と通信していることを示す未加工のログエントリからは、そのアドレスを所有していたワークロード（および Pod）、そのアドレスが存在していた名前空間、そのアドレスがアクセスしようとしていたアプリケーションについて、ほとんど何もわかりません。クラスタを検索する頃には、その Pod はすでに存在しない可能性すらあります。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud 環境では、ネットワーク層で</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/vpc/docs/using-flow-logs"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">VPC フローログ</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">がこの不足を補います。Google Cloud の仮想ネットワークは、Pod が行うすべての接続をすでに認識しているため、サンプリングされた各フローは、IP アドレスの背後にある</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/kubernetes-engine-overview"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">（GKE）メタデータ（Pod、ワークロード、名前空間、サービス）とともに記録されます。追加のエージェントやサイドカーをデプロイすることなく、L3 / L4 トラフィックのワークロード レベルのアトリビューションを取得できます。また、より詳細な情報が必要な場合は、すでに実行している可能性のあるクラスタ内のコンテナ ネットワーク インターフェース（CNI）とデータプレーンのオブザーバビリティ ツールを補完します。VPC フローログと GKE の連携の仕組み、およびその利用方法を詳しく見ていきましょう。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE メタデータが VPC フローログを補強する仕組み</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">VPC フローログは、Google Cloud の仮想ネットワーク内で直接トラフィックをサンプリングします。GKE クラスタをホストするサブネットでフローログを有効にすると、VPC ネットワークを通過する Pod トラフィックのすべてのレコードに、サンプリング時に IP アドレスを所有していた Kubernetes コンテキストがアノテーションとして付加されます。これにより、Pod がなくなった後もコンテキストが保持されます。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">クラスタ: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">クラスタの名前とロケーション。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ワークロード: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pod の名前と名前空間、および Pod を所有するコントローラ（Deployment、StatefulSet、DaemonSet など）。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">サービス名: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">トラフィックを受信した Kubernetes サービス。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ノードとゾーン:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 標準の VPC フィールド。フローの両端にあるノード VM とそのゾーンが含まれます。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE ワークロードを</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/app-hub/docs/overview?e=48754805"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">App Hub</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> に登録した場合、各レコードには、各エンドポイントのビジネス アプリケーション、環境、重要度を識別するアプリケーションレベルのラベル（</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">apphubSource</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> と </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">apphubDestination</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）も含まれます。これは、GKE マイクロサービスが、クラスタ外の App Hub 登録リソース（Cloud Run サービスなど）と通信する場合に特に便利です。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">始める前に</strong></h4>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ログバケットを</strong><a href="https://cloud.google.com/network-intelligence-center/docs/flow-analyzer/enable-log-analytics"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">オブザーバビリティ分析</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">（旧称、ログ分析）にアップグレードする: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Flow Analyzer は、アップグレードされたバケット（通常は </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">_Default</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）からのみ読み取ります。</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">注:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> このアップグレードは永続的です。</span></p>
<p><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gcloud logging buckets update _Default \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --location=global \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --enable-analytics</code></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">組織全体で VPC フローログを有効にする:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 組織レベルの構成 1 つで、将来のクラスタを含むすべての VPC ネットワークのすべてのサブネットをカバーします（ネットワーク管理者ロールが必要です）。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">include-all-metadata</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> をオンにして、すべてのレコードに名前空間と Pod の詳細が含まれるようにします。可視性と費用のバランスを取るために、セカンダリ サンプリング レート（</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">--flow-sampling</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）、集計間隔（</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">--aggregation-interval</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）、またはフィルタ式（</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">--filter-expr</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）を追加して、関心のあるトラフィックのみを表示できます。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gcloud network-management vpc-flow-logs-configs create CONFIG_NAME \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --location=global \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --organization=ORGANIZATION_ID \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --metadata=include-all-metadata</code></p>
<p><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/intranode-visibility"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ノード内の可視化</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">を有効にする:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Autopilot クラスタではすでに有効になっていますが、Standard クラスタでは有効にする必要があります。</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">注: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これを有効にすると、コントロール プレーンとノード コンポーネントが再起動され、ノードごとのオーバーヘッドが追加されるため、まず本番環境以外のクラスタでテストしてください。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gcloud container clusters update CLUSTER_NAME \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --location=LOCATION \</code><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">    --enable-intra-node-visibility</code></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これで準備は完了です。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Flow Analyzer でトラフィックを視覚的に分析</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">VPC フローログはすべてのレコードを Cloud Logging に書き込みます。ログ エクスプローラまたはログ分析で検索やクエリを実行できます。日常業務では、Network Intelligence Center の一部である</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/network-intelligence-center/docs/flow-analyzer/overview"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Flow Analyzer</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> が同じレコードをインタラクティブなグラフとテーブルに変換するため、数回のクリックでクラスタ、名前空間、ワークロード、Pod、サービスごとにトラフィックをグループ化してフィルタできます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">以下のチュートリアルでは、us-central1 の GKE Autopilot クラスタで、オープンソースのマイクロサービス デモである</span><a href="https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/microservices-demo" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Online Boutique</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を使用します。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">loadgenerator</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Deployment は、擬似的な買い物客トラフィックを </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">frontend</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> に送信します。frontend は、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">productcatalogservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">cartservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">currencyservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">recommendationservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">shippingservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">adservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">checkoutservice</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を呼び出します。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ケース 1: IP アドレスをワークロード名に変換する</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">インシデントを調査する場合でも、アーキテクチャの変更を評価する場合でも、最初の質問は常に同じです。どのエンドポイントがどの程度の量で通信しているか、というものです。</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE メタデータを使用すると、Flow Analyzer はエフェメラル IP アドレスのペアを提示するのではなく、Kubernetes ワークロード名を使用してこの質問に直接回答できます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Flow Analyzer での手順:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Network Intelligence Center</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> &gt; [</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Flow Analyzer</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] を開き、期間を [</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">直近 1 時間</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] に設定します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">[</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">送信元</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] と [</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">宛先</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] で、[</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">フローの整理の基準</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] を [</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE ワークロード</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] に設定します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">フィルタバーで、[</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE ワークロードが既知</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] フィルタを追加して、Pod 間のトラフィックのみが表示されるようにします。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">[</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しいクエリを実行</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] をクリックします。</span></p>
</li>
</ol></div>

<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">[</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 新しいクエリを実行</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] をクリックし、一番上の行の [</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">詳細</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">] をクリックします。</span></p>

<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">留意事項</strong></h4>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pod からインターネットへのトラフィック:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> VPC フローログには Pod アノテーションが含まれません。これは、フローがサンプリングされる前に、マスカレード エージェントが Pod IP をノード IP に置き換えるためです。内部の宛先をマスカレード以外のリストに保持すると、それらのフローの Pod 名が保持されます。ノード内の可視化で </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ip-masq-agent</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を実行する場合、接続の問題を回避するために、そのリストにクラスタのノードと Pod の IP 範囲も含める必要があります。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ブロックされたトラフィック:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> ネットワーク ポリシーによってブロックされたトラフィックは VPC ネットワークに到達しないため、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/network-policy-logging"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NetworkPolicy ロギング</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を使用して調査します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">サンプリング:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> フローログはサンプリングされるため、</span><a href="https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/vpc_flow_logs_understanding_byte_and_packet_counts.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">バイト数とパケット数</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">は、接続ごとのアカウンティングではなく、ランキングや傾向の把握に適しています。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">次のステップ</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">クラスタで VPC フローログがすでに有効になっている場合は、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">_Default</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> バケットをアップグレードして Flow Analyzer を開くだけで、上記の 3 つのチュートリアルを再現できます。詳細については、次のドキュメントをご覧ください。</span></p>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">企業のモノカルチャー</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">なぜ組織は、モダナイゼーションを制約するレガシー環境に依存し続けているのでしょうか。今回の調査が明らかにしたように、その理由はアーキテクチャの好みにあるのではなく、ベンダー ロックインの結果にほかなりません。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロバイダへの依存度が高いと、テクノロジーの選択肢が狭まるだけでなく、商業的な制約も生じます。この調査において、意思決定者の 39% が、レガシー ソフトウェアやクラウド プロバイダへの依存によって、複雑なライセンス ルールや契約への対処を余儀なくされたり、交渉における柔軟性が損なわれたりしていると回答しました。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">さらに懸念されるのは、単一ベンダーへの集中によって生じるシステム運用リスクです。オペレーティング システム、生産性向上アプリ、クラウド ホスティング、そして最新の AI モデルに至るまでを単一ベンダーのモノカルチャーに集中させることは、極めて重大な単一障害点を自ら作り出すことを意味します。自律型 AI エージェントのセキュリティは、それが動作するオペレーティング システムとネットワークのセキュリティに依存します。基盤となるスタックが脆弱であれば、組織は強靭なインテリジェンスをデプロイしているのではなく、自ら攻撃対象領域を広げているに過ぎません。</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph"><p><b>エージェント テクノロジーの加速</b></p><p>このレベルの自律的なアジリティを企業全体で実現するには、エージェントの時代を支えるために設計された最新のテクノロジーが不可欠です。以下に、そのためのアイデアをいくつかご紹介します。</p><ul><li><b>アーキテクチャとプロバイダの選択肢を求める:</b> AI やクラウドが、ローカル サーバー、メール、デスクトップ オペレーティング システム上で稼働するレガシー ソフトウェアに縛られてはなりません。組織には、製品そのもののメリットに基づいて最適な IT を自由に選択できるよう、契約上およびアーキテクチャ上の選択の自由が必要です。</li><li><b>安全性を重視した設計の基盤を徹底する:</b> 業界は、脆弱なソフトウェアをリリースしてから後追いでパッチを適用するという悪循環を断ち切る必要があります。ソフトウェア メーカーは、コードをリリースする前に脆弱性クラス全体を排除する、堅牢でレジリエントなアーキテクチャを構築する必要があります。これにより、人員不足に悩む IT チームを、日常的だが不可欠なパッチを延々と適用し続ける作業から解放できます。</li><li><b>AI を活用した防御を導入してレガシー デットを解消する:</b> すでに本番環境で稼働している数百万行もの脆弱なレガシー ソフトウェアに対し、組織は手動によるトリアージから、AI を活用したプロアクティブな防御に移行する必要があります。Security Command Center や Mandiant の脅威分析プラットフォームをデプロイすることで、チームはインテリジェンスを活用して、現在進行形の悪用パスを継続的に特定し、重大なリスクの優先順位付けを行って、複雑なハイブリッド環境全体での修復を自動化できます。</li></ul><p>エージェント型 AI がもたらす真価とは、業務の摩擦を自動化によって取り除き、人間の創意工夫を加速させることにあります。しかし、そのような未来は、数十年前のソフトウェアを基盤として構築することはできません。IT リーダーがロードマップを計画するにあたり、今こそ問うべきことがあります。それは、「自社のテクノロジー基盤は未来を加速させているのか、それとも過去を維持するためだけにメンテナンス費用を支払い続けているのか」ということです。</p><p>調査の詳細については、<a href="https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/the_innovation_paradox2026.pdf" target="_blank">こちら</a>をご覧ください。</p><p><b><i>-</i></b> <i>Google Cloud、リスクおよびコンプライアンス担当バイス プレジデント、</i><b><i>Jeanette Manfra</i></b></p></div>
