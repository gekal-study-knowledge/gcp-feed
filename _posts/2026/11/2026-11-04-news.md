### Leading in the agentic era: How to put people first as you adopt AI agents

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/lessons-for-adopting-ai-agents/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/lessons-for-adopting-ai-agents/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-04 02:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-04 06:30:07

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Over the past few years, our Google Cloud Consulting team has partnered with many companies to introduce AI into their daily operations.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In that time, we’ve seen the focus shift from generative AI that answers questions to agentic systems that fully carry out the work. These agents can create multi-step plans and manage a project from start to finish.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While budget, compute, and tooling can help launch an agent program, enterprise adoption takes intentional culture work. People need to feel empowered enough to experiment with agents, and leaders need to show teams what agents mean for their roles.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Based on our work across these organizations, we’ve put together five practical lessons for guiding enterprises through an agentic shift. </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Make workflows agent-executed, human-directed</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Most enterprise workflows run like a relay race: one person completes a task, checks it, and hands it off to the next person, who then does the same. Layering an AI agent onto that model gives you a faster runner for one leg, but the handoffs may stall or slow down other work. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Redesigning your workflows can remove blockers like these in a few ways: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strategic direction:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Move from human-in-the-loop validation (where a person checks each step) to a human-in-the-</span><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">lead</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> model. Agents take on the work, and people set the direction and own the outcomes.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Parallel dependencies:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> One agent can handle every dependency at once instead of passing work through a multi-person queue. For example, an agent can check product availability, credit limits, and contract paperwork at the same time without waiting on each step to finish.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Manage by exception:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Once the process is set up this way, the agent absorbs routine deterministic transactions, routing only ambiguous or high-judgment scenarios to human review. Cross-team stalls disappear, and your team stays in control.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. Turn hesitation into ownership</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In our experience, the biggest barrier to adoption is trust. Many employees worry about their value if an agent handles most of their day-to-day tasks. Others are concerned with who takes the blame if an agent makes a mistake. We encourage teams to treat agents the way they'd treat a junior team member:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Active delegation:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Frame agents as digital coworkers. That means assigning tasks to them, defining their scope, and checking their work for accuracy.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Clear responsibilities:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Set boundaries around which decisions an agent can make and which require human sign-off. When team members know exactly where accountability sits, they can better direct agents.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Comfort through exposure</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Confidence grows through familiarity. We recommend starting with low-stakes use cases (like scheduling internal meetings) to help motivate teams before moving to high-stakes operations (such as financial forecasting).   </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. Manage hybrid teams of people and agents</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Next, it’s important to support collaboration when you bring agents into your team. Let’s look at how this might work in practice,</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Focus on results:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Collaborate across teams to break down workflows at both the tool and task level. Cross-functional perspectives reveal an end-to-end view of the process, helping you pinpoint where agentic capabilities belong and how to prioritize ideas by technical feasibility and business impact.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Reinforce behaviors:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Top performers on a hybrid team are the people who guide their agents well. Reward team members for auditing an agent's reasoning and catching when its logic drifts from company goals.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Coach your teams:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Show your teams how to work with agents so people can spend more time on creative work and decision-making.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">4. Build agentic literacy through experimentation</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Your teams need to understand how agents solve problems. People who know where an agent's logic breaks down (and why) can guide and judge its output.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Beyond the tool:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Literacy covers both writing a good prompt and auditing the result. Agents choose the most likely path instead of following deterministic rules, so people need to know when to trust an agent's output and when to check it.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Safe learning zones:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Give business roles the same sandbox access that technical teams get to build agentic literacy across the whole organization.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Celebrating intelligent failure:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Experimentation is how teams learn. In our experience, we’ve seen that when teams build an agent that fails because the data wasn't accurate, that team then understands how important data readiness is. Even if it didn’t work out, this should still be encouraged so your teams keep experimenting. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">5. Govern agents with a hub-and-spoke model</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To roll agents out across an enterprise, you need central guardrails that don't slow down the teams closest to the work. A hub-and-spoke model does both: it gives teams a shared foundation to build on and room to move at their own pace.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The hub:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Establish a central AI council team that sets guidelines, maintains ethical guardrails, and manages a library of approved tools. That way, teams don't rebuild the same things. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The spokes:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Allow individual teams to adapt those tools to their own needs, experiment quickly, and give feedback on what works in practice.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Within Google Cloud Consulting, we use our internal </span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8S9AInJ9Ic" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Catalog</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to share low-code agents and skills that drive cross-team learning, innovation, and efficiency. However, you don’t need to build a custom platform to implement this. Here are a few approaches:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Filter and sort agents made by employees through a simple internal web app.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Publish and share agents with built-in Gemini Enterprise features like Agent Gallery.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Work directly with technical teams that help organizations create tailored solutions.   </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Lead with a people-first approach to AI</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Becoming an agentic organization is about your people. Give your teams systems and guardrails to move fast, room to experiment, and leadership that promotes accountability. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ultimately, the overarching goal is to build an organization where human ingenuity and agents support and amplify one another.</span></p>

### How Nammu21 automates syndicated loans on Google Cloud Universal Ledger

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/financial-services/how-nammu21-automates-syndicated-loans-on-google-cloud-universal-ledger/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/financial-services/how-nammu21-automates-syndicated-loans-on-google-cloud-universal-ledger/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-04 03:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-04 06:30:07

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The syndicated loan and private credit markets, both critical components of corporate finance, have long been constrained by manual processes, leading to multi-week settlements, valuation opacity, and trade friction. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To solve these long-standing challenges, fintech innovator </span><a href="https://www.nammu21.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> partnered with Google Cloud on a new proof-of-concept. Using its AI-powered platform to structure legal agreements and instantiate them as smart contracts on the </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/universal-ledger"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Universal Ledger (GCUL)</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Nammu21 automates the entire loan lifecycle, paving the way for improved settlement times and greater market efficiency.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Digitizing the loan market</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While loan markets provide billions in capital to businesses worldwide, their underlying infrastructure has not kept pace with the speed of digital finance. This landscape requires continuous coordination across facility agents, borrowers, and diverse lender syndicates, while also managing highly customized credit agreements with dynamic tranches and complex covenants. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In particular, relying on fragmented, manual interpretation of legal text often results in significant operational drag and data silos. According to the </span><a href="https://blog.lma.eu.com/challenging-the-timeline-issue-no-4/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Loan Market Association</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, secondary market settlement times for par/near par loans in Q4 2024 still averaged 42 days, with a median of 27 days. These notoriously slow timelines tie up capital, increase counterparty risk, and create a heavy reconciliation burden for participants.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21 transforms bespoke credit documents into programmable assets at scale by rendering each facility's governing terms as a Digital Credit Instrument (DCI). The DCI maps every term — including conditions, consents, eligibility criteria, and operational mechanics that determine what is permitted under a facility — directly back to its originating legal provision. These elements are then expressed as executable behaviours against a loan's live state. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To instantiate these instruments, the proof-of-concept utilizes the secure, high-performance execution environment of GCUL, an institutional-grade, permissioned distributed ledger that helps automate complex financial workflows to deliver near-instant transaction finality and predictable costs without the volatility or security concerns of public blockchains.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A purpose-built stack for digital credit</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Combined with Nammu21’s data intelligence, GCUL provides the foundational infrastructure for converting legal document logic into live, programmable contracts. Designed to handle the privacy, scale, and multi-party coordination required by institutional finance, GCUL provides a permissioned, high-performance distributed ledger that delivers deterministic, instant transaction finality for high-value financial settlements.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Programmable contracts on GCUL are written in </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, GCUL's domain-optimized programming language. As a strict, statically-typed subset of Python, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> is designed to be safe, readable, and auditable, making it ideal for processing complex financial logic.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For regulated financial institutions, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> offers distinct operational advantages over other ledger programming models. The language simplifies development by removing the need for complex memory management or workarounds to meet highly restrictive call stack limits, allowing engineers to focus on the financial logic itself. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GCUL also provides robust, built-in permissioning, requiring explicit user authorization before a contract can store data on their behalf. This solves the "unsolicited asset" problem — common on public networks — where unwanted tokens can be sent to an organization's wallet, creating compliance and accounting issues. With GCUL, institutions can maintain full control over which assets they interact with to support their high-value, highly regulated use cases.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21 serves a dual role in this architecture. First, the platform renders the DCI programmatically from the original legal agreement without manual document reading to establish the terms. Next, it derives </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contracts that define the structure of the obligation and govern the loan on GCUL, using the DCI to supply the terms that determine what the loan documents permit, require and block.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Technical architecture: The loan lifecycle on GCUL</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21’s loan implementation on GCUL demonstrates the value of creating a single, immutable source of truth. The proof-of-concept modeled a $1.945 billion Term Loan B — one segment of a broader multi-facility transaction — where the agent bank, borrower, transferor, and transferee all worked off a permissioned view of the same on-ledger rendering.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The lifecycle begins when an agent bank selects a specific credit agreement transformed by Nammu21. The platform instantly provides the structured terms from the document, which are used to instantiate a unique </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contract on GCUL, establishing a verifiable, on-ledger digital twin of that specific loan facility.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Transacting with familiar, regulated money on a programmable ledger provides an architecture purpose-built for institutional finance, grounded in three core pillars:</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Compliance via gated execution: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21’s </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contracts instantiated with governing terms, validate every action against the terms transformed from the agreement before execution, mitigating potential covenant breaches or invalid transfers upfront while eliminating the risk of silent failures. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Role-based privacy:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GCUL enforces strict, role-based access, ensuring that trading strategies, positions, and sensitive loan data remain confidential and are visible only to the necessary counterparties in a transaction.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Settlement finality: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Every on-ledger action completes near-instantly and anchors on canonical data derived from the original credit agreement, replacing provisional, reconciliation-heavy models with real-time, trusted verification.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The business impact of moving on-ledger</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This proof-of-concept illustrates a structural shift in how credit markets can operate. When each facility's governing terms are rendered as a DCI and executed on GCUL, the operational mechanics of lending markets change fundamentally, rather than incrementally. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Values can be reconciled bilaterally, but governance logic cannot – and the market’s current friction stems directly from each party's independent interpretation of the rules. Resolving this issue on-ledger is what enables settlement times to compress, back-office overhead to shrink, and counterparty risk to be mitigated by design rather than by manual checking. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This same architecture can be applied wherever credit terms are bespoke and manually interpreted. By freeing up capital that would otherwise be trapped in lengthy settlement cycles, new liquidity is unlocked and a more dynamic and efficient secondary loan market can emerge.</span></p>

### Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2 が Google Cloud で利用可能に

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">※この投稿は米国時間 2026 年 10 月 28 日に、Google Cloud blog に</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/gemini-robotics-embodied-reasoning-2-is-now-available-on-google-cloud?e=48754805&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">投稿</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">されたものの抄訳です。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">今年に入って、Google は </span><a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini Robotics 2</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を発表しました。これは、ロボットが現実世界で自ら考え、行動し、適応することを可能にする次世代のインテリジェンス レイヤです。このブレークスルーの中核を担うのが、</span><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-robotics-er-2/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">（ER 2）</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">です。これは、ロボットの高度な頭脳として機能する特化型 Gemini モデルです。Gemini Robotics ER 2 は、固定アームから等身大ヒューマノイドまで、あらゆる形態のロボットに対応します。高度な時空間の動画理解、長期的な計画能力、組み込みのセーフティ ガードレールを組み合わせることで、これを実現しています。これにより、ロボットは人間と自然に会話する、低レベルのアクション モデルやハードウェア API をオーケストレートする、数百もの意思決定を伴う数分間のタスクを実行する、複数のロボットチーム間で連携する、といったことが可能になります。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">このたび、Google はこの最先端のエンボディド推論モデルを企業の本番環境向けに提供開始します。</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini Robotics ER 2 は、Gemini プラットフォームにて公開プレビュー版としてご利用いただけます。</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これにより組織は、Google DeepMind による最新のロボット向け</span><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-robotics-er-2/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">エンボディド推論モデル</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">と、Google Cloud のエンタープライズ グレードのセキュリティ、マルチエージェント オーケストレーション、エンドツーエンドのライフサイクル管理を初めて組み合わせて活用できるようになります。安全で適応性の高い物理 AI のシミュレーション、評価、大規模なデプロイがかつてないほど容易になります。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">エンタープライズ ロボティクス向け物理エージェントの強化</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini Robotics ER 2 は、ロボットを物理エージェントへと進化させます。このモデルは、高度な時空間推論と長期的な計画能力を組み合わせることで、ロボットがタスクを完了するための手順をオーケストレートし、自己修正を行い、未知の状況にも適応できるようにします。エージェント環境を構築するために、開発者は視覚言語行動（VLA）モデルやナビゲーション API といった低レイヤの制御インターフェースをツールとして宣言し、マルチモーダルな動画、音声、テキストをモデルに直接ストリーミングできます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ロボットの開発および運用を行う企業にとっての主なメリットは以下のとおりです。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. 実作業タスクの検証、理解、自己修正</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">現場での実作業において、ロボットは次のステップへ進む前に、前のステップが実際に成功したかどうかを把握する必要があります。Gemini Robotics ER 2 は、連続する映像フィードをリアルタイムで推論処理することで、未知の状況や初めて直面する状況にもその場で柔軟に適応します。このモデルの主な機能は以下のとおりです。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">タスクの進捗状況をバイナリ ステータス、離散カテゴリ、または連続スコアに分類します。これにより、リアルタイムの状況認識精度が向上し、ワークフロー全体をやり直すことなく、失敗したステップのみをその場で調整およびイテレーションできるようになります。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">

### How Nammu21 automates syndicated loans on Google Cloud Universal Ledger

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/financial-services/how-nammu21-automates-syndicated-loans-on-google-cloud-universal-ledger/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/financial-services/how-nammu21-automates-syndicated-loans-on-google-cloud-universal-ledger/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-04 03:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-04 06:30:15

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The syndicated loan and private credit markets, both critical components of corporate finance, have long been constrained by manual processes, leading to multi-week settlements, valuation opacity, and trade friction. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To solve these long-standing challenges, fintech innovator </span><a href="https://www.nammu21.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> partnered with Google Cloud on a new proof-of-concept. Using its AI-powered platform to structure legal agreements and instantiate them as smart contracts on the </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/universal-ledger"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Universal Ledger (GCUL)</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Nammu21 automates the entire loan lifecycle, paving the way for improved settlement times and greater market efficiency.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Digitizing the loan market</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While loan markets provide billions in capital to businesses worldwide, their underlying infrastructure has not kept pace with the speed of digital finance. This landscape requires continuous coordination across facility agents, borrowers, and diverse lender syndicates, while also managing highly customized credit agreements with dynamic tranches and complex covenants. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In particular, relying on fragmented, manual interpretation of legal text often results in significant operational drag and data silos. According to the </span><a href="https://blog.lma.eu.com/challenging-the-timeline-issue-no-4/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Loan Market Association</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, secondary market settlement times for par/near par loans in Q4 2024 still averaged 42 days, with a median of 27 days. These notoriously slow timelines tie up capital, increase counterparty risk, and create a heavy reconciliation burden for participants.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21 transforms bespoke credit documents into programmable assets at scale by rendering each facility's governing terms as a Digital Credit Instrument (DCI). The DCI maps every term — including conditions, consents, eligibility criteria, and operational mechanics that determine what is permitted under a facility — directly back to its originating legal provision. These elements are then expressed as executable behaviours against a loan's live state. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To instantiate these instruments, the proof-of-concept utilizes the secure, high-performance execution environment of GCUL, an institutional-grade, permissioned distributed ledger that helps automate complex financial workflows to deliver near-instant transaction finality and predictable costs without the volatility or security concerns of public blockchains.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A purpose-built stack for digital credit</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Combined with Nammu21’s data intelligence, GCUL provides the foundational infrastructure for converting legal document logic into live, programmable contracts. Designed to handle the privacy, scale, and multi-party coordination required by institutional finance, GCUL provides a permissioned, high-performance distributed ledger that delivers deterministic, instant transaction finality for high-value financial settlements.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Programmable contracts on GCUL are written in </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, GCUL's domain-optimized programming language. As a strict, statically-typed subset of Python, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> is designed to be safe, readable, and auditable, making it ideal for processing complex financial logic.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For regulated financial institutions, </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> offers distinct operational advantages over other ledger programming models. The language simplifies development by removing the need for complex memory management or workarounds to meet highly restrictive call stack limits, allowing engineers to focus on the financial logic itself. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GCUL also provides robust, built-in permissioning, requiring explicit user authorization before a contract can store data on their behalf. This solves the "unsolicited asset" problem — common on public networks — where unwanted tokens can be sent to an organization's wallet, creating compliance and accounting issues. With GCUL, institutions can maintain full control over which assets they interact with to support their high-value, highly regulated use cases.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21 serves a dual role in this architecture. First, the platform renders the DCI programmatically from the original legal agreement without manual document reading to establish the terms. Next, it derives </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contracts that define the structure of the obligation and govern the loan on GCUL, using the DCI to supply the terms that determine what the loan documents permit, require and block.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Technical architecture: The loan lifecycle on GCUL</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21’s loan implementation on GCUL demonstrates the value of creating a single, immutable source of truth. The proof-of-concept modeled a $1.945 billion Term Loan B — one segment of a broader multi-facility transaction — where the agent bank, borrower, transferor, and transferee all worked off a permissioned view of the same on-ledger rendering.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The lifecycle begins when an agent bank selects a specific credit agreement transformed by Nammu21. The platform instantly provides the structured terms from the document, which are used to instantiate a unique </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contract on GCUL, establishing a verifiable, on-ledger digital twin of that specific loan facility.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Transacting with familiar, regulated money on a programmable ledger provides an architecture purpose-built for institutional finance, grounded in three core pillars:</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Compliance via gated execution: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Nammu21’s </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">gculpy</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> contracts instantiated with governing terms, validate every action against the terms transformed from the agreement before execution, mitigating potential covenant breaches or invalid transfers upfront while eliminating the risk of silent failures. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Role-based privacy:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GCUL enforces strict, role-based access, ensuring that trading strategies, positions, and sensitive loan data remain confidential and are visible only to the necessary counterparties in a transaction.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Settlement finality: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Every on-ledger action completes near-instantly and anchors on canonical data derived from the original credit agreement, replacing provisional, reconciliation-heavy models with real-time, trusted verification.</span></p>
<h2><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The business impact of moving on-ledger</span></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This proof-of-concept illustrates a structural shift in how credit markets can operate. When each facility's governing terms are rendered as a DCI and executed on GCUL, the operational mechanics of lending markets change fundamentally, rather than incrementally. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Values can be reconciled bilaterally, but governance logic cannot – and the market’s current friction stems directly from each party's independent interpretation of the rules. Resolving this issue on-ledger is what enables settlement times to compress, back-office overhead to shrink, and counterparty risk to be mitigated by design rather than by manual checking. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This same architecture can be applied wherever credit terms are bespoke and manually interpreted. By freeing up capital that would otherwise be trapped in lengthy settlement cycles, new liquidity is unlocked and a more dynamic and efficient secondary loan market can emerge.</span></p>
