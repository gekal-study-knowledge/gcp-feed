### Data + AI Anywhere: AlloyDB Omni integration with local models

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/alloydb-omni-integration-with-local-models/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/alloydb-omni-integration-with-local-models/)

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Open models like Gemma 4 from Google DeepMind are a great option to keep your data local. But how can a local AI model integrate with on-premises data?</span></p>
<p><a href="https://cloud.google.com/alloydb/omni?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB Omni</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Google’s 100% PostgreSQL-compatible database that runs anywhere, now integrates with locally hosted large language models (LLMs). With AlloyDB Omni, you can build agentic experiences on your own infrastructure and on your own terms. Whether it’s in the cloud, in your datacenter, or even on your laptop, you can have data plus AI anywhere.</span></p>
<h2><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Built-in AI for your local environment</strong></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB Omni is a downloadable, run-anywhere edition of AlloyDB that easily deploys to your own infrastructure, including on-premises. AlloyDB Omni packs powerful, built-in AI, including the same ScaNN indexing technology powering Google Search. When you pair AlloyDB Omni with a model, you can store vector embeddings at scale; search them semantically; and even query the database using natural language. You can use virtually any model you like, whether that’s a frontier Cloud-hosted model like Gemini or a local model. And with locally hosted models you maintain complete control over your data environment.</span></p>
<h2><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Connecting to Gemma 4, Qwen3.8, and other local models</strong></h2>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You can now connect AlloyDB Omni to local models like </span><a href="https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-4/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemma 4</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and </span><a href="https://huggingface.co/Qwen" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Qwen3.8</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. No matter which model you choose, the architecture remains the same.</span></p></div>

### How Sinch modernized its enterprise data warehouse from Snowflake to BigQuery to power AI

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/how-sinch-migrated-from-snowflake-to-bigquery-to-power-ai/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/how-sinch-migrated-from-snowflake-to-bigquery-to-power-ai/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 02:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-24 04:51:47

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As the scale of global communications grows larger and larger, enterprise platforms must process billions of interactions with customers across a complex network of touchpoints without breaking them. A prime example is </span><a href="https://sinch.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Sinch</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, one of the world’s leading cloud communications and customer engagement (CPaaS) companies, which processes billions of customer interactions, SMS routing events, voice calls, and transactional messages.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Managing massive international messaging traffic brings significant architectural challenges, so Sinch built a distributed, multi-cloud data footprint across AWS and GCP, with separate Snowflake instances running in the EU, US, and Australia. To eliminate data silos, curb monthly billing spikes, establish a single governed source of truth, and unlock the next generation of predictive AI capabilities, Sinch partnered with </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/consulting"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Professional Services</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to migrate its enterprise data warehouse to Google </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/bigquery"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">BigQuery</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here’s how we engineered Sinch’s new, modern, cost-efficient, and AI-ready platform architecture </span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The challenge: untangling distributed warehouses and escalating spikes</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Before it embarked on this modernization journey, Sinch faced several architectural and operational friction points:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Cross-regional data sprawl:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The company’s separate Snowflake instances in the EU, US, and Australia required intricate inter-region replication, which caused  multi-directional dependencies and complex cross-warehouse data management.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Resource spikes and commercial inefficiencies:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Heavy monthly billing runs drove severe compute spikes in the US, even as other regions had underutilized capacity.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">High-volume scanning vs. dialect disparities:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> A single US email event table could generate up to 100 TB of daily scans. Meanwhile, mission-critical SQL relied on Snowflake-specific concurrency and table-swapping paradigms for concurrency validation, which lacked 1:1 equivalents in cloud-native SQL.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">“Google Cloud's engineering expertise helped us overcome several technical challenges during our migration, particularly around optimising Spark partitioning and restructuring incremental data loads. This allowed us to identify more efficient approaches, improve performance and ensure we built a scalable, reliable solution for the future.”<br /></span><strong style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">Dan Peters, senior manager, Data Delivery, Sinch</strong></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Phase 1: validating architecture with a target MVP</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To mitigate operational risk and prevent downtime for Sinch’s critical enterprise workloads (including ERP and NetSuite integrations), we initiated a phased migration framework:</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">A use case pilot validation:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The migration kicked off with a dedicated use case MVP that spanned 20 tables and 125 GB of refined data and automatically validated end-to-end data fidelity.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Layered strategy for coupled pipelines:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Rather than attempting to cleanly slice interconnected business domains in the EU, the team adopted a layered approach —  migrating raw and base (bronze and silver) layers, establishing automated data ingestion, and stabilizing core entities before repointing consumption marts.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Orchestration consolidation:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Sinch consolidated 36 independent ETL workflows into a single parameterized orchestrator by using </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/workflows"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Workflows</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, which streamlined CI/CD deployment logic, and reduced operational overhead.</span></p>
</li>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Re-engineering for scale: key architectural pillars</span></h3>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Decoupled storage and optimized ingestion</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Sinch transitioned its data ingestion pipelines to Dataproc Serverless (running Apache Spark 3.5), which directly pulled refined data into native BigQuery tables from S3 and Databricks Unity Catalog. And to overcome batch quota limits and resource exhaustion on billion-row datasets, Sitch optimized jobs by increasing Spark repartitioning factors to 50.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For its incremental pipelines, the team eliminated data duplication while minimizing query read costs by replacing slow, keyless merge statements with an optimized insert-and-overwrite windowing strategy.</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. AI-assisted SQL and macro translation</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In order to accelerate the translation of more than 1,200 dbt models across global projects, Sinch utilized Google Cloud BigQuery Migration Service alongside a set of custom AI translation skills. Here’s how:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The team refactored Snowflake-specific table versioning and concurrency management paradigms into deterministic transaction blocks and view abstraction pointers. This ensured atomic updates and consistent data access.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Sinch leveraged specialized Google skills for other regional migration — specifically the </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">dbt-sf-to-bq-translator</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><a href="https://github.com/google/skills/tree/main/skills/cloud/dbt-sf-to-bq-translator" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">skill</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> powered by the underlying BigQuery Translation API — to automate the conversion of dbt models from Snowflake to BigQuery SQL syntax. This significantly reduced manual refactoring effort and error rates.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Concurrency checks that relied on row-level locking were redesigned into serialized batch validations.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Integer overflow risks in massive calculation tables were solved by adopting a tiered precision strategy (favoring </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">NUMERIC</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> over </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">BIGNUMERIC</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">), which cut storage costs and preserved absolute precision.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
