### How lemonflow built real-time agents for EV charging using Gemma and TPUs

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/startups/building-real-time-agents-for-ev-charging-using-gemma-and-tpus/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/startups/building-real-time-agents-for-ev-charging-using-gemma-and-tpus/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-12 02:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-12 08:18:00

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">Welcome back to The Blueprint, a regular feature where we highlight how Google Cloud customers are tackling unique and common challenges across industries using the latest AI and cloud technologies. We hope to inspire others looking to innovate in their work.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As the electric vehicle revolution gains steam in Europe, every charging session is a moment of truth, for the driver and for the transition itself. At </span><a href="https://lemonflow.ai" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">lemonflow</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, we build AI agents purpose-built for EV charging: support agents for drivers and monitoring agents for charging networks. Our support agents answer drivers around the clock in more than 90 languages, and today they help an EV driver every 90 seconds. </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Challenge</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Before we founded lemonflow, we worked closely with leading mobility players across Europe and saw first-hand how often charging goes wrong: Between 15% and 25% of EV charging attempts in Europe end in failure. Across Europe’s rapidly expanding EV market, where battery-electric car sales grew </span><a href="https://www.electrive.com/2026/01/27/eu-wide-new-electric-car-registrations-rise-by-30" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">30% in 2025</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and </span><a href="https://zevalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Toward-health-competition_Feb-2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">thousands of charge point operators</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> run public chargers, drivers who encountered stalled charging points were often left stranded or stuck in slow call center queues.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We set out to change that hands-on: to bring the cutting edge of AI to EV charging, and to build the world’s best conversational AI for distributed hardware, an automated platform that resolves real-time charging issues via phone, chat, email, and WhatsApp. However, handling live voice support created several technical requirements:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strict latency thresholds:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Frustrated drivers want immediate assistance. Our testing confirmed that serving latencies above 800 milliseconds risk conversational barge-ins, causing drivers to interrupt the agent and break the interaction flow.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Managed API bottlenecks:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Initially relying on managed LLM APIs, we faced latency deviations during peak hours (spiking from 1.0–1.2 seconds to over 1.7 seconds) and experienced HTTP 429 rate-limit errors when global usage peaked.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Compute-intensive conversations:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Every agent works from one comprehensive prompt, paired with dynamic tool calls that read and act on live charger data. This design creates intense bursts of compute demand during calls, making throughput on shared, managed capacity difficult to guarantee under peak loads.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Highly fragmented ecosystem:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> We needed a standardized conversational engine capable of bridging around 30 distinct Charge Point Management Systems (CPMS), the backends operators use to run their chargers, each with its own APIs, data models and quirks.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Hundreds of tailored agents: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">No two operators run support the same way. Each has its own tariffs, payment methods, escalation paths and hours for human backup, and most run several agents, for example one per country, brand or hotline, with behavior that can change by time of day. Each agent’s prompt is unique and must be served at full speed and improved without breaking any of the others.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To deliver ultra-low-latency voice interactions and guaranteed serving capacity, we needed direct control over our model hosting environment.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Solution</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We wanted every driver to get best-in-class support that feels effortless, almost magical: no queue, no menu, just a short conversation that ends with the car charging. Picture a driver on a long cross-border trip, pulling into a motorway service station at 11 p.m. She plugs in, but the charger won’t start: the previous session never closed properly. She calls the number on the charger and is answered in her own language. Within seconds, our agent spots the stuck session in the charger’s logs, restarts the charger remotely and stays on the line until power is flowing.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To get there, we had to solve our latency bottlenecks and secure dedicated compute capacity. We migrated from managed APIs to self-hosting the open </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Gemma 4</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> model on </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud TPU v6e</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> hardware in the EU.</span></p>
<p><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/startups/why-your-startup-needs-open-models-alongside-frontier-apis?e=0"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Like many startups using Gemma</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, the compatibility of these open models with Gemini allowed us to transition our existing prompt infrastructure with minimal modification.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Moving to self-hosted TPUs unlocked the more complete architectural control our engineering team required to establish a deterministic core layer that handles EV-specific guardrails, guards against prompt injections, and standardizes previously fragmented backend protocols into our internal format before ever reaching the LLM. Control had a second payoff: because the model and serving stack change only when we change them, every test result stays meaningful. That stability laid the foundation for Agent Coach, our background coaching engine for AI agents, described below.  </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We then developed an intelligent routing gateway to prioritize workloads according to operational urgency, serving live voice calls first, followed by web chats, and deferring automated re-simulations to low-traffic periods.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Custom optimizations to the open-source vLLM serving engine and memory tiering between the TPU and host memory let a single TPU slice keep many more operator agents ready for instant answers, without recalculating their context.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We also implemented automated fallback routing to self-hosted GPUs and Gemini Enterprise endpoints. Thanks to TPU reliability, Gemma answers more than 99.9% of all live conversation turns, and these fallbacks serve primarily as deployment buffers during system updates. Primary or fallback, every request is served from secure EU compute.</span></p>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Outcome</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For the e-mobility companies we work with, and for their drivers, the result is support that simply works: our agents typically answer every call, handle more than 80% of conversations end to end without a human handover, and earn 95% driver satisfaction. Behind the scenes, self-hosting Gemma on Google Cloud TPUs transformed our performance:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Rapid deployment cycles:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Prompt update and test cycle times dropped from 2–3 hours to about 10 minutes, a more than tenfold faster improvement loop.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Sub-second serving latency:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> We lowered our P50 latency to around 350 milliseconds and our P90 latency to around 600 milliseconds. That is roughly three times faster than our managed-API baseline and fast enough to sharply reduce customer barge-ins.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Zero throttling and flat latency:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Dedicated TPU infrastructure eliminated HTTP 429 rate-limit errors, and latency stays flat through peak afternoon call traffic.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Testing without limits:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> On dedicated capacity, every TPU cycle that live conversations don’t need becomes testing capacity, so our team can experiment and validate at will.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Built for scale: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Today, our agents support more than 40 charging networks in 17 countries, covering more than 250,000 charge points, around the clock. And this is just the beginning: the same platform is ready to take on many more networks and markets.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With additional compute capacity, we introduced </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Coach</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, a background engine that provides agents with relevant context during live calls and turns real conversations into permanent test cases, so every call makes the next one better.  We also added nightly automated regression testing across thousands of fixed conversational scenarios.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Looking ahead, we are preparing to move to Ironwood, Google’s seventh-generation TPU, as soon as it is available in our EU region. With six times the high-bandwidth memory per chip, Ironwood will multiply the number of operator agents a single TPU can keep ready in memory for instant answers.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ultimately, our goal is that no driver is ever left stranded at a charger. They get an instant, expert answer in their own language, the charger is rebooted remotely if necessary, and our AI monitoring agents catch the next fault before another driver ever notices. Open models on dedicated Cloud TPUs give us the speed and control to make that the standard across Europe.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Details</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Our platform runs two kinds of AI agents: support agents that talk to drivers in real time, served by Gemma on Cloud TPUs for minimal latency, and monitoring agents that analyze conversations and charger logs in the background on Gemini, so operators can fix faults before the next driver is affected. To keep its core platform consistent across many kinds of e-mobility companies, from charge point operators (CPOs) and e-mobility service providers (eMSPs) to charger manufacturers, lemonflow built a translation layer that converts varied CPMS interfaces into a standardized format.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When a driver calls the support line posted on a charger, the call reaches our Conversational Engine through the operator’s own hotline. The engine then pulls the charger’s live status and Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP) logs, the messages chargers exchange with their backend, from the operator’s CPMS in real time. Because charging stations often lack digital displays, drivers are frequently unaware of underlying issues like pending mobile 3D-Secure payment authorizations, session timeouts, charging cards not enabled for roaming, power reduced by local load management, or a car that ended the session at its own charge limit. Our engine feeds structured log summaries directly into Gemma on TPU to inform interactions with drivers.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Direct model control allows us to stream token output selectively. Partial text tokens stream instantly to our Text-to-Speech (TTS) engine, while structured tool-call tokens stream asynchronously to backend systems to execute real-world actions, such as remotely restarting chargers, unlocking stuck cables, stopping sessions that failed to end, sending drivers details via SMS or email, or notifying service technicians.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Deterministic security controls screen inputs before they reach the TPU-hosted LLM, blocking malicious prompts. This architecture is designed with European critical-infrastructure requirements in mind, and it still keeps conversations snappy, with a typical response starting in around 350 milliseconds.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In the end, all of this should stay invisible to the driver: they just get a fast answer and a charger that works. We’re proud to work with Google Cloud to make that the norm and help EV adoption keep growing.</span></p></div>

### Introducing new AI functions for AlloyDB: ai.classify and ai.extract

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/ai-classify-and-ai-extract-functions-in-alloydb-ai/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/databases/ai-classify-and-ai-extract-functions-in-alloydb-ai/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-12 02:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-12 08:18:00

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Transactional databases store vast amounts of unstructured text — clinical notes, customer reviews, support tickets, invoices, and property descriptions. Historically, working with this text meant building complex application code: You had to export data from the database, call external model endpoints, write custom prompt wrappers, parse unpredictable responses, manage retry logic, and update database records. This application-side loop creates operational friction. It introduces query latency, bloats application code, increases model token costs, and creates data compliance risks by moving sensitive information across multiple network boundaries.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We built </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/alloydb/ai"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB AI</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to bring Gemini world knowledge directly into your operational database. Rather than treating artificial intelligence as a disconnected external service, we integrated model intelligence directly into the database engine. Today, we are announcing the preview of two new SQL functions in the AlloyDB AI query engine — </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.classify</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.extract</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> — available in the </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">google_ml_integration</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> extension (version 1.6.1 or later) on PostgreSQL 18, allowing you to perform classification and structured extraction directly inside your database using standard SQL. Supported by smart batching for AI functions (previously known as AI Function Acceleration), these functions deliver production scalability and performance. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Now, let’s take a look at these new functions and how to use them.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Classifying text directly in SQL with ai.classify</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.classify</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> function categorizes text into user-defined classes without requiring manual prompt engineering. You can specify categories as a simple list of labels or provide a list containing label names and descriptions to guide model context.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Key capabilities include:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Single-label classification (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">output_mode =&gt; 'single'</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">) to assign the single best category</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Multi-label classification (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">output_mode =&gt; 'multi'</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">) to return all matching categories</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Category descriptions using 2D arrays to provide explicit context for each label</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Few-shot prompting by supplying inline examples</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Flexible execution modes for individual rows (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">TEXT</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">), arrays (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">TEXT[]</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">), and cursors (</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">REFCURSOR</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">)</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here’s a quick example showing how you can classify customer feedback directly within a SQL query:</span></p></div>
<div class="block-code"><dl>
    <dt>code_block</dt>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Extracting structured JSON with ai.extract</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.extract</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> function transforms unstructured text into structured JSON data matching your target schema — specified as a JSON schema or a text list of fields. It handles system instructions internally, saving you from writing prompt templates or response parsers.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Key capabilities include:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Schema flexibility supporting standard JSON schemas or plain text field lists</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Automatic transformation of unstructured text into machine-readable JSON</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Overloaded function signatures for single rows, arrays, and cursor batching</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here’s an example extracting clinical fields from free-form medical notes:</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Speeding up queries with smart batching</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Running AI evaluations row-by-row can slow down query execution. AlloyDB addresses this with smart batching for AI functions. When you use </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.classify</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.extract</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> in SQL </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">WHERE</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> clause expressions, the database engine automatically groups inputs into optimized micro-batches before sending them to Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform models.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Smart batching delivers significant performance and efficiency improvements:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Faster inference for query execution compared to row-at-a-time evaluations</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Reduces total model token spend by optimizing input prompt overhead</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Native classification and extraction functions support operational workflows across key industry sectors:</span></p>
<ul>

<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Healthcare:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Securely convert free-text clinical notes into structured records containing patient names, symptoms, and prescribed medications, keeping patient data safely within the confines of the database.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Financial services:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Extract vendor details, invoice dates, and line-item totals from scanned document text.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Legal technology:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Pull governing law, liability caps, and termination notice periods from contract documents.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Real estate:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Extract property types, bedroom counts, and price ranges from descriptive listing text.</span></p>

<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Knowledge graphs:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Extract entities and relationships from documents to populate PostgreSQL Property Graph (PGQ) structures.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3D Maps と Gemini で空間認識型のファン エクスペリエンスを構築</span></h3>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">どの地域でも、コーヒー、ファストフード、ピザ、コンビニエンス ストアなどの業態ごとに、それぞれ独自の基準が自然と形成されています。そのため、「評価が最も高いのはどこか」ではなく、「どこで地域の人々の期待を上回っているか」という質問が有効です。この違いをブランド マーケット フィットと呼びます。</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ブランド マーケット フィットを予測するために AKQA が調査した内容</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">この調査は、Google Maps Platform の 2 つのインサイト プロダクトである Places Insights と Population Dynamics Insights を使用して実施されました。</span></p>
<p><a href="https://mapsplatform.google.com/maps-products/geospatial-analytics/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Places Insights</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> は、数百万もの米国企業のスポット数や評価の集計データを提供します。BigQuery 上で自社のビジネスデータと Google の包括的なプレイス データセットを安全に結合することで、カスタムの統計情報を導き出すことができます。このデータから、米国のすべての郵便番号ごとに、日常的に利用される 15 の</span><a href="https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/placesinsights/place-types?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">プレイスタイプ</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">の平均評価を測定しました。その後、11 の主要ブランドの約 85,000 店舗をその地域の平均評価と比較しました。</span></p>
<p><a href="https://mapsplatform.google.com/maps-products/geospatial-analytics/?utm_experiment=13103221&amp;product_exp_arm=13103221#population-dynamics-insights" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Population Dynamics Insights</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> は、もう 1 つのデータを提供します。検索トレンド、モビリティ、環境データを統合し、プライバシーの保護に配慮した豊富なエンベディングを生成します。このエンベディングを自社の成果データと結合することで、店舗業績の予測やデータギャップの解消に役立てることができます。これにより、「同じような行動パターンを持つ市場は、プレイスを同じように評価しているか」というシンプルな問いを国全体のスケールで投げかけることができます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">答えは「はい」です。私たちはこの問いをあえて厳しい条件で検証しました。ある州におけるブランド フィットを予測する際、モデルには他の州のデータのみから学習させるという手法をとったのです。このモデルは、11 のブランドすべてで同じ傾向を示しました。</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">評価に占める割合はわずかでも、店舗評価の明暗を分ける「場所」</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">同じブランドが同じ地域で 2 つの店舗を運営している場合に、それらの店舗の評価が大きく異なるケースは珍しくありません。郵便番号が同じである 47,570 の同一ブランドの店舗を対象に測定し、それらのペアを比較すると、</span><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">評価を構成する重みの内訳が店舗j自身（79.6%）と場所（20.4%）であること</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">が正確に示されます（20.4% は 星 5 つのうち 1 つ分に相当します）。</span></p></div>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">同じカテゴリの 2 つのブランドが異なる場所で成功を収めている例</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">最も象徴的なのが、2 つのチキンチェーンの例です。同じ食品カテゴリに属しながらも、地理的な条件はほぼ正反対です。</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">チキンチェーン A</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">店舗が点在するアメリカの郊外や地方都市でその地域の平均を上回る評価を得ています。そこは、カフェやブランチ スポット、犬連れ OK のテラスではなく、ドライブスルーやガソリン スタンドが立ち並ぶような場所です。</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">チキンチェーン B</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">人口密度が高く、テイクアウト中心の地域で高い評価を得ています。そこはデリやコンビニが多く、駐車場よりも人通りが重視されるような場所です。</span></p></div>

<p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">ブランドの価値提案には「地理的条件」があります。今では、市場にリソースを投入する前に、その地理的条件を測定できるようになりました。</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">検証と限界</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これらのブランドが 1,445 の新しい店舗（モデルが構築されたときには存在しなかった店舗）をオープンするのを、2 年半にわたって追跡調査してきました。好意的な反応を予測した場所では、新店舗は予想以上に好評であり、冷ややかな反応を予測した場所では、予想よりも厳しい結果となりました。このモデルは、調査対象となった 11 のブランドすべてに当てはまりました。</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">同様に重要なのは、この分析によって明確になった「限界」です。</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">チェーンがどこに店舗をオープンするかは予測できません。それは不動産に関する決定であり、今回の検証でもその事実が裏付けられました。店舗がどのように受け入れられるかを予測するのであって、どこに店舗をオープンするかを予測するのではありません。</span></li>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">この分析は個人の好みについて示すものではなく、あくまで「場所」に関するものです。</span></li>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">原因を示すものでも、売上を測定するものでもありません。また、業績の悪い店舗を救うこともできません。80% の法則を念頭に置く必要があります。</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">このモデルを他のブランドで活用する方法</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">十分な数の店舗を持つブランドであれば、この手法で次の 3 つの実用的な問いに答えることができます。</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">自社の価値提案が、すでに期待以上の成果を出しているのはどこか？</span></li>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">（モデルの重みではなく）平易な言葉で表すと、これらの場所に共通する特徴は何か？</span></li>
<li><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">国内で、これらとよく似た場所は他にどこがあるか？</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これは、新規出店地の選定、テスト都市の抽出、そして地域にフィットする顧客目線の価値提案の策定に活用できる、新たな判断材料となります。従来の問いは、「ターゲット ユーザーはどこにいるのか？」でした。この手法により、「</span><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">ブランドがどこにフィットするか？</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">」という新たなレイヤが加わります。</span></p>
<p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">この手法は、Google Maps Platform の Places Insights（件数）と Population Dynamics Insights（人口エンベディング）を活用して開発されました。調査対象には、11 の QSR ブランド、約 85,000 店舗、米国における 33,791 エリアの郵便番号が含まれています。ここに示されたすべての結論は測定データに基づいており、その適用限界についても検証済みです。</span></p>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Speeding up queries with smart batching</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Running AI evaluations row-by-row can slow down query execution. AlloyDB addresses this with smart batching for AI functions. When you use </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.classify</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">ai.extract</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> in SQL </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">WHERE</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> clause expressions, the database engine automatically groups inputs into optimized micro-batches before sending them to Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform models.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Smart batching delivers significant performance and efficiency improvements:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Faster inference for query execution compared to row-at-a-time evaluations</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Reduces total model token spend by optimizing input prompt overhead</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
