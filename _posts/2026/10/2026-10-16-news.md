<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">Editor's note: Generative AI startups are moving rapidly beyond static clip generation to build interactive, real-time worlds. In this technical deep dive, we explore how AI startup </span><strong style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">Visko</strong><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;"> collaborated with Google Cloud TPU engineers to deploy Orbis — its continuously streaming Live World Model—on Cloud TPUs, going from initial kickoff to production handover in just 30 days.</span></p>
<hr />
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">What makes a live video world model so challenging to serve in production? The bottleneck isn't average throughput — it's beating a relentless real-time clock. Unlike offline video generators where a user submits a prompt and waits in a queue, a live world model streams continuously. Every new chunk of video and synchronized audio must finish rendering before the viewer finishes watching the previous one, or the world freezes.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Today, we're excited to share how </span><a href="https://visko.ai" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Visko</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, an AI startup pioneering interactive world models, teamed up with Google Cloud to solve this exact challenge. Working side by side, Visko and Google Cloud engineers deployed Visko's end-to-end live inference pipeline — combining video generation, super-resolution upscaling, and synchronized audio—onto a single </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/tpu?e=48754805"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud TPU slice</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Together, the teams brought end-to-end generation down, rendering at 1.07x playback speed.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here is an inside look at how we engineered the pipeline to hit that milestone in a single month, the architectural optimizations that moved the needle, and practical lessons for other startups building ultra-low-latency generative AI on Google Cloud.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Redefining interactive media with Live World Models</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While most generative video platforms focus on rendering fixed, pre-baked clips, Visko is building what it calls </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Live Models</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: video world models that generate continuously and respond on the fly.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Visko's flagship model, </span><a href="https://www.visko.ai/models" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Orbis</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, streams live video starting from a single text or image prompt. As the stream plays, viewers can enter new steering prompts in real time, dynamically evolving the scene without restarting or breaking continuity. For instance, in one test session, the livestream opens on a cat napping beside a crackling fireplace; when the viewer steers the scene to introduce a bowl of food, the bowl materializes seamlessly, and the cat stretches, stands up, and meows.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Since launching its public demo on September 1, 2026, Orbis has unlocked new possibilities across interactive entertainment, live commerce, AI companions, immersive education, and robotics simulation. To scale these interactive sessions reliably, Visko partnered with Google Cloud TPU engineers in August 2026 to port the Orbis live pipeline to Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU). Within 30 days, the full multimodal pipeline was running in real time on TPUs.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Visko’s ambition is to power a new generation of interactive worlds that people can step into and shape in real time. Streaming continuous video and audio under a strict clock is a fundamentally different system challenge than offline clip generation. Working side by side with Google Cloud's TPU engineers, we brought the full Orbis pipeline to TPUs in just 30 days — giving us the low-latency compute and memory bandwidth required to bring Live Models to creators and developers at scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">"Visko is bringing an exciting new class of real-time, steerable workloads to the Cloud TPU ecosystem. By co-engineering their live multimodal pipeline on a singleTPU slice using JAX, torchax, and custom Pallas kernels, we proved how quickly a lean startup team can go from PyTorch prototype to render at 1.07x playback speed on Google Cloud's AI Hypercomputer."<br /></span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">– </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Dave Elliott, Head of Developer Advocacy for AI, Google Cloud</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Live video is a hard-deadline problem</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When you serve an offline clip generator, latency is forgiving — the user waits once while the backend processes frames. A live world model has no waiting room.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Every single chunk must be denoised, decoded from latents, upscaled to 1664x936 (at 18 fps), and paired with generated audio faster than realtime playback speed, for as long as the session runs.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Two structural constraints make live world models far more demanding than standard batch inference:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Batch size of one:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Each live stream represents an individual viewer's interactive session. You cannot mask latency by grouping dozens of concurrent requests into a massive batch — the standard playbook for keeping AI accelerators saturated.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strict sequential dependency:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Every chunk depends directly on the one before it. Because the video model maintains a rolling memory of recent video latents so that chunk N+1 smoothly continues the action from chunk N, you cannot pre-render chunks out of order or parallelize across future timestamps.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For startup infrastructure engineers coming from LLM serving, it helps to think of live world modeling not as a tokens-per-second throughput benchmark, but as an </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">unforgiving per-step latency SLO</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Architecting the multimodal pipeline on a single TPU slice</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To keep infrastructure lean and cost-effective as Visko scales, the team designed the live pipeline to chain three distinct generative models on a single </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/multislice-introduction"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud TPU slice</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> running in BF16 precision.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When a viewer submits an initial prompt or a live steering update, all three generative models — the core video diffusion model, the super-resolution upscaler, and the audio generator — share the same chips, taking turns on the hardware for each chunk.</span></p></div>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Inside the stack: Three optimizations that unlocked real-time streaming</span></h3>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Ulysses context parallelism across all TPU chips</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A video chunk expands into a massive sequence of spatiotemporal tokens inside the video transformer. To distribute that workload across the TPU chips at batch size 1, the team implemented </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ulysses context parallelism</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of splitting model weights, Ulysses splits the token sequence across chips. Immediately before the attention layer, a high-speed all-to-all interconnect exchange regroups the tensors so that each chip holds the </span><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">entire</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> video sequence, but only for one-quarter of the attention heads. After each chip computes attention over the full sequence for its assigned heads, a second all-to-all exchange restores the sequence-split layout for the feed-forward layers.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. Custom Pallas splash attention from MaxDiffusion</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To accelerate the attention computation itself, the team integrated a custom Pallas </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">splash attention</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> kernel from </span><a href="https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxdiffusion" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">MaxDiffusion</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Google Cloud's open-source reference library for diffusion models on Cloud TPU.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Splash attention brings FlashAttention-style tiling to TPU architecture: rather than materializing the full quadratic attention matrix in High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), it computes attention in compact tiles that remain inside ultra-fast on-chip VMEM. Startup teams can drop the exact kernel used in this port straight into their own pipelines via </span><a href="https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxdiffusion/blob/main/src/maxdiffusion/kernels/custom_splash_attention.py" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">custom_splash_attention.py</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. A 5.8x audio speedup by moving from torchax to pure JAX</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We first brought all three Orbis models onto Cloud TPU with *torchax*, which runs PyTorch code on TPU through JAX and XLA without a rewrite. For the video and upscaling models, `torchax` paired with Pallas attention kernels met our latency target directly, and both stay in `torchax` in the final pipeline.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Only the audio model needed a rewrite. Moving the audio stage from `torchax` to *native JAX* made it *5.8x faster* and brought the full pipeline under the playback deadline.</span></p>
<p style="padding-left: 40px;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Startup Builder Tip: Port strategically.<br /></strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The takeaway isn't "skip torchax" — it's to use it as a fast bring-up bridge. torchax got our PyTorch models running on TPU in days rather than weeks, giving us the end-to-end telemetry needed to pinpoint true bottlenecks. Measure the full pipeline first, and invest engineering cycles rewriting only the components that dominate your latency budget.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Field notes for AI builders: Cloud TPU lessons</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaways from tuning live multimodal inference on Cloud TPU:</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ulysses context parallelism for single stream video</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* At batch size 1, splitting the token sequence across chips with `all-to-all` interconnect exchanges was faster than both tensor parallelism and ring attention.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Native BF16 for continuous streams</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* FP8 quantization cuts memory bandwidth pressure, but small quantization errors build up across chunks in a continuous stream. Native BF16 kept visual quality intact within the latency budget.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Selective JAX ports</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* `torchax` kept the video and upscaling models in PyTorch, so the team only rewrote the audio bottleneck in native JAX.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">*XLA compilation caching:*</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The first boot spends a few minutes compiling XLA graphs. Caching compiled artifacts brings worker restarts under a minute.</span></p>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Performance and reasoning at petabyte scale</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">While agent context windows are typically limited to ingesting hundreds of kilobytes at a time, enterprise workflows sometimes join petabytes of data across clouds. An agent-native data platform that needs to combine data across sources, like product and customer data, to your CRM cloud and warehouse telemetry needs to be able to process data at scale. When you do this over MCP, you are limited to megabyte scale because of context windows and payload size. By integrating at every layer of the data and AI stack, the borderless Lakehouse provides enterprise-grade scalability, enabling agents to reason over petabytes of data with similar performance to it being stored locally. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">At the networking layer, Cross-Cloud Interconnect and </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/lakehouse/docs/about-cross-cloud-data-access"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">cross-cloud caching</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> provide high-throughput, low-latency paths to AWS and Azure, eliminating variable egress fees. When an agent joins Iceberg tables in Amazon Azure Data Lake Storage with data in BigQuery, every uncached byte it scans crosses a cloud boundary, so the network sets the pace. Cross-Cloud Interconnect gives the Lakehouse a dedicated, private connection between Google Cloud and AWS or Azure, at 10 Gbps or 100 Gbps per connection. Lakehouse queries travel over that connection rather than the public internet, so latency stays low and predictable. Cross-cloud caching serves repeat reads from inside Google Cloud, so agents can query data in other clouds without migrating it.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The borderless Lakehouse includes several key optimizations to integrate Iceberg metadata within the query engine. BigQuery now supports </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/lakehouse/docs/use-iceberg-v3-binary-deletion-vectors"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Apache Iceberg V3 specifications,</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> including binary deletion vectors and sort orders. These features allow the engine to handle row-level updates and deletes more efficiently by eliminating the need to rewrite entire data files. We also upgraded BigQuery’s execution engine with a new FlatBuffers-based Parquet reader and parallelized I/O to help reduce CPU overhead and serialization bottlenecks and narrow the performance gap between open formats and native storage.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This builds upon recent performance innovations to the query engine designed to support agent workloads, which often come in herds and need to process small queries quickly and cost effectively. ### Temporal Serverless Workers on Cloud Run: Scale workers ahead of demand

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/serverless/temporal-serverless-workers-on-cloud-run-worker-pools/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/serverless/temporal-serverless-workers-on-cloud-run-worker-pools/)

- **Fetched**: 2026-10-16 03:07:53

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The open-source </span><a href="https://temporal.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> execution platform is rapidly becoming a foundational layer of modern software infrastructure — especially for orchestration in distributed systems and AI agent workloads. However, deploying and scaling workers in Temporal comes with unique challenges. Workers poll for tasks instead of serving requests, so request-driven serverless platforms can be an awkward fit. This often results in running worker fleets on Kubernetes or VMs, which you have to manage and pay for continuously. You build a metrics pipeline and tune autoscaling triggers, and scaling still trails bursts because of metrics lag. Every deployment means juggling upgrades and versions, and you pay for that compute whether or not there's work in the queue.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We built </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/deploy-worker-pools"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud Run worker pools</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> for exactly this kind of pull-based work: Your instances connect to a queue or stream, take the next item, and process it. Starting today, </span><a href="https://temporal.io/cloud" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal Cloud</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, which is the fully managed version of Temporal’s open-source platform, can scale them for you. Currently in preview, </span><a href="https://docs.temporal.io/serverless-workers" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal Serverless Workers</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> on Cloud Run let you deploy your workers as a worker pool, and Temporal sizes the pool to match the work on your task queues. When there's no work, it can scale the pool to zero. There's no cluster or autoscaler to run, and your worker code stays the same.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">How Cloud Run worker pools fit with Temporal</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A worker pool is Cloud Run's resource for continuous background work. It has no public endpoint, and each instance is allocated CPU for its whole lifetime, which matches how a Temporal worker runs. Temporal manages your Cloud Run worker pools as follows:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Your Temporal workers run as Cloud Run worker pool instances in your project and poll Temporal Cloud for tasks.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal Cloud tracks your task queues and sets the worker pool's instance count through the Cloud Run Admin API, using the IAM permissions you grant. Temporal never opens a connection to your instances, so you don't need to open ports or add firewall rules for it.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You run each build version as its own Cloud Run worker pool. An older build's worker pool can sit at zero instances until a workflow that started on it needs it again.</span></p>
</li>
</ol>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Your Temporal workers run in your project, under your service account, and on your network. Temporal decides how many of them run.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">What you can build with Temporal Serverless Workers</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The combination of Cloud Run worker pools with Temporal Serverless Workers opens up a variety of interesting use cases:</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Autoscaled agentic workers:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> AI agents run multi-step chains of LLM reasoning and tool calls. Temporal's durable execution and Cloud Run worker pools work together here. Temporal workers spin up instantly to handle bursts of LLM steps, embeddings or API calls, then scale to zero when the queue empties. Workers get keyless access to Google Cloud AI APIs. You can also attach a GPU for inference, and the workers attached to the GPUs will still scale to zero. Temporal runs each model and tool call as a durable activity; if a worker disappears mid-step, the activity retries on another worker and the workflow continues.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Long-running </strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Workflows</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><a href="https://docs.temporal.io/workflows" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal Workflows</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> can run indefinitely, allowing for building applications that track the state of real-world resources over long periods of time. This pattern is typically known as the “</span><a href="https://docs.temporal.io/design-patterns/entity-workflow%5C" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Entity Pattern</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">,” because the Workflow represents the state of some entity. However, these long-running Workflows don’t usually have consistent compute requirements: Some individual steps are short, some are long, and other times, the Workflow is sleeping, waiting for external input, such as from a human or another system. Cloud Run worker pools provide flexible compute to support this pattern: Individual steps can run for as long as is required, and Temporal will automatically scale down the worker pool when there is no work to be done.</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Bursty batch work:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Worker pools bill only while instances run, so a worker pool that sits idle between batch runs costs nothing while it's at zero instances. For jobs like billing runs or media transcoding, Temporal scales the pool out as batches arrive and back in once the queue drains. Your maximum instance count caps how many instances you pay for. Tasks beyond that capacity wait in the queue rather than being dropped.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Why Cloud Run worker pools for Temporal workers</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Before, running Temporal workers on Cloud Run services took some workarounds:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">an HTTP listener, because services expect containers to listen on a port</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">instance-based billing, so workers kept their CPU between requests</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">minimum instances, so the service didn't scale to zero</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By running Temporal workers on Cloud Run worker pools, you don’t need those workarounds anymore, and you have:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">No endpoint to manage.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> There's no public URL or load balancer to lock down, and no health-check port to implement.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Steady CPU at a lower price.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Instances don't idle or throttle. Cloud Run doesn't remove instances because they look idle, so Temporal alone decides when the pool shrinks. Worker pools list at almost 40% less per vCPU-hour and GB-hour than instance-billed Cloud Run services.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Scaling without revision churn.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Changing the instance count doesn't create a new revision, so Temporal can resize the pool as often as the load changes.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">How Temporal manages scaling and versioning of worker pools</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Temporal </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">decides when to add capacity using one main signal: the delay between a task landing in the task queue backlog and a worker picking it up. If that delay grows, the available workers are saturated. Scaling occurs as follows:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Server-side detection:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The Temporal server tracks this backlog-to-worker latency for each task queue.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Push notification:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> When the latency crosses its trigger conditions, the server sends a push notification to the worker controller instance.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Provisioning:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The worker controller then adds capacity to the worker pool. New instances take time to start and connect before they can poll for tasks, so capacity lags behind the signal.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Because Temporal keeps spare capacity, the pool grows ahead of demand instead of after tasks pile up. Scale-in is more conservative. Temporal waits for a stabilization period after the last time a task found no available worker (90 seconds by default), and it pauses between successive reductions. Cloud Run’s configurable graceful shutdown extends shutdown from 10 seconds to 10 minutes. That gives SIGTERM-aware workers time to finish or checkpoint. The instance count stays between a minimum and a maximum that you set. If the first task after an idle period can't wait for an instance to start, set the minimum above zero.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Small teams, short runway, and constant pressure to ship faster — it’s the reality we see every day among the founders we work with at Google for Startups. <h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">What is the Gemini agent?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of prompting separate chatbots for writing, coding, and data analysis, the Gemini agent is a single agent that takes high-level objectives and returns finished work. Three principles define it:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Unified:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> A single agent can now handle knowledge work, data analysis, creative asset generation, and writing and running code. For complex jobs, it dynamically spins up temporary, job-specific sub-agents and coordinates their work in parallel or in sequence.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Omnipresent access:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> It works across the surfaces you use every day — on the web, command line, desktop, and mobile, as well as natively inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Chat, and Calendar.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Persistent:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> It runs in the cloud with continuous memory across four dimensions (session, semantic, procedural, and episodic). It onboards itself like a new hire — learning your team, tools, and business context so you don't have to re-explain your startup with every prompt, and keeping multi-day tasks moving even after you close your laptop.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For founders wearing five hats at once, Gemini works like a digital chief of staff that's already briefed on your calendar, projects and documents. Ask it to research market trends, build a financial model in Sheets and turn both into a pitch deck, without repeating what the project or goals are at each step. It also works ahead of you. When an investor emails asking for your latest product update as a deck, Gemini spots the request and offers to handle it; one click and it comes back with completed work, ready for sharing or review.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Five takeaways for startups</strong></h3>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Coworker agents and skills help you scale your output</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Beyond personal assistance, you can create persistent </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">coworker agents</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> that work with your entire team. Each agent comes with its own Workspace account, email address, Drive storage, and directory presence. You can define a specific role — such as a launch coordinator, QA triager, or financial analyst — and your teammates can add agents to a Chat space or </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">@mention</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> it in a Google Doc comment to delegate work.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You can also equip agents with reusable </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">skills</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> — or let Gemini write skills for itself as it learns your processes. </li>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">定常状態のパフォーマンスに合わせて CPU リクエストを厳密にサイジングすると、これらの初期化ワークロードでは起動時に CPU スロットリングが発生し、readiness プローブが遅延します。コールド スタートの遅延を防ぐために、CPU リクエストをオーバープロビジョニングするケースがよく見られます。しかし、アプリケーションが安定すると、それらの余分な CPU リソースはアイドル状態になり、何のメリットもないままクラウド費用を増加させることになります。</span></p></div>

      <p><b>CPU 起動ブーストが役立つ仕組み</b></p><p>CPU 起動ブーストは、起動時にワークロードに一時的な vCPU「ブースト」を与え、初期化が完了すると自動的にベースラインに戻すことで、この問題を解決します。</p><p>主な利点:</p><ul><li><b>コールド スタートの高速化</b>: アプリケーションの初期化時間を最大 <b>2 倍</b>短縮し、予期しないトラフィックの急増時に自動スケーリングの応答性を向上させます。</li><li><b>クラウド支出の最適化</b>: アイドル状態の起動ヘッドルームに費用をかけるのではなく、実際のランタイムのニーズに合わせて定常状態の CPU リクエストを適切にサイジングします。</li><li><b>Pod の再起動が不要</b>: 動的なリソースのサイズ変更は、実行中のコンテナ内で直接行われます。</li><li><b>柔軟なポリシー制御</b>: Pod レベルのシンプルな乗数係数（起動時に CPU を 2 倍にするなど）を適用したり、複雑なマルチコンテナ Pod に対してコンテナ固有のきめ細かいルールを定義したりできます。</li></ul>
    </div>
  </div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">仕組み: Kubernetes のインプレース Pod サイズ変更</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これまで、Pod のリソース リクエストや上限を変更するには、Pod を削除して再作成する必要がありました。このように影響の大きいプロセスは、コンテナの再起動、キャッシュの無効化、そしてノードの再スケジューリングによるオーバーヘッドを引き起こしていました。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">これを解決するために、CPU 起動ブーストは Kubernetes の</span><a href="https://kubernetes.io/docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/resize-container-resources/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">インプレース Pod サイズ変更（IPPR）</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を基盤としています。</span></p>
<p><a href="https://github.com/kubernetes/enhancements/tree/master/keps/sig-node/1287-in-place-pod-resize" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">KEP-1287</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> として管理されている IPPR は、動的なインプレース リソース ミューテーションを導入しました。Kubernetes 1.27 でアルファ版として導入され、v1.33 でベータ版に昇格、v1.35 で一般提供（GA）となった IPPR を使用すると、Kubernetes コントロール プレーンと </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">kubelet</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> が、</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">コンテナ プロセスを再起動することなく</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、実行中の Pod のコンテナ CPU とメモリのリクエストを更新できます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE は、VPA 内の IPPR を活用して、Pod のアドミッション時に起動時の CPU ブーストを適用し、準備完了後にスムーズに引き下げます。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">CPU 起動ブーストの仕組み（Pod ライフサイクルの概要）</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">CPU 起動ブーストは、次の 3 つのフェーズで動作します。</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">アドミッション フェーズ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Pod をデプロイすると、VPA アドミッション Webhook が作成リクエストをインターセプトします。ポリシー（2 倍の乗数や +2 vCPU など）に基づいて引き上げられた CPU リクエストを計算し、追跡アノテーションとともに Pod 仕様に挿入します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">起動フェーズ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Pod がスケジュールされ、ブーストされた CPU 割り当てで初期化されます。アプリケーションは、CPU スロットリングを発生させることなく、クラスの読み込み、JIT コンパイル、モジュールの解析を最高速度で完了します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ブースト解除フェーズ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Pod の </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">readinessProbe</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> が成功すると（さらに、構成された </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">durationSeconds</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> のクールダウン遅延が経過すると）、VPA アップデータはインプレース サイズ変更リクエストを発行します。コンテナは中断されることなく実行を続け、CPU リクエストはベースライン レベルに戻ります。</span></p>
</li>
</ol>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">前提条件と提供状況</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">CPU 起動ブーストは、GKE でプレビュー版として提供されています。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE バージョン</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Standard クラスタと Autopilot クラスタのバージョン </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">1.36.0-gke.4447000</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 以降。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">クラスタモード</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: GKE Autopilot でネイティブに有効（VPA はデフォルトで有効）。GKE Standard では、垂直 Pod 自動スケーリング（VPA）が有効になっていることを確認するだけです。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ワークロードのサポート</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Deployment や StatefulSet などの標準の Kubernetes コントローラと連携します。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/configure-capacity-buffer"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE Capacity Buffers API</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> でトラフィックの急増に対応</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: 大規模な起動オーバーヘッドを削減することで、より少ないノードでワークロードの密度を高め、全体的な使用率を向上させることができます。厳格な SLO を維持しながら、最小限の運用オーバーヘッドでトラフィックの急増を吸収するために、GKE Capacity Buffers API の導入を検討してください。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE Autopilot のリソース比率</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: GKE Autopilot では、Pod が有効な CPU とメモリの比率を維持する必要があります。起動時にブーストされた CPU 比率に対応できるよう、ベースラインのメモリ割り当てを確保します。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">たとえば、ユーザーの休暇の予約を支援する、パーソナライズされた旅行エージェントを構築するとします。ユーザーは数日間にわたってエージェントと何度もやり取りし、旅行プランのアイデア出しから具体的な購入・予約の検討に至るまで、さまざまな質問をします。真にシームレスなエクスペリエンスを提供するには、このエージェントは、以前のセッションで設定されたフライトの好み（例: 「直行便のみ」）、ホテルの予算、食事の要望（例: 「グルテンフリーのメニューが必要」）を記憶しておく必要があります。さらに重要なのは、会話が観光のおすすめや旅行プランの計画といった細かい内容に深入りしても、これらの重要情報を保持する必要がある点です。エージェントは、追加の質問や訪問先に関する魅力的な詳細情報によって、ユーザーの基本的な要件や決定事項を忘れたり上書きしたりすることがないようにしなければなりません。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ステートフルなワークフローは、LLM のステートレスな性質と本質的に矛盾する</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">AI エージェントは、マルチターンの会話や長期にわたるワークフローに活用されていますが、大規模言語モデル（LLM）はセッション間でステートレスのままとなっています。ユーザーが数日後にエージェントを再び利用する際、モデルはコンテキスト ウィンドウが空の状態で処理を開始します。アプリケーションがエージェントの長期メモリを使用して過去のコンテキストを再構築するように設計されていない限り、ユーザーは目標とコンテキストを最初から説明し直す必要があるため、ストレスのたまる断片的なユーザー体験になってしまいます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">100 万トークンのコンテキスト ウィンドウが標準となった現在、この問題に対する手っ取り早い解決策としてよく使われるのは「コンテキスト スタッフィング」です。これは、未加工のチャット履歴からツールの実行ログまで、プロンプトに収まるあらゆるものを会話のターンごとに詰め込むというものです。実際、これは AI モデルが使用され始めた初期によく見られたパターンです。しかし、この手っ取り早い解決策では、規模が大きくなるとすぐに問題が発生します。メッセージごとのトークン消費量が倍増し、単純なプロンプトでも応答時間が 30 秒を超えるようになり、モデルでは「</span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">lost in the middle（情報の埋もれ）</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">」と呼ばれる精度低下が生じ、大量のプロンプト テキストに埋もれた重要な指示を見落とすようになります。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">もう一つの一般的な回避策は、ローリング サマリーを使用することです。これは LLM に古いメッセージを定期的に要約して、短い文章に圧縮するよう指示する手法です。これによりプロンプトのサイズは削減されますが、LLM ベースの要約は本質的に情報の欠落を伴います。数回の圧縮を繰り返すうちに、些細ながらも重要な詳細がバックグラウンド ノイズとして除去されてしまうからです。数ターンも経つと、エージェントはユーザーが最初に設定した制約を、いつの間にか無視するようになります。以前の会話やマルチターン タスクの記憶を保持するには、ユーザー体験の低下や、新たなボトルネックの発生を回避したうえでの、よりスケーラブルなアプローチが必要であることは明らかです。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">2 層メモリ アーキテクチャの実装</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ガードレールと制約を遵守し、信頼性と費用対効果に優れたエンタープライズ エージェントを構築するには、</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">2 層メモリ アーキテクチャ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">の採用をおすすめします。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">短期セッション バッファ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: トークン数で制限されたスライディング ウィンドウを使用して、アクティブな会話ターンをメモリにキャッシュします。これにより、デバイスをまたいだ複数回のやり取りでもコンテキスト ウィンドウのデータ量を一定に保ち、直近のメッセージを常に最新の状態に保つことができます。この階層では、ターンごとに 1 ミリ秒未満の高速かつ高スループットなルックアップが必要となるため、</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Memorystore for Valkey</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> がアクティブなセッション状態の維持に最適です。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">長期永続メモリ</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: 重要な事実、ユーザーの好み、エピソード的事実をセッションをまたいで保存します。この階層では、トランザクションの整合性、データ ガバナンス、リレーショナル データとベクトルにわたるハイブリッド検索が必要となります。これらは、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/alloydb/ai"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB AI</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> がネイティブに提供する機能です。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">長期記憶とアクティブ セッション バッファの両方をリレーショナル データベースに直接保存することも可能ですが、インメモリ キャッシュを備えた 2 層アーキテクチャの方がパフォーマンスとスケーラビリティが向上します。アクティブ セッション バッファは非常に一時的なもので、会話のターンごとに 1 ミリ秒未満の高スループットな更新が必要です。これらの高頻度で発生する書き込みをインメモリ Key-Value キャッシュで処理することで、リレーショナル データベースでの書き込みの増大とテーブルの肥大化を防ぎます。これを行わないと、頻繁な行の削除や負荷の高いバキューム処理が必要になってしまいます。これは、データベースの前段にキャッシュ階層を追加して、アクセスが集中している行への高頻度なルックアップをオフロードするのと同様の仕組みです。この分業により、プライマリ データベースは負荷を抑えて応答性の高い状態を維持し、トランザクションの整合性、複雑なハイブリッド ベクトル検索、長期的な分析クエリの実行など、本来の目的にリソースを集中させることができます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Memorystore for Valkey の短期キャッシュと AlloyDB AI のネイティブ機能を組み合わせることで、エンティティ抽出、メモリ圧縮、セッションをまたいだメモリ、ハイブリッド検索をデータベース層内で直接実行し、アクティブなプロンプトを軽量かつ高速に維持し、優れた費用対効果を実現できます。</span></p></div>

<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB AI の核となる技術的メリット</strong></h4>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB AI</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> は、コア AI 機能をデータベース エンジンに直接組み込むことで、永続的なエージェント メモリの実装に伴う運用上のオーバーヘッドを削減します。その主な特長は以下のとおりです。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">データベース内トランザクション自動エンベディング（</strong><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/ai/generate-manage-auto-embeddings-for-tables"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ai.initialize_embeddings</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">）</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: AlloyDB は、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/products/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform?e=48754805"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Platform</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">（旧称 Vertex AI）とのネイティブ統合を使用して、テキスト列のベクトル エンベディングを自動的に生成し、1 秒あたり最大 3,000 個のエンベディングを処理できます。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">incremental_refresh_mode =&gt; 'transactional'</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を使用すると、AlloyDB は同じトランザクション内でソースデータが変更されるとエンベディングを最新の状態に保つため、カスタム エンベディング パイプライン、外部スケジューラ、複雑な再試行ロジックが不要になります。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">データベース内生成 AI 関数（</strong><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/ai/evaluate-semantic-queries-ai-operators"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ai.generate</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">）</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: AlloyDB では、Gemini などの基盤モデルを SQL クエリから直接実行できます。これを使用すると、データベース内でクエリを分解できます。つまり、ユーザーの複合的な質問を単一の要素からなるサブクエリに分割し、相対的な時間表現（「前回のセッション」など）を明示的な識別子に解決できます。アプリケーション側で個別にラウンドトリップを行う必要もありません。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">組み込みの Reciprocal Rank Fusion（</strong><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/ai/run-hybrid-vector-similarity-search"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ai.hybrid_search</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">）によるネイティブ ハイブリッド検索</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: AlloyDB は、エンジン内で直接 Reciprocal Rank Fusion（RRF）を実行する組み込み SQL 関数を提供します。ベクトル コサイン類似度（HNSW または ScaNN インデックスに対する </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">&lt;=&gt;</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）と PostgreSQL 全文検索（BM25、RUM、または GIN を使用）を 1 回のデータベース呼び出しで組み合わせ、セマンティック マッチングと正確なキーワード検索を融合させます。同時に、メタデータ フィルタ プッシュダウン（</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">filter_condition</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">）をサポートすることで、パフォーマンスの向上と確定的なスコープ分離を実現します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">IAM 認証情報を使用した Agent Platform との直接統合</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: AlloyDB は、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/products/iam"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud IAM</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> サービス アカウントのロールとデータベース認証を使用して、Google Cloud のプライベート ネットワーク経由で </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Platform</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 基盤モデルに直接接続します。これにより、アプリケーション コードで API キーを保存、ローテーション、受け渡す必要がなくなります。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">運用、ベクトル、ガバナンスの統合エンジン</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: AlloyDB は、リレーショナル ビジネスデータ、ベクトル エンベディング、全文インデックス、エンタープライズ権限を、ACID 準拠の単一の PostgreSQL データベースに統合します。これにより、個別の運用データベースとベクトル データベース間で発生するデータのドリフトや統合の複雑さを回避できます。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">主な実装パターン</strong></h4>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. AI 関数を使用したデータベース内メモリ圧縮</strong></p>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">エンタープライズ ガバナンスとマルチテナント セキュリティ</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">エンタープライズの本番環境でエージェント メモリを実行するには、厳格なセキュリティ境界とアクセス制御が必要です。まず、</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">スコープとマルチテナントの分離</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を維持する必要があります。</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">agent_entities</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> の </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">user_id</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">project_id</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">、</span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">scope</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 列にインデックスを付け、PostgreSQL の行レベル セキュリティ（RLS）を適用することで、同じデータベース クラスタ内の部門、チーム、個々のユーザー間でメモリストアを分離できます。</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/parameterized-secure-views-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">パラメータ化されたセキュアビュー</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">（PSV）は、もう一つの確定的なアプリケーション レベルのセキュリティ レイヤを提供し、悪意のあるプロンプトや過度に広範な SQL クエリから保護するのに役立ちます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">さらに、上記の実装パターンに示すように、</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">メモリ ライフサイクルの自動管理</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を提供する必要があります。スケジュールされた SQL コンパクション クエリと時間ベースのパーティション プルーニングを組み合わせることで、データベースのフットプリントとクエリのレイテンシを長期にわたって予測可能な状態に維持できます。このアプローチの詳細については、</span><a href="https://codelabs.developers.google.com/alloydb-agentic-tiered-memory" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">対応する Codelab</span></a><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。</span></p>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Live video is a hard-deadline problem</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When you serve an offline clip generator, latency is forgiving — the user waits once while the backend processes frames. A live world model has no waiting room.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Every single chunk must be denoised, decoded from latents, upscaled to 1664x936 (at 18 fps), and paired with generated audio faster than realtime playback speed, for as long as the session runs.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Two structural constraints make live world models far more demanding than standard batch inference:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Batch size of one:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Each live stream represents an individual viewer's interactive session. </li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strict sequential dependency:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Every chunk depends directly on the one before it. </li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For startup infrastructure engineers coming from LLM serving, it helps to think of live world modeling not as a tokens-per-second throughput benchmark, but as an </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">unforgiving per-step latency SLO</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>

<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Inside the stack: Three optimizations that unlocked real-time streaming</span></h3>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Ulysses context parallelism across all TPU chips</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A video chunk expands into a massive sequence of spatiotemporal tokens inside the video transformer. To distribute that workload across the TPU chips at batch size 1, the team implemented </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ulysses context parallelism</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of splitting model weights, Ulysses splits the token sequence across chips. Immediately before the attention layer, a high-speed all-to-all interconnect exchange regroups the tensors so that each chip holds the </span><span style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">entire</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> video sequence, but only for one-quarter of the attention heads. After each chip computes attention over the full sequence for its assigned heads, a second all-to-all exchange restores the sequence-split layout for the feed-forward layers.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. Custom Pallas splash attention from MaxDiffusion</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To accelerate the attention computation itself, the team integrated a custom Pallas </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">splash attention</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> kernel from </span><a href="https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxdiffusion" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">MaxDiffusion</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, Google Cloud's open-source reference library for diffusion models on Cloud TPU.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Splash attention brings FlashAttention-style tiling to TPU architecture: rather than materializing the full quadratic attention matrix in High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), it computes attention in compact tiles that remain inside ultra-fast on-chip VMEM. Startup teams can drop the exact kernel used in this port straight into their own pipelines via </span><a href="https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/maxdiffusion/blob/main/src/maxdiffusion/kernels/custom_splash_attention.py" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">custom_splash_attention.py</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. A 5.8x audio speedup by moving from torchax to pure JAX</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">We first brought all three Orbis models onto Cloud TPU with *torchax*, which runs PyTorch code on TPU through JAX and XLA without a rewrite. For the video and upscaling models, `torchax` paired with Pallas attention kernels met our latency target directly, and both stay in `torchax` in the final pipeline.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Only the audio model needed a rewrite. Moving the audio stage from `torchax` to *native JAX* made it *5.8x faster* and brought the full pipeline under the playback deadline.</span></p>
<p style="padding-left: 40px;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Startup Builder Tip: Port strategically.<br /></strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The takeaway isn't "skip torchax" — it's to use it as a fast bring-up bridge. torchax got our PyTorch models running on TPU in days rather than weeks, giving us the end-to-end telemetry needed to pinpoint true bottlenecks. Measure the full pipeline first, and invest engineering cycles rewriting only the components that dominate your latency budget.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Field notes for AI builders: Cloud TPU lessons</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaways from tuning live multimodal inference on Cloud TPU:</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ulysses context parallelism for single stream video</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* At batch size 1, splitting the token sequence across chips with `all-to-all` interconnect exchanges was faster than both tensor parallelism and ring attention.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Native BF16 for continuous streams</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* FP8 quantization cuts memory bandwidth pressure, but small quantization errors build up across chunks in a continuous stream. Native BF16 kept visual quality intact within the latency budget.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- *</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Selective JAX ports</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">:* `torchax` kept the video and upscaling models in PyTorch, so the team only rewrote the audio bottleneck in native JAX.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">- </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">*XLA compilation caching:*</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> The first boot spends a few minutes compiling XLA graphs. Caching compiled artifacts brings worker restarts under a minute.</span></p>

<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Omnipresent access:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> It works across the surfaces you use every day — on the web, command line, desktop, and mobile, as well as natively inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Chat, and Calendar.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
