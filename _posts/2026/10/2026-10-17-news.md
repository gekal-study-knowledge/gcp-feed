### Architecting autonomy: A design pattern for high-compute, governed agents in biotech

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/healthcare-life-sciences/architecting-scalable-agentic-biotech-workloads/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/healthcare-life-sciences/architecting-scalable-agentic-biotech-workloads/)

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In production systems today, most AI agents operate as lightweight dispatchers. They parse a prompt, make an API call, retrieve a record, and hand back control. Their execution is fast, stateless and cheap. Biotechnology does not work this way. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Folding a protein target, screening a 50,000 compound ligand library, or evaluating structure-activity relationships (SAR) are not quick API lookups. They are sustained, stateful, GPU-bound simulations that run for minutes or even hours across specialized silicon. When you ask a general-purpose large language model (LLM) endpoint to coordinate a complex drug discovery workflow, the standard paradigm breaks: Timeouts occur, context windows overflow with atomic coordinates, and proprietary chemical Intellectual Property (IP) risks exposure to external networks. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Building enterprise grade research and development (R&amp;D) agents requires moving past the monolithic assistant model. In this blog post, we will discuss how to use a three-layer architecture: separating cognitive orchestration (the brain) from computational execution (the muscle), and anchoring both inside an enterprise governance plane to build scalable agents for drug discovery use cases in biotech. </span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The execution layer: scaling the muscle - high-compute execution</span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;"> </span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The execution layer is responsible for the heavy scientific computations. The first architectural decision is determining where workloads should run:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strategic, proprietary pipelines require dedicated GPU infrastructure within your security perimeter</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Routine or standardized workloads can safely be delegated to managed cloud endpoints</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this architecture, protein folding is driven by </span><a href="https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/teams/nim/collections/bionemo-esmfold" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ESMFold</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and generative design by </span><a href="https://docs.nvidia.com/nim/bionemo/genmol/latest/overview.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GenMol</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Both run as </span><a href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/products/nim-microservices/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NVIDIA Inference Microservices</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or NIMs deployed on </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (GKE) using G4 VMs backed by </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/introducing-g4-vm-with-nvidia-rtx-pro-6000"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GPUs.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A critical hurdle in containerized GPU workloads is the cold-start penalty, the latency delay required to pull multi-gigabyte model weights from an image registry and stage them into GPU memory (VRAM).</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To ensure low latency responsiveness, we build the following as a recommendation into the reference architecture:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Maintain warm pools:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Pin the GKE node pool to a minimum of one active node.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Preload weights:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Model weights are loaded directly into VRAM at pod initialization, ensuring containers are immediately ready for inference.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Resilient scheduling:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GKE manages pod lifecycles and restarts failed instances without tearing down the underlying VM infrastructure. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This approach trades custom, manual CUDA kernel tuning for NVIDIA's pre-compiled, containerized runtimes, dramatically reducing infrastructure engineering overhead while ensuring deterministic throughput.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In biotech, chemical structures represent millions of dollars in proprietary IP. A docking request transmits SMILE strings or 3D coordinate matrices that must never leave company custody.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Containerized NIMs eliminate the friction between rapid prototyping and rigorous data isolation. During early exploration, researchers can query NVIDIA hosted endpoints. When transitioning to proprietary lead candidates, the exact same container is deployed to GKE inside a private </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/vpc?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">VPC</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. The agent’s orchestration logic remains identical; only the target URI updates from an external proxy to an internal VPC endpoint. No structural data leaves the enterprise perimeter.</span></p>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The cognitive layer: Multi-agent reasoning</span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With compute execution isolated, the cognitive layer manages agentic workflow sequencing. Built on </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/build/runtime"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Runtime</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, within the Gemini platform, this reference architecture shows a system that deploys a hierarchical multi-agent structure, one lead orchestrator, and two specialized domain sub-agents (Lead Optimization and Safety Screening) . The multi-agent design can be modified to suit the use case at hand. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Based on this design, when an end-user submits a high-level task such as </span><strong style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">“ Analyze Candidate 4b against Target X and produce a SAR report”</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, the system applies four core orchestration patterns discussed below; </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">In-process native agent handoffs: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The lead orchestrator delegates tasks to specialist sub-agents using Agent Runtime’s native SDK rather than network-serialized agent-to-agent HTTP protocols. This eliminates intermediate network hops and keeps internal handoffs instantaneous. Only the outermost boundary communicates outward to user-facing surfaces like the </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise?utm_source=google&amp;utm_medium=cpc&amp;utm_campaign=1713762-Gemini_Enterprise-DR-NA-US-en-Google-BKWS-EXA-GEnterprise&amp;utm_content=c-Hybrid+%7C+BKWS+-+MIX+%7C+Txt_Gemini+Enterprise-189528400785&amp;utm_term=gemini+enterprise&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=23370621055&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwl97RBhBWEiwAa9rbXeetAjZcIRGcaAR57VWVWk7ik5DJ9ESqHiDav-2ervoiyzzRZ1ZWyRoCj6sQAvD_BwE&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> agent.</span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Passing bulky payloads by reference: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLMs degrade quickly when forced to ingest massive arrays of 3D spatial coordinates. In this architecture, raw structural data (PDB files, docking poses, trajectory logs) is written directly to </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/storage?utm_source=google&amp;utm_medium=cpc&amp;utm_campaign=Cloud-SS-DR-GCP-1713658-GCP-DR-NA-US-en-Google-SKWS-BRO-storage&amp;utm_content=c-Hybrid+%7C+SKWS+-+BRO+%7C+Txt-Infrastructure-Storage-Cloud+Storage-4642545472&amp;utm_term=cloud+storage&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=23767162264&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwl97RBhBWEiwAa9rbXYcqs8qBJehBeWaezdgKd4am7QbV7uo3__CJIZqoa8lqgP8ErM0LkBoCJ3cQAvD_BwE&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud Storage</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Agents pass lightweight, signed object URIs and high-level structural summaries, keeping context windows compact and inference costs down.</span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Decoupling workflow logic from biochemical reasoning: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">General-purpose LLMs excel at task planning and synthesis, but are prone to hallucinating biochemical nuances. To control this behavior, this architecture maintains strong separation of concerns, namely, workflow and tasks decomposition are handled by Gemini. Domain chemistry and pharmacological reasoning are handled by specialized </span><a href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Nemotron</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> models hosted on dedicated GPU nodes. </span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Context-driven evaluation over rigid cutoffs: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Traditional computational screening enforces arbitrary numerical cutoffs (e.g., discard if binding affinity ,-7.5 kcal/mol) However, therapeutic context fundamentally alters risk tolerance. Preventive cardiovascular therapy taken daily by healthy patients requires extremely strict safety margins, or a novel oncology candidate targeting an intractable cancer may accept higher toxicity if efficacy is demonstrated.</span></li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By passing disease context directly into the specialized reasoning models, drug candidates are evaluated against dynamic, target-specific profiles rather than static thresholds, preventing the premature culling of viable drug leads.</span></p>

<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The enterprise governance and asymmetric autonomy</span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Deploying high-compute agents in enterprise environments requires robust data sovereignty, audit trails, and process control. The </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini platform</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> establishes these safeguards natively with the following components: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/iam/docs/agent-identity-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Identity</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Every autonomous agent operates with a cryptographically verifiable identity. Thus, for a biotech use case, when a candidate molecule is advanced or rejected, the event is permanently logged to that specific agent principal. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/govern/gateways/agent-gateway-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Gateway</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This serves the single ingress/egress point for every interaction within the perimeter. Centralized IAM and regional residency rules help ensure sensitive molecular data remains within designated cloud regions. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/govern/agent-registry"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Registry</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This feature maintains a curated catalog of approved agents, validated tool schemas, and authorized Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, preventing rogue tools from executing in production. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://cloud.google.com/security/products/model-armor?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Model Armor</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This functionality inspects incoming prompts and outgoing payloads in real time to prevent prompt injection and data exfiltration. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff is clear. Identity, RBAC, auditing, residency, and data protection are native properties of the Gemini platform; this allows enterprise customers to focus on science rather than having to build a compliance stack for every project. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In high-stakes research and development, full autonomy introduces unacceptable liability, while requiring approval for trivial steps can create frictions. The solution addressing this to use asymmetric gating in the following ways;</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Automate reversible operations:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> For example, allow the agent to resolve chemical naming synonyms, pull structural PDBs, and run preliminary Lipinski screens autonomously without interrupting the researcher.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Gate irreversible operations:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> For instance, initiating multi-GPU docking run, advancing a lead compound to wet-lab synthesis, or archiving a therapeutic program must require explicit human sign-off.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The pattern, and the path forward</strong></h3>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">High-compute autonomous agents succeed when the boundaries between cognitive planning, numerical computation, and system governance are clean in the following ways:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Keep the orchestrator focused on decomposition and synthesis, leaving intensive biophysical simulation to specialized, containerized microservices and models.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Eliminate context bloat by passing complex chemical data by reference through cloud storage.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Anchor the workflow in enterprise governance, pairing cryptographic agent identity with asymmetric human oversight.</span></p>
</li>
</ol>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">As generative and structural biology models expand toward whole-cell modeling and complex dynamic simulations, this decoupled architecture ensures research systems scale efficiently while keeping proprietary IP secure and auditable.</span></p>

### Four evaluation engineering lessons learned from building agent plugins

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/four-eval-engineering-lessons/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/four-eval-engineering-lessons/)
- **Published**: 2026-10-17 01:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-10-17 04:19:28

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The real challenge of building an agentic system isn't the construction, it's the measurement.</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Evaluation engineering is a critical foundation for building reliable AI systems. Setting up systematic, repeatable tests to measure your agent’s true behavior can be the difference between shipping a tool you can trust and one that silently breaks, fails to call APIs, or makes mistakes. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">I’ve run evaluation engineering initiatives for years at Google and d</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">eveloping the newly released</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/introducing-the-google-cloud-developer-plugin-for-ai-coding-agents?e=48754805"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Developer plugin</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> served as a live testbed to stress-test these key principles across real-world developer workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here are four eval lessons we learned from building the new plugin that you can apply to your own builds.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Grade the execution steps instead of the summary</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When we first evaluated agents, we set up an LLM-as-judge to inspect the agent’s final text reply and scored it against a checklist. However, we quickly realized it’s fundamentally flawed because the grader only sees what the agent wrote, not what it actually did. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When we ran our initial 11-prompt evaluation suite for the Google Cloud Developer plugin, the automated grader marked it as failing three prompts. But when we opened the raw tool-call logs and read the transcripts, the plugin had actually passed all three. On a Cloud Run cleanup prompt, for instance, our rubric required checking command syntax before running anything. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The agent ran </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">gcloud help run services list</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> as its very first step. It did the work, but its final message was focused on warning the user about permanent data loss rather than reciting that it had checked the help docs.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The grader penalized the agent for doing the right thing simply because it didn’t brag about it in the summary. If your rubric line says "did it run X?", a LLM judge is guessing unless you evaluate the actual tool-call trajectory.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Don't grade an agent on its closing pitch. Score the raw execution trajectory to verify what commands were actually executed, validated, and scoped.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. Design evals for asynchronous operations</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When you start reading tool-call trajectories, how you extract that telemetry matters just as much as what you're reading. Extracting agent telemetry into a flattened, sequential list of events obscures concurrent tool dispatch, which is one of the critical causes of behavioral failure. Modern agent harnesses often batch multiple tool invocations into a single assistant turn using an array of calls.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In our evaluations, when an agent was asked to inspect an environment and look up documentation, the harness dispatched the skill-loading call and two documentation searches simultaneously within a single tool_calls payload. Because activating a skill is a multi-stage process, where the initial call returns a short launch stub and the subsequent turn injects the full instruction body, the instructions meant to govern the search arrived in the prompt context after the system had already executed the searches.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The system never gave the skill a dedicated turn to let its guidance influence the agent's actions. If telemetry pipelines strip out turn structures and merge calls into a flat timeline, engineers will waste days wondering why an agent disregarded unambiguous instructions that a developer embedded in a skill.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Preserve turn structure in telemetry extractors so you can identify when client-side parallel dispatch issues tool calls before injected guidance enters the context window. </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. Test live runs and "dry runs" side-by-side</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You need to inspect real API calls to catch harness quirks, like parallel dispatch. But if you only test your agent in one environment condition, you're flying half-blind. From day one, you need to run two conditions side-by-side: a live run and a plan-only run. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In the live condition, the agent has real, sandboxed credentials and actually executes commands. This is how you see if syntax works, parameters serialize cleanly, and the agent exercises restraint before doing something destructive. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Live runs frequently hit an unexpected permission error on step two and abort before you ever reach the behavior you’re trying to test. That's why you need the plan-only condition, where you strip credentials and tell the agent to explain what it would do without running anything. This strips out permission roadblocks and shows you the model’s pure reasoning and routing intent. Roughly half our most important findings came from the delta between the two.</span></p></div>

The plan-only run sailed right through and produced an 8,000-character plan that completely ignored organization policies, admin approvals, and governance. The live run masked a major cognitive flaw that the plan-only run exposed in seconds.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Run live and plan-only evaluations side by side. Live runs test actual execution and safety restraint; plan-only runs expose the model's raw reasoning and routing without getting tripped up by permission errors.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">4. Use “golden sets” as durable baselines</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Frontier model architectures change every few months, agent harnesses improve, and plugin manifest standards will inevitably evolve. When building the Google Cloud Developer plugin, we treated evaluations as continuous integration testing for these non-deterministic environments. That means we stress-tested the composite stack, including the harness, the live model, and the tools running together on realistic tasks.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of guessing from the final text output, this approach captures the whole trajectory: what the agent thought, what commands it actually ran, and whether it solved the problem without breaking anything. Then you score that run against golden sets curated by real human experts who know what the ideal path looks like.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Build golden sets based on what the entire system should actually do in the wild, including your harness, model, and plugins. Models change, but those baseline runs are your safety net.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Reminder: Use evaluation suites over “one-shot” demos</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A common mistake I see among engineers is what I like to call the one-shot demo. This is when someone feeds a prompt into a LLM, watches it build a complete 3D game or solves a Rubik’s cube in a single pass and calls it an eval. But in reality, this is just a vanity metric. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To do quality work with AI, I truly believe that you need evaluation suites. Without evaluations, teams are like literally running into a dark cave blind. Engineering an enterprise-grade agent means testing the system's ability to handle multi-step interactions, manage permissions, and avoid destructive outcomes like accidentally deleting a project because you asked it to "find opportunities to save costs."</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Where to go from here</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Building an agent plugin or MCP server is the beginning. Shipping something you can actually trust with real cloud infrastructure requires significantly more work in evaluations to ensure it’s doing what you expect it to do. At the end of the day, the only thing that outlasts model churn is a stress-tested eval suite. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Check out the Google Cloud Developer plugin </span><a href="https://g.dev/cloud/dev-setup" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">documentation</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or explore this </span><a href="https://g.dev/cloud/agent-plugins-codelab-agy" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">codelab</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to get started with the plugin in Antigravity.</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span></p></div>

### The SAVE method: Learn the fundamentals to engineer trustworthy generative media

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/fundamentals-to-engineer-trustworthy-gen-media/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/fundamentals-to-engineer-trustworthy-gen-media/)
- **Published**: 2026-10-17 01:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-10-17 04:19:28

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Consulting works with organizations every day to build generative media that’s trustworthy, brand-aligned, and stands out. Instead of relying on clever or complicated prompting, we’ve identified four recurring basics to generate consistent results, what we call the “SAVE” method. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this blog, we’ll share more about this method and how you can apply it to your own work. </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">S</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> - Separate the researcher from the storyteller</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">If in a single prompt, if you’re requesting a technical summary, key product features, and an engaging video script all at once, you’re setting the model up to fail. Creative tasks require fluid language and pacing, while factual output requires constraint. Forcing both jobs into one context window will likely end with the model fabricating details to make the narrative flow better.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of one prompt, consider splitting the work into specialized steps, like a researcher and a storyteller. Here’s how they might work together: </span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The researcher</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> reads the source documents, extracts core claims, aligns with approved messaging, and outputs a structured brief (like a clean checklist or bulleted factsheet).</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The storyteller</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> then takes that verified brief and focuses purely on style, pacing, visual metaphors, and tone.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By decoupling research from your creative generation, your agent is never tasked with deciding what’s true, but how to make the truth more engaging and effective.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> - Approve verified facts only</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">If you hand a creative generative model documents that are unedited, it might mix up speculative ideas with business facts. The media generator should never have direct access to raw source files. It should only use verified, structured inputs. To avoid this: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Lock down core claims</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Approved statements, product names, and concrete business outcomes should be fixed upstream rather than left for the model to extract on the spot.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Champion contextual packaging</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Pass each fact along with its explicit constraints included. Examples may include things like "use this point in the opening statement" or "do not extrapolate beyond this approved bullet point."</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This way, you ground the generation in verified facts and reduce the risk of unsupported claims entering the creative generation stage.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">V</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> - Verify before you render</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Discovering a factual error in an animated video or rendered asset after the final export wastes both compute budget and turnaround time. The most effective human-in-the-loop checkpoint is right before generation begins.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To fix this, review the script and storyboard. Have product leads or legal teams sign off on the extracted facts, narrative outline, and voiceover text while it’s still simple, editable text. This way, you can catch errors early. Swapping an outdated feature name or fixing a typo takes two seconds in a text file. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Treat text as your testing ground. Once approved, generating the final media becomes a low-risk, predictable step.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">E</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> - Engineer guardrails into the system</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Telling a model to “keep voiceover word count short, never reference unapproved topics, and format links correctly" in a prompt works until it doesn’t, and an edge case slips through. Relying on prompt obedience for brand safety is a gamble and can damage trust.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Where requirements can be checked deterministically, enforce them in the system rather than relying only on prompt instructions. For example, try these three actions first: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Programmatic boundary checks</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Use standard code to validate word counts, verify that URLs are live and authorized, check colors against your brand palette, and format text strings.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Independent safety gates</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Run automated filters outside the generation pipeline to catch policy violations or out-of-bounds claims before an asset is ever delivered.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Model modularity</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: When your safety and brand rules are set, you don't have to rewrite your entire governance playbook every time a newer, faster generative model is launched. You simply plug the new model into your existing, verified pipeline.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Generating studio-quality media shouldn't mean constantly worrying about what the model might fabricate next. The SAVE method shifts the burden of accuracy away from prompt phrasing and into the architecture where it belongs. </span></p>

<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Scaling inference requires system-level optimization</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When evaluating models for agentic coding and tool use, inference efficiency happens at two distinct layers: </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">how efficiently the model reasons</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">how efficiently the serving system executes multi-turn loops</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Model-level efficiency: Reflection’s Beam</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Reflection designed Beam (</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">~501B</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> total parameters with </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">~23B</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> active parameters per token, and an approximately </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1M</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">-token context window) specifically for text-native software engineering and multi-step tool use. It plugs directly into the coding harnesses developers already use, including Reflection’s Mirror CLI, Claude Code, Hermes, OpenCode, and , to resolve GitHub issues, run CLI and DevOps workflows, and complete long-horizon tasks.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Because Beam activates only 23B parameters per token and reaches solutions using </span><a href="https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">3x to 4x fewer reasoning tokens</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> than other comparable open weight models, it dramatically reduces the inference-time compute required for each reasoning step.</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">System-level efficiency: AI Hypercomputer optimized serving</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Once developers deploy an agent into production, a second set of bottlenecks could appear at the serving layer. In an agentic coding loop, the model is called dozens of times in a single session. With every turn, the coding harness appends new file reads, code diffs, compiler logs, and tool outputs to a shared conversation history that quickly grows to tens or hundreds of thousands of tokens.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Even when the model itself generates 3x to 4x fewer reasoning tokens on each turn, the </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">accumulated context window across turns</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> creates two major infrastructure challenges:</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">First, </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">prefill compute becomes a bottleneck</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Standard Kubernetes load balancers distribute requests using stateless round-robin routing. When a coding harness sends Turn 15 of a session, it often lands on a different replica than Turn 14. That replica is forced to recompute the entire historical prompt from scratch, wasting GPU compute and creating long prefill queues that delay other users.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Second, </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">decode becomes bottlenecked by KV cache capacity</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Holding long-context KV caches for dozens of concurrent agent sessions quickly fills up GPU High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Once HBM fills up, the serving engine has to evict older KV blocks. As soon as those blocks are evicted, cache hit rates drop and GPU throughput stalls.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To preserve Beam’s model-level efficiency at cluster scale, the serving stack has to be optimized as a distributed system. That is why our recipes pair Beam with</span> <a href="https://llm-d.ai/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">llm-d</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (with vLLM) and</span> <a href="https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NVIDIA Dynamo</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (with SGLang).</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Inside the AI Hypercomputer inference architecture</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To run Beam’s </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">BF16</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">FP8</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, and </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">NVFP4</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> checkpoints at peak efficiency on Day 0, we designed a vertically integrated architecture on AI Hypercomputer where each layer handles a specific part of the agentic serving lifecycle.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Rather than treating inference pods as stateless endpoints, each layer of the stack is co-designed around the multi-turn agent lifecycle:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Prefix- and load-aware routing (GKE + llm-d EPP / Dynamo router):</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Tracks cached prefixes and queue depth across worker pods, steering each follow-up turn from a coding harness to the replica holding its warm KV prefix while preventing queue saturation via turn-priority flow control.</span></p>

<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Disaggregated compute and speculative decoding (A4X Max &amp; G4 instance)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Runs vLLM and SGLang with native Blackwell NVFP4, Expert Parallelism (EP) for Beam’s 23B active experts, Data-Parallel Attention, and DFlash2 speculative decoding. Separating </span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Prefill and Decode (P/D) pools</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> ensures heavy prompt bursts never stall active token generation.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Tiered KV cache (GPU Memory -&gt; DRAM &amp; Local SSD)</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Active KV blocks stay in high-bandwidth GPU memory (</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Tier 1</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">), while warm KV blocks evicted during long-horizon sessions overflow into host CPU DRAM and local NVMe SSDs (</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Tier 2</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">) via vLLM’s OffloadingConnector or SGLang's HiCache, allowing historical context to be fetched in milliseconds instead of recomputed from scratch.</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Across both platforms, layering these optimizations shifts the Pareto frontier to deliver higher output throughput and faster per-user interactivity simultaneously:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Baseline deployment (Stateless round-robin routing).</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> In a standard Kubernetes deployment without prefix awareness, consecutive turns from the same coding session land on different replicas, forcing constant prefill recomputation on historical context and causing throughput to plateau as concurrency scales.</span></p>

<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">KV-cache-aware &amp; flow control routing.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Enabling prefix-aware routing in NVIDIA Dynamo or llm-d’s Sticky EPP with turn-priority flow control keeps multi-turn agent trajectories pinned to the replica holding their warm KV cache, immediately cutting Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) latency and freeing up GPU compute, resulting in a significant increase in token throughput.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Host DRAM and Local SSD (LSSD) KV offloading.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Offloading evicted KV blocks from GPU memory into host DRAM and local NVMe SSDs prevents cache thrashing when dozens of long-context coding sessions exceed on-device GPU memory capacity.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Disaggregated serving, speculative decoding, and KV pool tuning.</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Tuning hybrid KV cache memory allocation, enabling speculative decoding, and disaggregating prefill and decode workers isolates token generation from heavy prompt bursts while the draft model multiplies output throughput to keep per-agent token interactivity fast.</span></p>

<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">仕組み: AutoscalingMetric と KEP-2021 を使用した HPA</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE 内でゼロにスケールする鍵は、2 つの重要なコンポーネントの統合にあります。</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">AutoscalingMetric を使用した HPA:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> これは、Google Cloud Managed Service for Prometheus からの外部シグナルの直接読み取りをサポートするマネージド指標シグナル パイプラインです。AutoscalingMetric を使用した HorizontalPodAutoscaler（HPA）は、Pub/Sub、Cloud Monitoring、ロードバランサのシグナルからオートスケーラーに指標を送信するための、統合された高パフォーマンスのパスを提供します。アダプターの煩雑さを伴わずに済みます。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">KEP-2021:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> HPA で </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">minReplicas: 0 を可能にする</code><a href="https://kubernetes.io/blog/2026/09/02/kubernetes-v1-37-hpa-scale-to-zero-beta/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Kubernetes Enhancement Proposal</span></a> </p>
<p class="p1">に基づいて構築されたこのメカニズムにより、指標がしきい値を下回ると、<span class="s1">HPA </span>はすべての<span class="s1"> Pod </span>を停止できます。また、指標が保留中の作業を示したらすぐに、<span class="s1">HPA </span>が<span class="s1"> Deployment </span>を「起動」できるようにします。</p>
</li>
</ol>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">最初の「ゼロへのスケーリング」ワークロードの構成</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ネイティブなゼロへのスケーリングを実装するには、指標定義と HPA という 2 つの主要なオブジェクトが必要です。次の例では、Pub/Sub サブスクリプションの未配信メッセージ数に基づいてワーカーをスケーリングします。</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">指標のソースを定義する</strong></p>
<p><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">AutoscalingMetric</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> CRD を使用して、外部の Cloud Monitoring 指標をクラスタにマッピングします。</span></p></div>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ゼロへのスケーリング機能は、Cloud Monitoring からの外部指標を GKE でサポートすることで実現しています。</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/autoscale-using-metrics#define-custom-metrics-promql"><code style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AutoscalingMetric</code></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> カスタム リソースを拡張することで、複雑なサードパーティ アダプタを使用せずに、Google Managed Service for Prometheus から指標をクエリできるようになりました。これにより、レイテンシが短縮され、セキュリティが簡素化されます。また、ネイティブな「ゼロへのスケーリング」ワークロードを構成するための重要な基盤となります。このインテグレーションについて詳しくは、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/native-support-for-prometheus-metrics-in-gke"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE での外部指標のネイティブ サポート</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">に関するブログ投稿をご覧ください。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">キャパシティ バッファによる起動レイテンシの管理</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">ゼロからのスケーリングにおける最大の課題は、いわゆるコールド スタートです。これは、GKE がノードをプロビジョニングし、コンテナがそれを pull して起動するまでにかかる時間です。そこで役立つのが</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-standby-buffers-speed-up-autoscaling-for-less-spend?e=0&amp;hl=ja"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE キャパシティ バッファ</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">です。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">キャパシティ バッファは、プールされたウォーム状態のキャパシティとして機能します。GKE は、ゼロにスケールできる複数のワークロードで共有できる少量のウォーム コンピューティング リソースを維持することで、HPA が 0 から 1 にジャンプしたときに、Pod がすぐに要求できるリソースを確保します。これにより、新しい GKE ノードがスピンアップするまでの 60～90 秒の待ち時間がなくなり、起動レイテンシが数分から瞬時に短縮されます。その間、ワークロードの費用はゼロに抑えられます。</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">キャパシティ バッファには、アクティブとスタンバイの 2 種類があります。小さなアクティブ バッファは、ゼロにスケールされた数百のワークロードに対応できます。各ワークロードがレプリカを維持するのではなく、アクティブ バッファがクラスタ全体にサービスを提供するワイルドカード キャパシティとして機能します。スタンバイ バッファはアクティブ バッファに比べて大幅に低コストで、サイズが大きくなるため、クラスタで持続的な負荷が発生した場合にアクティブ バッファを迅速に補充できます。これらを併用することで、即時のスケーリングを実現しながら、低コストも維持できます。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">今後について</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google は、GKE の弾力性に関するロードマップを継続的に拡大しています。たとえば、開発環境を午後 8 時にゼロにスケールダウンし、午前 7 時にスケールアップするとします。繰り返し発生するスケーリングをより細かく制御する方法に注目し、ゼロへのスケーリングの時間枠を事前に定義できるようにしましょう。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ゼロへのスケーリングを今すぐ始めましょう</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">アイドル状態のリソースに料金を支払う時代は終わりを迎えようとしています。イベント ドリブンなワークロードや散発的なワークロードに対して GKE のネイティブなゼロへのスケール機能を有効にすることで、起動パフォーマンスを犠牲にすることなく費用を削減できます。ゼロへのスケールを開始するには、次の手順を行います。</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">アイドル期間を伴う、需要が変動する</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/optimize-workload-resource-utilization"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ワークロードを特定</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/autoscale-using-metrics#define-custom-metrics-promql"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AutoscalingMetric</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> を構成し、</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/tutorials/scale-to-from-zero-hpa"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">minReplicas をゼロに設定</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">クラスタまたはワークロードに</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/capacity-buffer"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">キャパシティ バッファ</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を追加して、応答時間を短縮します。</span></p>
</li>
</ol>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In production systems today, most AI agents operate as lightweight dispatchers. They parse a prompt, make an API call, retrieve a record, and hand back control. Their execution is fast, stateless and cheap. Biotechnology does not work this way. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Folding a protein target, screening a 50,000 compound ligand library, or evaluating structure-activity relationships (SAR) are not quick API lookups. When you ask a general-purpose large language model (LLM) endpoint to coordinate a complex drug discovery workflow, the standard paradigm breaks: Timeouts occur, context windows overflow with atomic coordinates, and proprietary chemical Intellectual Property (IP) risks exposure to external networks. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Building enterprise grade research and development (R&amp;D) agents requires moving past the monolithic assistant model. In this blog post, we will discuss how to use a three-layer architecture: separating cognitive orchestration (the brain) from computational execution (the muscle), and anchoring both inside an enterprise governance plane to build scalable agents for drug discovery use cases in biotech. </span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The execution layer: scaling the muscle - high-compute execution</span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;"> </span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The execution layer is responsible for the heavy scientific computations. The first architectural decision is determining where workloads should run:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Strategic, proprietary pipelines require dedicated GPU infrastructure within your security perimeter</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Routine or standardized workloads can safely be delegated to managed cloud endpoints</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this architecture, protein folding is driven by </span><a href="https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/teams/nim/collections/bionemo-esmfold" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ESMFold</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and generative design by </span><a href="https://docs.nvidia.com/nim/bionemo/genmol/latest/overview.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GenMol</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Both run as </span><a href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/products/nim-microservices/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NVIDIA Inference Microservices</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or NIMs deployed on </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (GKE) using G4 VMs backed by </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/introducing-g4-vm-with-nvidia-rtx-pro-6000"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GPUs.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A critical hurdle in containerized GPU workloads is the cold-start penalty, the latency delay required to pull multi-gigabyte model weights from an image registry and stage them into GPU memory (VRAM).</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To ensure low latency responsiveness, we build the following as a recommendation into the reference architecture:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Maintain warm pools:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Pin the GKE node pool to a minimum of one active node.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Preload weights:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Model weights are loaded directly into VRAM at pod initialization, ensuring containers are immediately ready for inference.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Resilient scheduling:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GKE manages pod lifecycles and restarts failed instances without tearing down the underlying VM infrastructure. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This approach trades custom, manual CUDA kernel tuning for NVIDIA's pre-compiled, containerized runtimes, dramatically reducing infrastructure engineering overhead while ensuring deterministic throughput.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In biotech, chemical structures represent millions of dollars in proprietary IP. A docking request transmits SMILE strings or 3D coordinate matrices that must never leave company custody.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Containerized NIMs eliminate the friction between rapid prototyping and rigorous data isolation. During early exploration, researchers can query NVIDIA hosted endpoints. When transitioning to proprietary lead candidates, the exact same container is deployed to GKE inside a private </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/vpc?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">VPC</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. The agent’s orchestration logic remains identical; only the target URI updates from an external proxy to an internal VPC endpoint. No structural data leaves the enterprise perimeter.</span></p>
<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The cognitive layer: Multi-agent reasoning</span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With compute execution isolated, the cognitive layer manages agentic workflow sequencing. Built on </span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/build/runtime"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Runtime</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, within the Gemini platform, this reference architecture shows a system that deploys a hierarchical multi-agent structure, one lead orchestrator, and two specialized domain sub-agents (Lead Optimization and Safety Screening) . The multi-agent design can be modified to suit the use case at hand. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Based on this design, when an end-user submits a high-level task such as </span><strong style="font-style: italic; vertical-align: baseline;">“ Analyze Candidate 4b against Target X and produce a SAR report”</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, the system applies four core orchestration patterns discussed below; </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">In-process native agent handoffs: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The lead orchestrator delegates tasks to specialist sub-agents using Agent Runtime’s native SDK rather than network-serialized agent-to-agent HTTP protocols. This eliminates intermediate network hops and keeps internal handoffs instantaneous. Only the outermost boundary communicates outward to user-facing surfaces like the </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise?utm_source=google&amp;utm_medium=cpc&amp;utm_campaign=1713762-Gemini_Enterprise-DR-NA-US-en-Google-BKWS-EXA-GEnterprise&amp;utm_content=c-Hybrid+%7C+BKWS+-+MIX+%7C+Txt_Gemini+Enterprise-189528400785&amp;utm_term=gemini+enterprise&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=23370621055&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwl97RBhBWEiwAa9rbXeetAjZcIRGcaAR57VWVWk7ik5DJ9ESqHiDav-2ervoiyzzRZ1ZWyRoCj6sQAvD_BwE&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> agent.</span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Passing bulky payloads by reference: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">LLMs degrade quickly when forced to ingest massive arrays of 3D spatial coordinates. In this architecture, raw structural data (PDB files, docking poses, trajectory logs) is written directly to </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/storage?utm_source=google&amp;utm_medium=cpc&amp;utm_campaign=Cloud-SS-DR-GCP-1713658-GCP-DR-NA-US-en-Google-SKWS-BRO-storage&amp;utm_content=c-Hybrid+%7C+SKWS+-+BRO+%7C+Txt-Infrastructure-Storage-Cloud+Storage-4642545472&amp;utm_term=cloud+storage&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=23767162264&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwl97RBhBWEiwAa9rbXYcqs8qBJehBeWaezdgKd4am7QbV7uo3__CJIZqoa8lqgP8ErM0LkBoCJ3cQAvD_BwE&amp;hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cloud Storage</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. Agents pass lightweight, signed object URIs and high-level structural summaries, keeping context windows compact and inference costs down.</span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Decoupling workflow logic from biochemical reasoning: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">General-purpose LLMs excel at task planning and synthesis, but are prone to hallucinating biochemical nuances. To control this behavior, this architecture maintains strong separation of concerns, namely, workflow and tasks decomposition are handled by Gemini. Domain chemistry and pharmacological reasoning are handled by specialized </span><a href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Nemotron</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> models hosted on dedicated GPU nodes. </span></li>
<li style="text-align: justify;"><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Context-driven evaluation over rigid cutoffs: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Traditional computational screening enforces arbitrary numerical cutoffs (e.g., discard if binding affinity ,-7.5 kcal/mol) However, therapeutic context fundamentally alters risk tolerance. Preventive cardiovascular therapy taken daily by healthy patients requires extremely strict safety margins, or a novel oncology candidate targeting an intractable cancer may accept higher toxicity if efficacy is demonstrated.</span></li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By passing disease context directly into the specialized reasoning models, drug candidates are evaluated against dynamic, target-specific profiles rather than static thresholds, preventing the premature culling of viable drug leads.</span></p>

<h4><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The enterprise governance and asymmetric autonomy</span></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Deploying high-compute agents in enterprise environments requires robust data sovereignty, audit trails, and process control. The </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Gemini platform</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> establishes these safeguards natively with the following components: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/iam/docs/agent-identity-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Identity</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Every autonomous agent operates with a cryptographically verifiable identity. Thus, for a biotech use case, when a candidate molecule is advanced or rejected, the event is permanently logged to that specific agent principal. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/govern/gateways/agent-gateway-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Gateway</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This serves the single ingress/egress point for every interaction within the perimeter. Centralized IAM and regional residency rules help ensure sensitive molecular data remains within designated cloud regions. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/govern/agent-registry"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Agent Registry</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This feature maintains a curated catalog of approved agents, validated tool schemas, and authorized Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, preventing rogue tools from executing in production. </span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="https://cloud.google.com/security/products/model-armor?hl=en"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Model Armor</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: This functionality inspects incoming prompts and outgoing payloads in real time to prevent prompt injection and data exfiltration. </span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff is clear. Identity, RBAC, auditing, residency, and data protection are native properties of the Gemini platform; this allows enterprise customers to focus on science rather than having to build a compliance stack for every project. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In high-stakes research and development, full autonomy introduces unacceptable liability, while requiring approval for trivial steps can create frictions. The solution addressing this to use asymmetric gating in the following ways;</span></p>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The real challenge of building an agentic system isn't the construction, it's the measurement.</span><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Evaluation engineering is a critical foundation for building reliable AI systems. Setting up systematic, repeatable tests to measure your agent’s true behavior can be the difference between shipping a tool you can trust and one that silently breaks, fails to call APIs, or makes mistakes. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">I’ve run evaluation engineering initiatives for years at Google and d</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">eveloping the newly released</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/introducing-the-google-cloud-developer-plugin-for-ai-coding-agents?e=48754805"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Cloud Developer plugin</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> served as a live testbed to stress-test these key principles across real-world developer workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Here are four eval lessons we learned from building the new plugin that you can apply to your own builds.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">1. Grade the execution steps instead of the summary</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When we first evaluated agents, we set up an LLM-as-judge to inspect the agent’s final text reply and scored it against a checklist. However, we quickly realized it’s fundamentally flawed because the grader only sees what the agent wrote, not what it actually did. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When we ran our initial 11-prompt evaluation suite for the Google Cloud Developer plugin, the automated grader marked it as failing three prompts. But when we opened the raw tool-call logs and read the transcripts, the plugin had actually passed all three. On a Cloud Run cleanup prompt, for instance, our rubric required checking command syntax before running anything. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The agent ran </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">gcloud help run services list</span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> as its very first step. It did the work, but its final message was focused on warning the user about permanent data loss rather than reciting that it had checked the help docs.</span></p></div>

If your rubric line says "did it run X?", a LLM judge is guessing unless you evaluate the actual tool-call trajectory.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Don't grade an agent on its closing pitch. Score the raw execution trajectory to verify what commands were actually executed, validated, and scoped.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">2. Design evals for asynchronous operations</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When you start reading tool-call trajectories, how you extract that telemetry matters just as much as what you're reading. Extracting agent telemetry into a flattened, sequential list of events obscures concurrent tool dispatch, which is one of the critical causes of behavioral failure. Modern agent harnesses often batch multiple tool invocations into a single assistant turn using an array of calls.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In our evaluations, when an agent was asked to inspect an environment and look up documentation, the harness dispatched the skill-loading call and two documentation searches simultaneously within a single tool_calls payload. Because activating a skill is a multi-stage process, where the initial call returns a short launch stub and the subsequent turn injects the full instruction body, the instructions meant to govern the search arrived in the prompt context after the system had already executed the searches.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The system never gave the skill a dedicated turn to let its guidance influence the agent's actions. If telemetry pipelines strip out turn structures and merge calls into a flat timeline, engineers will waste days wondering why an agent disregarded unambiguous instructions that a developer embedded in a skill.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Preserve turn structure in telemetry extractors so you can identify when client-side parallel dispatch issues tool calls before injected guidance enters the context window. </span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">3. Test live runs and "dry runs" side-by-side</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You need to inspect real API calls to catch harness quirks, like parallel dispatch. But if you only test your agent in one environment condition, you're flying half-blind. From day one, you need to run two conditions side-by-side: a live run and a plan-only run. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In the live condition, the agent has real, sandboxed credentials and actually executes commands. This is how you see if syntax works, parameters serialize cleanly, and the agent exercises restraint before doing something destructive. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Live runs frequently hit an unexpected permission error on step two and abort before you ever reach the behavior you’re trying to test. That's why you need the plan-only condition, where you strip credentials and tell the agent to explain what it would do without running anything. This strips out permission roadblocks and shows you the model’s pure reasoning and routing intent. Roughly half our most important findings came from the delta between the two.</span></p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For example, on one of our onboarding prompts, a live run was blocked immediately on a project list call. The plan-only run sailed right through and produced an 8,000-character plan that completely ignored organization policies, admin approvals, and governance. The live run masked a major cognitive flaw that the plan-only run exposed in seconds.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Run live and plan-only evaluations side by side. Live runs test actual execution and safety restraint; plan-only runs expose the model's raw reasoning and routing without getting tripped up by permission errors.</span></p>
<h3><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">4. Use “golden sets” as durable baselines</span></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Frontier model architectures change every few months, agent harnesses improve, and plugin manifest standards will inevitably evolve. When building the Google Cloud Developer plugin, we treated evaluations as continuous integration testing for these non-deterministic environments. That means we stress-tested the composite stack, including the harness, the live model, and the tools running together on realistic tasks.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Instead of guessing from the final text output, this approach captures the whole trajectory: what the agent thought, what commands it actually ran, and whether it solved the problem without breaking anything. Then you score that run against golden sets curated by real human experts who know what the ideal path looks like.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Takeaway</strong><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Build golden sets based on what the entire system should actually do in the wild, including your harness, model, and plugins. Models change, but those baseline runs are your safety net.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Reminder: Use evaluation suites over “one-shot” demos</strong></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">A common mistake I see among engineers is what I like to call the one-shot demo. This is when someone feeds a prompt into a LLM, watches it build a complete 3D game or solves a Rubik’s cube in a single pass and calls it an eval. But in reality, this is just a vanity metric. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To do quality work with AI, I truly believe that you need evaluation suites. Without evaluations, teams are like literally running into a dark cave blind. Engineering an enterprise-grade agent means testing the system's ability to handle multi-step interactions, manage permissions, and avoid destructive outcomes like accidentally deleting a project because you asked it to "find opportunities to save costs."</span></p>
