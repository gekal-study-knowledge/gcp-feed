<h2 class="release-note-product-title">Apigee X</h2>
<h3>Announcement</h3>
<p>On October 20th, 2026, we began maintenance updates of Apigee instances <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/api-platform/system-administration/maintenance-windows">configured for maintenance windows</a>.</p>
<p>If you set a preferred window for maintenance for your instance, and your instance version is
below <strong>1-18-0-apigee-6</strong>, your instance will be updated to <strong>1-18-0-apigee-6</strong> within the
next seven to 21 days. A notification containing the expected date of upgrade will be sent within the next two business days.</p>
<aside class="note">Note: Instances that meet either of the following two criteria will <b>not</b> be updated:
<ul>
<li>Your instance has a DNS misconfiguration, as described in <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/release/known-issues">Known Issue 445936920</a>.</li>
<li>Your instance uses an Apigee Java Library that has been removed, as described in <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/release/release-notes#October_16_2025">Apigee release notes dated October 16, 2025</a>.</li>
</ul></aside>
<p>For more information on participating in scheduled maintenance windows, see <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/api-platform/system-administration/maintenance">Maintenance overview</a> and <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/api-platform/system-administration/maintenance-windows">Manage Apigee instance maintenance windows</a>.</p>

### Building full virtual vehicles on Google Cloud: From AAOS to the smallest MCU

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/manufacturing/building-full-virtual-vehicles-on-google-cloud-from-aaos-to-the-smallest-mcu/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/manufacturing/building-full-virtual-vehicles-on-google-cloud-from-aaos-to-the-smallest-mcu/)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 01:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-10-20 04:01:59

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Imagining traditional gearheads getting their hands on software-defined vehicles is an amusing thought experiment. With their socket wrenches and air compressors at the ready, they might be confused to encounter core functions more likely to run on 1s and 0s than oil and belts. But across the auto industry these days, more and more vehicles feature prominent systems built using cloud-native development, continuous integration/continuous delivery, fast digital iteration, and plenty of AI.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">But there’s still a big gap to be found in many automakers’ and Tier 1 suppliers’ cloud-based development processes. On the one hand, these companies are commonly using software to develop vehicles’ digital entertainment and information screens, or other onboard high-performance computers. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">On the other, the dozens of microchips and control modules that make a car a car these days — such as gateways, body controllers, zone control units, sensor and actuator nodes — are still frequently hardwired to physical chips when they’re constructed, making them very difficult to update, reprogram, or expand after the vehicle drives off the factory floor. Teams have to wait for benches, samples, and rigs, and integration can easily slip to the end of the development process, where defects are most expensive to fix.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This inherent inefficiency has become a leading bottleneck. After all, manufacturers can’t really claim vehicles are software-defined if most of their electronics only function when connected to physical hardware and if developers are leaning heavily on actual hardware.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With Horizon, our agentic innovation platform for developing software-defined vehicles, our vision is to help manufacturers avoid these inefficiencies by working with partners like </span><a href="https://www.remotivelabs.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveLabs</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The cockpit already runs in the cloud</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Android team has invested a significant amount of time and effort over the last few years on enabling seamless development of the many digital systems in vehicles’ cockpits. </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/automotive" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Android Automotive OS</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (AAOS) runs inside Google’s official virtual testing environment for Android, </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/devices/cuttlefish" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cuttlefish</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, which itself runs comfortably on</span> <a href="https://cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/arm-on-compute"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Arm-based Google Cloud instances</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. With this approach, developers can build, boot, and test virtual versions of their infotainment stacks on the same Arm architecture that ships in vehicles.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">And with the recent launch of </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/whatsnew/android-17-release#sdv" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Android 17 and AAOS SDV</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, this capability is also extended to further domains, whenever AAOS SDV is used. Deploying it with Cuttlefish, for example, proves the model: representative software, on representative architecture, in the cloud, on demand. The question that still needs to be answered is how to extend that model to the rest of the vehicle, especially when other operating systems are involved.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The harness around the virtual vehicle</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">That’s where </span><a href="https://www.remotivelabs.com/products/remotivetopology" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> comes into play. As the harness around the virtual vehicle, it’s the layer that connects AAOS in Cuttlefish to a full, simulated electrical and electronic (E/E) architecture: the rest of the vehicle’s onboard computers, its data networks, and the live signals that make the system function like a real vehicle rather than a collection of standalone apps.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology automatically utilizes manufacturers’ existing network design files to build a working, virtual version of their cars’ networks. This is vehicle-as-code: the E/E architecture expressed as an executable specification, versioned and provisioned like infrastructure-as-code. In this way, an entire vehicle can be spun up on demand. Cockpit software talks to the rest of the vehicle exactly as it would in a real vehicle. As a result, developers get full and continuous signal and cross-domain visibility, so they can see real-time system status. And because it is built on standard Linux tooling, the same topology runs on a laptop and in a contiguous integration pipeline on Google Cloud.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Integrate across domains early, not late</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff for using this approach is that all onboard systems can be developed and integrated with software before any of them are connected to a vehicle’s hardware. That’s beneficial because the most difficult (and latest) integration in a vehicle’s development is bringing all its systems together. For example, advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) produce object lists, trajectories, and mode requests that the cockpit has to render and react to, while the infotainment system and information cluster have to reflect driving state while body and chassis nodes sit in the loop.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With the full topology live on Google Cloud, the infotainment system and ADAS can operate simultaneously within a virtual vehicle, exchanging real signals over the simulated network. This means it’s possible to exercise handoffs like an ADAS event surfacing in the cluster or a driver-mode change rippling across domains from day one in continuous integration long before the benches and samples line up. In this way, developers can be confident they’ll discover multi-domain integration long before the end of the process so that they can test continuously from the start.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The full vehicle frontier</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">At the heart of this work is a shared desire to bring scalable software development to even the smallest and most common nodes in a vehicle, its microchips and control modules. That’s because these are the parts that have traditionally been wired to hardware, and as a result are the biggest blocker to fully software-defined vehicles.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The ultimate goal is that the same software that runs in the cloud can run on vehicles’ actual control modules with no re-tooling or re-validation needed to move between them. That parity is natural with Arm-based Google Cloud infrastructure because the cloud and the car share an instruction set. Google's </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/products/axion"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Axion</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> makes it straightforward:</span> <a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/axion-based-n4a-vms-now-in-preview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">C4A metal, a bare-metal Arm instance</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, is provided explicitly for automotive systems development and Android software development, giving direct hardware access without unnecessary software layers and delivering the consistent performance that vehicle systems require.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Taken together, this holistic system allows teams, for the first time, to develop and integration-test an entire vehicle’s systems in the cloud and then apply it to the actual vehicle without having to rewrite it.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Why now, and where to start</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff is not abstract. Treating the vehicle as code and moving integration into the cloud compresses the slowest, most expensive phase of a program. Early customer engagements with RemotiveLabs have measured more than 10x faster integration cycles. And the development loop is now changing faster than the hardware loop: AI-assisted and agentic workflows can rapidly generate, test, and refine vehicle software, but only virtual systems provide the required scale. Every element that can’t be virtualized can slow the entire development process down.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology provides this architecture as vehicle-as-code and makes it accessible for developers, testers, and autonomous AI agents alike. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Best of all, there is no new third-party tooling to adopt just to get started. Instead, RemotiveLabs publishes an</span> <a href="https://github.com/remotivelabs/remotivelabs-topology-examples" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">open-source simplified vehicle on GitHub</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> that anyone can run in minutes on standard Linux tooling and platform definitions already in place. Full existing toolchains plug in as a manufacturer or Tier1 supplier scales, and RemotiveTopology can be easily dropped into an Arm-based Google Cloud environment.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For those building software-defined vehicles whose virtual development stops at the cockpit, it’s time to start with an open-source example, bring unique platform definitions, and run an entire vehicle in the cloud. </span><a href="https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/horizon-sdv/tree/main/gitops/modules/remotive-topology" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology is available in Horizon</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> for easy usage in combination with other Horizon modules.</span></p></div>
</details>

Valid records pass to the destination table; corrupted or non-compliant rows trigger </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">logging.error()</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> and return </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">NULL</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> to prevent query failure.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Real-time alerting</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: A Cloud Monitoring log-based metric notifies your team when error rates breach SLA thresholds.</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">SQL triage &amp; replay</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Data engineers query the quarantined payloads in BigQuery using standard SQL over the linked </span><code style="vertical-align: baseline;">_AllLogs</code><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> view, diagnose the root cause, and re-ingest recovered data.</span></p>
</li>
</ol>

### Who judges the judge? Three helpful ways to audit your LLM evaluators

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/three-ways-to-audit-your-llm-evaluators/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/three-ways-to-audit-your-llm-evaluators/)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 01:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-10-20 04:01:59

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Is your LLM-as-a-judge truly objective? </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">There’s a common misconception in tech that humans are subjective, so we should rely on AI for objective evaluation. But the reality is that LLMs are also subjective. Just like human reviewers, they can carry biases:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Verbosity bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: LLM judges usually favor </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">longer, confident-sounding answers</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, even when the underlying facts are wrong.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Position bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Swapping the order of two options can cause an LLM judge to flip its verdict </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">over 75% of the time</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The halo effect</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Because LLMs generate text piece by piece, </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16337" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">clean grammar and formatting can trick an AI judge</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> into giving top marks for accuracy while missing errors.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agreeableness bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: LLM judges are great at recognizing valid work. But with detecting errors, they may fail to catch incorrect, buggy, or unsafe outputs </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11822" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">up to 75% of the time</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Self-enhancement bias:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Also known as egocentric bias, AI evaluators systematically </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17012" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">overrate outputs that were generated by themselves</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or from their own family.</span></li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ultimately, LLM judges are not objective arbiters of truth. They are subjective models with their own distinct biases. Treating them as infallible tools that don't need their quality measured might leave your work compromised. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this post, we’ll explore what’s holding enterprises back from strong evals, and share an easy three-pillar method you can use to improve your quality. </span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Three helpful ways to audit your LLM judges </span></h3>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 1: Measure internal stability</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Before you can trust an AI judge’s score, you need to know that it isn't just guessing. Because LLMs are probabilistic, they can be inconsistent, giving different scores to the exact same text.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You can test this internal stability by running multi-pass consistency checks on a single output and calculating the variance. If a judge's scores fluctuate wildly on identical inputs, its rubric is too ambiguous.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 2: Validate human alignment</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When your evaluation datasets are mostly valid responses, a lenient AI judge can look great on paper. It might align closely with human reviewers overall, but miss a majority of invalid or even unsafe outputs.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To prove your LLM judge is catching real errors, you need chance-corrected metrics. These mathematical models filter out agreements driven purely by leniency or random chance, showing how closely your evaluator actually aligns with the informed decisions of human experts.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 3: Use multi-judge ensembles</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To avoid the halo effect mentioned earlier, teams should use decomposed prompting. Instead of asking one LLM judge to score an entire response, assign a specialized judge to evaluate a single criteria, like factual accuracy or tone. This isolates traits and forces independent scoring.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Second, to track performance in real time without human intervention, the established best practice is ensembling. This means running at least two different models under the hood to evaluate the exact same output simultaneously. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By finding the statistical consensus between these models, you can flag if their evaluations begin to diverge and avoid any scoring quality degradation.</span></p>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Developed in partnership with RadixArk, our native PyTorch backend for </span><a href="https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-torchtpu/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">SGLang</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> accelerates highly structured and agentic workloads on TPUs. <h4><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">llm-d for distributed LLM inference</span></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To run inference engines across large fleets, we are releasing llm-d 1.0 with Red Hat, IBM, and a community of more than 500 contributors and 25 organizations. <h4><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TorchTitan: Distributed high performance training at scale</span></h4>
<p><a href="https://github.com/google-pytorch/torchtitan" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">TorchTitan</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> is the PyTorch-native reference implementation for scalable distributed training and features a production-grade integration with TorchTPU. <div class="block-paragraph"><p>Today at the PyTorch Conference, we released llm-d 1.0, an open-source, Kubernetes-native stack for distributed LLM inference. Co-founded by Google with Red Hat, IBM and others, we worked with a community of more than 500 contributors over 25 organizations on this Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF) Sandbox project, bringing together two important communities: PyTorch, where vLLM and SGLang serve models, and Kubernetes, where distributed systems run and scale in production.</p><p>llm-d 1.0 arrives at a time when agents are changing the nature of inference. A year ago, most inference traffic was chat: one prompt in, one response out. Today, workloads are shifting to multi-step agents and reinforcement learning (RL) rollouts that fan out across tools, share long prefixes, and arrive in bursts. Serving them on a single machine — or behind a generic round-robin load balancer — breaks down fast. Replicas recompute the same prompts, accelerator memory fills up with duplicate state, and one bursty agent loop can stall interactive users.</p><p>llm-d solves this for teams serving their own models by turning a cluster of model servers into a coordinated serving pool. During the PyTorch Conference keynote, we showed this in action with 1,000 sandboxed agents on <a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/bringing-you-agent-sandbox-on-gke-and-agent-substrate">Agent Substrate on GKE</a> hitting a single llm-d endpoint across vLLM, SGLang, and a mixed fleet of Google Cloud TPUs and GPUs. As traffic scaled across the three stages below, cache-aware routing and tiered KV cache kept each agent's steps on the replicas holding its context, while flow control queued bursty agent loops so interactive requests stayed fast.</p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph"><h3><b>What 1.0 provides</b></h3><p>llm-d already delivered prefix- and load-aware routing, KV cache offloading, prefill/decode disaggregation, wide expert parallelism and batch inference across Google Cloud TPUs and GPUs. These capabilities provide:</p><ul><li><b>Multi-tenant quality of service.</b> Priority and fairness-aware routing via llm-d flow control, helping latency-sensitive requests and bursty agent workloads share a fleet to meet SLOs without starving one another.</li><li><b>A stable foundation.</b> v1 APIs and a hardened request path, validated on multiple clouds and accelerator types.</li><li><b>Safer upgrades.</b> Router high availability and zero-downtime updates, alongside <a href="https://llm-d.ai/docs/dev/architecture/model-server-orchestration/disaggregatedset" target="_blank">DisaggregatedSet</a>, which orchestrates prefill/decode-aware rolling updates without dropping context or disrupting active sessions.</li><li><b>Agent-aware serving.</b> Session-aware routing, turn-priority scheduling, and native Responses API state, helping agents reuse context across turns.</li><li><b>Expanded TPU integration</b>. Native PyTorch model serving with TorchTPU co-operative time-slicing to raise throughput and utilization across Google Cloud TPU fleets.</li></ul><p>The components are designed so that you can compose them into your own bespoke production environment. Keep your engine, model and cluster, and adopt only the serving components and configurations you need.</p><h3><b>llm-d under the hood</b></h3><p>Across the areas below, Google engineers worked with the llm-d community to connect upstream Kubernetes primitives, PyTorch serving engines, and accelerator-aware scheduling into a composable stack.</p></div>

<div class="block-paragraph"><p><b>One endpoint, many accelerators</b></p><p>An agent fleet is not one workload. Interactive turns need low latency, background loops and reinforcement learning (RL) rollouts need high throughput, and demand swings by the minute. llm-d puts your accelerator pools behind a single endpoint, with one router, one KV cache index, and one flow-control policy in front of the whole fleet. Agents call a single API; the router picks the replica for each step and keeps a session's context on the replicas that already hold it. Adding capacity, mixing accelerator generations, or moving a model to a different pool becomes a platform change, not an agent rewrite. <a href="https://developers.googleblog.com/torchtpu-running-pytorch-natively-on-tpus-at-google-scale/" target="_blank">TorchTPU</a> brings Google Cloud TPUs into that fleet, running PyTorch models natively inside upstream vLLM and SGLang without model rewrites. For more, see the <a href="https://llm-d.ai/docs/dev/infrastructure/providers/gke#tpu-configuration" target="_blank">llm-d on Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) TPU guide</a>.</p><p><b>Routing that remembers context</b></p><p>The llm-d router builds on Gateway API Inference, the Kubernetes project for inference-optimized routing that we launched. On GKE, <a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-gke-inference-gateway#llm-d-router">GKE Inference Gateway</a> runs the llm-d router as a managed service, simplifying installation and management and <a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/setup-multicluster-inference-gateway">extending routing across regions</a>. The router tracks the replicas holding each cached prefix and weighs load and predicted latency when it picks one. When accelerator memory fills up, rather than dropping it, llm-d uses vLLM’s OffloadingConnector, Mooncake, or SGLang’s HiCache to move KV cache to host memory and then to SSD or Google Cloud Managed Lustre. This setup helps prevent prefix-eviction storms under heavy, multi-turn, and long-context conversational loads across mixed TPU and GPU fleets.</p><p>On an agentic coding workload on Ironwood TPU (TPU 7x), prefix-aware routing plus host memory offload cut median time-to-first-token (TTFT) by more than 85% and raised total throughput 2.4x versus a plain Kubernetes Service, as of October 2026 (<a href="https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d/blob/main/guides/models/qwen3-coder-480b/README.md" target="_blank">source</a>).</p><p><b>Disaggregation that upgrades safely</b></p><p>Prefill is compute-heavy and decode is memory-bound, so running both on the same replicas makes each wait on the other. llm-d runs prefill, decode, and multimodal encode as separate pools, with wide expert parallelism spreading large mixture-of-experts models across nodes. In 1.0, <a href="https://llm-d.ai/docs/dev/architecture/model-server-orchestration/disaggregatedset" target="_blank">DisaggregatedSet</a> — built on LeaderWorkerSet, the Kubernetes API for multi-host inference that we started — manages these pools as one unit. Rolling updates move prefill and decode together on matching versions while each phase scales independently. For more, see the <a href="https://llm-d.ai/docs/well-lit-paths/foundations/pd-disaggregation" target="_blank">disaggregated serving guide</a>.</p><p><b>Async inference that runs alongside real-time inference</b></p><p>For latency-tolerant traffic, llm-d 1.0 introduces <a href="https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d-async" target="_blank">llm-d-async-processor</a>, which pulls requests from queues like Google Cloud Pub/Sub or Redis and routes them alongside real-time traffic. Using queue and pool-dispatch gates, the processor checks available capacity and enforces strict priority lanes and quotas so background jobs run in slack capacity without starving interactive users.</p><p><b>Time-slicing to put idle RL capacity to work</b></p><p>During RL training, accelerators often sit idle 40% to 60% of the time during rollout generation or weight updates. llm-d now supports <a href="https://github.com/llm-d-incubation/llm-d-rl-time-slicing" target="_blank">Google Cloud TPU and GPU time-slicing</a> to interleave async inference into those quiet cycles, raising aggregate accelerator duty cycles from a ~40% baseline to &gt;70% (<a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/introducing-co-operative-time-slicing-for-rl-in-llm-d">source</a>).</p><p><b>Benchmarks you can trust</b></p><p>Tuning a distributed inference stack across routers, KV cache tiers, and prefill/decode pools takes repeatable measurement under realistic multi-turn load. <a href="https://prism.llm-d.ai/" target="_blank">Prism</a> is llm-d’s open-source analysis dashboard for exploring published benchmarks across llm-d’s well-lit paths, backed by standardized <a href="https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d-benchmark" target="_blank">llm-d-benchmark</a> recipes and <a href="https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/inference-perf" target="_blank">inference-perf</a> workloads. Inspect the exact configurations behind the numbers, or upload runs from your own cluster to compare throughput, latency, and cost curves side by side.</p><h3><b>Running llm-d 1.0 on Google Cloud</b></h3><p>llm-d builds on Kubernetes components you may already run: Gateway API, LeaderWorkerSet, Dynamic Resource Allocation, Prometheus, and OpenTelemetry. Running llm-d on Google Cloud brings these pieces together with managed infrastructure:</p><ul><li><a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-gke-inference-gateway#llm-d-router"><b>GKE Inference Gateway</b></a> is the llm-d router operating in Gateway Mode, a managed GKE Gateway that handles the L7 proxy and load balancing and calls the llm-d Endpoint Picker on every request, simplifying installation and management so you don't run the proxy yourself. More importantly, GKE Inference Gateway is how you scale llm-d <a href="https://cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/setup-multicluster-inference-gateway">across clusters and regions</a> with global failover and Model Armor safety checks.</li><li><b>Autoscaling on inference signals</b>. KEDA and Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) scale pools based on queue depth, saturation, and predicted latency, while Kueue enforces per-model quota on shared accelerators.</li><li><b>Mixed accelerator node pools</b>. Google Cloud TPU and GPU pools run behind the same llm-d endpoint, deployed with verified Helm charts.</li><li><b>KV cache offloading across layers</b>. Google Cloud Managed Lustre provides a shared remote tier beneath local TPU and GPU HBM and host CPU RAM so long-context workloads run without exhausting accelerator memory.</li></ul><h3><b>In production</b></h3><p>This GKE llm-d stack carries production traffic, and it has been tested at fleet scale:</p><ul><li>Gemini platform (formerly Vertex AI) serves production traffic through GKE Inference Gateway, doubling its prefix cache hit rate from 35% to 70%, cutting TTFT by more than 35% for coding agents and cutting P95 TTFT by more than 50% under bursty chat traffic (<a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/how-gke-inference-gateway-improved-latency-for-vertex-ai">source</a>).</li><li>In a published benchmark across a 17,000-node multi-region GKE deployment serving a mixture-of-experts model in the US and Europe, multi-cluster GKE Inference Gateway and the llm-d router scaled throughput 2.9x across three clusters with a 99.9% success rate and less than 1% routing overhead (<a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/gpu-and-tpu-utilization-with-multi-cluster-gke-inference-gateway">source</a>).</li><li>In a published benchmark on A3 Mega cluster serving a 70B-parameter model, llm-d with multi-connector KV cache offloading to Google Cloud Managed Lustre in addition to Host CPU RAM, delivered an approximately 40% improvement in TTFT and a 30% reduction in end-to-end latency responding to 50k-token prompts compared to CPU RAM offload only, while reducing GPU-hour requirements by nearly 60% (<a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/scaling-llm-inference-multi-node-kv-cache-offloading-with-gke-managed-lustre">source</a>).</li></ul><p>Organizations running llm-d on Google Cloud are putting these same capabilities to work in different ways, for instance prefix-aware routing to raise cache hit rates, open interfaces that fit an existing service mesh, and flow control to hold latency for priority tiers when traffic spikes.</p><p></p></div>

<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">企業や個人の開発者は、複数の AI 推論モデルを頻繁に実行しています。適切なアーキテクチャを使用することで、モデルの呼び出し方法を簡素化すると同時に、一元化されたガバナンスを提供できます。この投稿では、ネットワークにおける AI 推論モデルの提供に焦点を当てた 2 つのリファレンス アーキテクチャについて説明します。1 つは</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/kubernetes-engine-overview"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine（GKE）</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">用、もう 1 つはその他のすべてのバックエンド タイプ用です。まず、後ほど紹介するリファレンス アーキテクチャの共通点について説明します。次に、GKE バックエンドのアーキテクチャに固有のコンポーネントについて説明し、最後に、すべてのバックエンド タイプに共通するアーキテクチャの要素を確認します。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">エントリ ポイント</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">推論呼び出しのフロントエンドとして機能する、高い安定性、セキュリティ、信頼性を持つエントリ ポイントの背後にモデルのデプロイを公開できます。このエントリ ポイントは、ポリシー、セキュリティ、ロジックを適用できる制御ゾーンとしても機能します。Cloud ロードバランサと推論ゲートウェイはどちらもエントリ ポイント機能を提供します。これらのタイプのエンドポイントは、TLS によるセキュアな接続の終端、API 管理コンポーネントとの統合、Service Extensions による機能の拡張、Model Armor の機能を活用したセキュリティ強化が可能です。</span></p>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">設計における共通サービス</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">どちらのリファレンス設計でも、次のサービスを使用します。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/vpc/docs/private-service-connect#endpoints"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Private Service Connect 推論エンドポイント</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: 消費者の Virtual Private Cloud（VPC）ネットワーク内にエントリ ポイントを固定します。トラフィックはプライベート内部</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/vpc/docs/subnets#valid-ranges"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">IP アドレス</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">に到達するため、推論呼び出しはプライベート ネットワーク内に保持されます。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/api-platform/get-started/what-apigee"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Apigee API Management</strong></a><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">（省略可）</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Apigee Extension Processor コールアウトを介して統合され、リクエストがコンピューティング リソースに到達する前に、クライアント ID の検証、レート制限、割り当ての適用を処理します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/model-armor/overview"><strong style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Model Armor</strong></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: インライン AI 安全性チェックポイントとして機能し、プロンプト インジェクションや機密データの漏洩がないか、プロンプトと出力補完をスクリーニングします。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">推論プール</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: 同じモデルのレプリカを含む論理グループ。Gateway はプロンプトを受け取ると、HTTPRoute ルールを評価し、モデル識別子に基づいて適切な推論プールを選択します。プールには初期サイズがあり、動的に自動スケーリングされるように構成できます。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">モデル レプリカセット</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: シングルノードまたはマルチノードの GPU または TPU ノードプールにデプロイされた個々のモデルレプリカ（推論サーバー インスタンス）。レプリカセットは、これらのモデルレプリカの均一なグループを表します。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">GKE のトラフィック フローの例</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">この GKE ベースのアーキテクチャを使用してバックエンド モデルを呼び出すクライアント アプリケーションは、次のようなフローをたどります。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">内向き</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: コンシューマー VPC 内のクライアント アプリケーションが、ローカルの Private Service Connect エンドポイントに対して OpenAI 互換の API 呼び出しを発行し、リージョン内部アプリケーション ロードバランサを使用して GKE Inference Gateway に直接ルーティングします。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ペイロード検査</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Gateway は、リクエスト本文で指定されたターゲット モデル パラメータを読み取り、HTTP ヘッダーに追加します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">コントロール プレーンの検証</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Apigee が使用されている場合、クライアントの認証情報と割り当てがチェックされます。Model Armor がプロンプトをスクリーニングし、ポリシー違反やデータ漏洩がないかを確認します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">バックエンドの選択</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Gateway は HTTPRoute マッピングを評価してターゲット プールを特定し、プレフィックス キャッシュ共有コンテキストを照合して、リアルタイムの Prometheus データに基づいて最も負荷の低い GPU または TPU レプリカにルーティングします。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">外向き</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: レプリカが推論ワークロードを実行します。出力トークンは Model Armor を通過して最終的なレスポンス検証が行われ、その後、プライベート接続を介してストリーミングで送り返されます。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

</ul>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">すべてのバックエンドのトラフィック フローの例</strong></h4>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">このアーキテクチャを使用してバックエンド モデルを呼び出すクライアント アプリケーションは、次のようなフローをたどります。</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プライベート内向き</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: クライアント アプリケーションは、プライベート IP アドレス空間を介して Private Service Connect エンドポイントをターゲットにします。リージョン内部アプリケーション ロードバランサがリクエストを受信します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">モデル名の抽出</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: ロードバランサは、ペイロードを Cloud Run 本文ベースのルーター コールアウトに送信します。このコールアウトは、JSON ペイロードを検査して X-Gateway-Model-Name ヘッダーを挿入します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ポリシーと安全性の適用</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: リクエストは Apigee に渡されて ID と割り当てが検証され、その後 Model Armor に渡されて機密データが削除され、悪意のあるプロンプトがブロックされます。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">NEG ルーティング</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: ロードバランサの URL マップがモデルヘッダーを検査し、一致するバックエンド NEG（Agent Platform、GKE、Cloud Run、ハイブリッド、インターネット）にリクエストを転送します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プライベート配信</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: ターゲット バックエンドがモデル プロンプトを実行し、Model Armor が補完をスクリーニングして、結果が内向きパスに沿ってプライベートに返されます。</span></p>
</li>
</ol></div>

<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロダクト、テクノロジー、ツールに関する最新情報</strong></h4>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Google Kubernetes Engine の更新:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GKE チームはプラットフォームのスケーラビリティの向上に全力を注いでいます。9 月には、さまざまな形や規模で改善が行われました。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新機能: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">エージェント ワークロードに、より高密度の実行ランタイムが必要ですか？新しいオープンソースの</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/agent-substrate-available-on-gke?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE Agent Substrate</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> は、標準的なコンテナ ランタイムよりも 10 倍高い密度で数百万のサンドボックスを実行できるように設計されています。また Agent Substrate は、ネイティブのゼロトラスト カーネルとネットワーク分離により、1 秒あたり 500 回を超える一時停止 / 再開アクティベーションを実行しながら、500 ミリ秒未満での再開オペレーションを実現します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロダクトの最新情報:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> GKE に</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-adds-native-scale-to-zero-capabilities?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">ゼロへのスケーリング</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">機能が組み込まれました。ワークロードをスケールダウンするために複雑なコンポーネントを構成する必要はありません。AutoscalingMetric を使用した HPA と KEP-2021 のサポートにより、その作業はすぐに自動的に行われます。詳細については、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-adds-native-scale-to-zero-capabilities?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちらのブログ記事をお読みください</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロダクトの最新情報: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">さらに、GKE HPA（前述の AutoscalingMetric を使用）では、標準指標に加えてカスタム PromQL 指標に基づいてスケールアップとスケールダウンを行えるようになりました。これにより、ビジネスにとって意味のある独自の条件に従ってワークロードをトリガーできます。詳しくは</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/native-support-for-prometheus-metrics-in-gke?e=48754805"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新機能: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">もう一つのスケーラビリティ機能は</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/pod-snapshots"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE Pod スナップショット</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">です。これは、CPU や GPU のメモリを含むワークロードの実行状態を保存し、オンデマンドで復元できるようにするものです。内部テストによると、GKE Pod スナップショットにより、AI 推論の起動時間を最大 89% 短縮できます。詳しくは</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-pod-snapshots"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しい移行ツール: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">最後に、コンテナ ワークロードを AWS EKS から GKE に移行したいと考えていたものの、困難で摩擦の多いエンジニアリング作業を恐れていた方のために、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-agentic-migration?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE エージェント型移行</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をリリースしました。これは、不安定なアドホック プロンプトに代わり、決定論的なガードレールで保護された AI 支援移行パイプラインを提供する専用のエージェント プラグインです。エージェント スキルとローカルの Model Context Protocol（MCP）サーバーを組み合わせた設計になっており、AI を使用して複雑な AWS EKS IaC と Kubernetes マニフェストを GKE ランディング ゾーンに直接変換します。</span><a href="https://github.com/gke-labs/gke-agentic-migration/blob/main/docs/onboarding-guide.md" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">オンボーディング ガイド</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を確認して利用を開始しましょう。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">機能の更新: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">強化学習（RL）と評価のワークロードは非常に複雑です。標準的なエージェント RL ループでは、LLM ポリシーが GPU 上でコード スニペットなどのアクションを生成し、分離された CPU サンドボックス内で実行して報酬シグナルを観察します。しかし、このループをスケールアップして数万の並列ロールアウトをサポートしようとすると、アクセラレータのアイドル状態、イメージのカーディナリティ、コントロール プレーンの飽和など、インフラストラクチャのボトルネックが発生します。そこで、RL 向けに最適化された GKE Agent Sandbox に加えて、Agent Sandbox RL オーケストレーション SDK、一般的な RL ジムやハーネスとのネイティブ連携機能を開発しました。すべて一般提供が開始されています。詳しくは</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/accelerate-agentic-rl-with-gke-agent-sandbox?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ストレージの最新情報: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">AI は大量のデータをトレーニングして作成します。そのデータは、ブロック ストレージ システム、ファイル システム、オブジェクト ストア、データベースのいずれかに保存する必要があります。この重要なレイヤがエージェントの要求に対応できるよう、ストレージ ポートフォリオの強化を発表しました。</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロダクトの最新情報:</strong><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/storage-data-transfer/filestore-agent-volumes?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Filestore エージェント ボリューム</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">は、エージェント ワークロード向けに高パフォーマンスで柔軟な永続ファイル ストレージを提供します。GKE Agent Substrate および GKE Agent Sandbox との緊密なインテグレーションにより、Filestore エージェント ボリュームは、分離された専用ファイル ワークスペースを GKE Agent Sandbox にミリ秒単位で自動的に割り当て、アタッチします。プレビュー版へのアクセスは</span><a href="http://forms.gle/vYPkcFiZVoTjf7Ah7" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">からリクエストできます。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しいプロダクト:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 生成 AI と RAG のデータレイヤ（Milvus、Pinecone、Qdrant、Vespa、Redis、インメモリ コンテキスト キャッシュなど）を実行している場合は、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/compute/compute-engine-m4n-vms?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">M4N ファミリーの VM</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。一般提供が開始されたこれらの VM は、主要なハイパースケーラーの中でコアあたりの IOPS とスループットが最高です。M4N インスタンスは、Google Cloud のカスタム</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/titanium"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Titanium</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> オフロード アーキテクチャおよび </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/hyperdisks"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Hyperdisk Extreme</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> と組み合わせることで、ホストあたり合計で最大 25,000 MiB/秒（25 GiB/秒）のストレージ スループットと最大 100 万 IOPS を実現します。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しいプロダクト:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> もう一つの新しい Compute Engine プロダクトである</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/compute/storage-optimized-z4d-vm-and-bare-metal-instances?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Z4D の一般提供が開始</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">され、AI / ML のトレーニングおよび推論ワークロード向けの強力なストレージ ソリューションとしてご利用いただけるようになりました。ベアメタル インスタンスとして構成すると、Z4D はエージェント型マイクロ VM に必要な大容量のローカル SSD（LSSD）と低レイテンシの両方を提供するため、ホストごとに数千の分離されたサンドボックスをネイティブのパフォーマンスと効率で実行できます。Z4D マシンの詳細は</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/compute/storage-optimized-z4d-vm-and-bare-metal-instances?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">でご確認ください。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">プロダクトの最新情報: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">現在の AI トレーニングや推論のパイプラインは、ほとんどのストレージ管理システムが対応できないほどのスピードでデータを作成するため、ストレージ資産を把握しようとするチームにとって課題となっています。</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/storage-intelligence/advisor-overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Storage Intelligence アドバイザー</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">の新バージョンでは、「バケットには何が入っているか？」という疑問の答えが簡単にわかり、予期しない変更をすばやく特定できます。さらに、強化された</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/batch-operations/overview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">バッチ オペレーション</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">により、バケット全体にわたる一括変更、たとえば、ストレージ クラスの移動、古いデータや一時データの一括削除、メタデータ、タグ付け、保持、暗号化の変更の適用などを自動化できます。Storage Intelligence アドバイザーの最新情報については、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/storage-data-transfer/storage-intelligence-advisor-and-batch-operations-updates/"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちら</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">をご覧ください。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">新しいプロダクト:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 最後に、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/products/alloydb"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">AlloyDB for PostgreSQL</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> の新バージョンは、Google の先駆的なインフラストラクチャである Colossus 分散ファイル システムと Jupiter ネットワークを統合し、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/databases/alloydbs-agentic-database-architecture?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">エージェントの時代における妥協のない新しいデータベース アーキテクチャ</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">を実現します。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<h4><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">実践ガイドとハウツー</strong></h4>
<ul>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ハウツー:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> 世界中に分散している GPU と TPU を、単一のエントリ ポイントの背後にある単一のプールとして動作させたいとお考えの場合は、</span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/products/containers-kubernetes/gpu-and-tpu-utilization-with-multi-cluster-gke-inference-gateway?e=48754805&amp;hl=ja"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">こちらのブログ記事</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">でその方法をご確認いただけます。エッジでは、</span><a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/setup-multicluster-inference-gateway"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">マルチクラスタ GKE Inference Gateway</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> が、グローバルなマルチリージョン トラフィック分配と高可用性を担います。その下では、</span><a href="https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d-router" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">LLM-d ルーター</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">が、使用率を高く維持するための複雑なメモリ認識型スケジューリング アルゴリズムを処理します。このアーキテクチャは、ランタイム、モデル、アクセラレータに依存しないように意図的に設計されており、テストでは、マルチクラスタ GKE Inference Gateway を介してトラフィックをルーティングしたところ、オーバーヘッドは 1% 未満でした。</span></p>
</li>
<li style="vertical-align: baseline;">
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">ガイド: </strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">AI モデルのトレーニングや推論に TPU 上の GKE を使用されているか、使用される予定ですか？巨大な大規模言語モデル（LLM）のトレーニングや、高スループットの推論サービングの実行には、Cloud TPU や Cloud GPU などの特殊な AI ハードウェアへの多額の投資が必要です。費用対効果を最大化するには、</span><a href="https://discuss.google.dev/t/a-tale-of-tpu-observability-on-gke-part-1/397955" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">GKE のワークロード ライフサイクル指標を理解する</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">必要があります。この詳細なガイドでは、不透明なクラスタの動作を実用的なテレメトリーに変換する方法を説明します。</span></p>
</li>
</ul>

### Building full virtual vehicles on Google Cloud: From AAOS to the smallest MCU

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Imagining traditional gearheads getting their hands on software-defined vehicles is an amusing thought experiment. With their socket wrenches and air compressors at the ready, they might be confused to encounter core functions more likely to run on 1s and 0s than oil and belts. But across the auto industry these days, more and more vehicles feature prominent systems built using cloud-native development, continuous integration/continuous delivery, fast digital iteration, and plenty of AI.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">But there’s still a big gap to be found in many automakers’ and Tier 1 suppliers’ cloud-based development processes. On the one hand, these companies are commonly using software to develop vehicles’ digital entertainment and information screens, or other onboard high-performance computers. </span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">On the other, the dozens of microchips and control modules that make a car a car these days — such as gateways, body controllers, zone control units, sensor and actuator nodes — are still frequently hardwired to physical chips when they’re constructed, making them very difficult to update, reprogram, or expand after the vehicle drives off the factory floor. Teams have to wait for benches, samples, and rigs, and integration can easily slip to the end of the development process, where defects are most expensive to fix.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">This inherent inefficiency has become a leading bottleneck. After all, manufacturers can’t really claim vehicles are software-defined if most of their electronics only function when connected to physical hardware and if developers are leaning heavily on actual hardware.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With Horizon, our agentic innovation platform for developing software-defined vehicles, our vision is to help manufacturers avoid these inefficiencies by working with partners like </span><a href="https://www.remotivelabs.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveLabs</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The cockpit already runs in the cloud</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The Android team has invested a significant amount of time and effort over the last few years on enabling seamless development of the many digital systems in vehicles’ cockpits. </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/automotive" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Android Automotive OS</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> (AAOS) runs inside Google’s official virtual testing environment for Android, </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/devices/cuttlefish" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Cuttlefish</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, which itself runs comfortably on</span> <a href="https://cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/arm-on-compute"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Arm-based Google Cloud instances</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">. With this approach, developers can build, boot, and test virtual versions of their infotainment stacks on the same Arm architecture that ships in vehicles.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">And with the recent launch of </span><a href="https://source.android.com/docs/whatsnew/android-17-release#sdv" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Android 17 and AAOS SDV</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, this capability is also extended to further domains, whenever AAOS SDV is used. Deploying it with Cuttlefish, for example, proves the model: representative software, on representative architecture, in the cloud, on demand. The question that still needs to be answered is how to extend that model to the rest of the vehicle, especially when other operating systems are involved.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The harness around the virtual vehicle</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">That’s where </span><a href="https://www.remotivelabs.com/products/remotivetopology" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> comes into play. As the harness around the virtual vehicle, it’s the layer that connects AAOS in Cuttlefish to a full, simulated electrical and electronic (E/E) architecture: the rest of the vehicle’s onboard computers, its data networks, and the live signals that make the system function like a real vehicle rather than a collection of standalone apps.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology automatically utilizes manufacturers’ existing network design files to build a working, virtual version of their cars’ networks. This is vehicle-as-code: the E/E architecture expressed as an executable specification, versioned and provisioned like infrastructure-as-code. In this way, an entire vehicle can be spun up on demand. Cockpit software talks to the rest of the vehicle exactly as it would in a real vehicle. As a result, developers get full and continuous signal and cross-domain visibility, so they can see real-time system status. And because it is built on standard Linux tooling, the same topology runs on a laptop and in a contiguous integration pipeline on Google Cloud.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Integrate across domains early, not late</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff for using this approach is that all onboard systems can be developed and integrated with software before any of them are connected to a vehicle’s hardware. That’s beneficial because the most difficult (and latest) integration in a vehicle’s development is bringing all its systems together. For example, advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) produce object lists, trajectories, and mode requests that the cockpit has to render and react to, while the infotainment system and information cluster have to reflect driving state while body and chassis nodes sit in the loop.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">With the full topology live on Google Cloud, the infotainment system and ADAS can operate simultaneously within a virtual vehicle, exchanging real signals over the simulated network. This means it’s possible to exercise handoffs like an ADAS event surfacing in the cluster or a driver-mode change rippling across domains from day one in continuous integration long before the benches and samples line up. In this way, developers can be confident they’ll discover multi-domain integration long before the end of the process so that they can test continuously from the start.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The full vehicle frontier</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">At the heart of this work is a shared desire to bring scalable software development to even the smallest and most common nodes in a vehicle, its microchips and control modules. That’s because these are the parts that have traditionally been wired to hardware, and as a result are the biggest blocker to fully software-defined vehicles.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The ultimate goal is that the same software that runs in the cloud can run on vehicles’ actual control modules with no re-tooling or re-validation needed to move between them. That parity is natural with Arm-based Google Cloud infrastructure because the cloud and the car share an instruction set. Google's </span><a href="https://cloud.google.com/products/axion"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">Axion</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> makes it straightforward:</span> <a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/axion-based-n4a-vms-now-in-preview"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">C4A metal, a bare-metal Arm instance</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, is provided explicitly for automotive systems development and Android software development, giving direct hardware access without unnecessary software layers and delivering the consistent performance that vehicle systems require.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Taken together, this holistic system allows teams, for the first time, to develop and integration-test an entire vehicle’s systems in the cloud and then apply it to the actual vehicle without having to rewrite it.</span></p>
<h3><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Why now, and where to start</strong></h3>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">The payoff is not abstract. Treating the vehicle as code and moving integration into the cloud compresses the slowest, most expensive phase of a program. Early customer engagements with RemotiveLabs have measured more than 10x faster integration cycles. And the development loop is now changing faster than the hardware loop: AI-assisted and agentic workflows can rapidly generate, test, and refine vehicle software, but only virtual systems provide the required scale. Every element that can’t be virtualized can slow the entire development process down.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology provides this architecture as vehicle-as-code and makes it accessible for developers, testers, and autonomous AI agents alike. </span><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Best of all, there is no new third-party tooling to adopt just to get started. Full existing toolchains plug in as a manufacturer or Tier1 supplier scales, and RemotiveTopology can be easily dropped into an Arm-based Google Cloud environment.</span></p>
<p><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">For those building software-defined vehicles whose virtual development stops at the cockpit, it’s time to start with an open-source example, bring unique platform definitions, and run an entire vehicle in the cloud. </span><a href="https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/horizon-sdv/tree/main/gitops/modules/remotive-topology" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">RemotiveTopology is available in Horizon</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> for easy usage in combination with other Horizon modules.</span></p></div>

### Who judges the judge? Three helpful ways to audit your LLM evaluators

- **Link**: [https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/three-ways-to-audit-your-llm-evaluators/](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/three-ways-to-audit-your-llm-evaluators/)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 01:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-10-20 04:02:03

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<div class="block-paragraph_advanced"><p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Is your LLM-as-a-judge truly objective? </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">There’s a common misconception in tech that humans are subjective, so we should rely on AI for objective evaluation. But the reality is that LLMs are also subjective. Just like human reviewers, they can carry biases:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Verbosity bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: LLM judges usually favor </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">longer, confident-sounding answers</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">, even when the underlying facts are wrong.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Position bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Swapping the order of two options can cause an LLM judge to flip its verdict </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">over 75% of the time</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">The halo effect</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: Because LLMs generate text piece by piece, </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16337" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">clean grammar and formatting can trick an AI judge</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> into giving top marks for accuracy while missing errors.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Agreeableness bias</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">: LLM judges are great at recognizing valid work. But with detecting errors, they may fail to catch incorrect, buggy, or unsafe outputs </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11822" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">up to 75% of the time</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">.</span></li>
<li><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Self-enhancement bias:</strong><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> Also known as egocentric bias, AI evaluators systematically </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17012" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration: underline; vertical-align: baseline;">overrate outputs that were generated by themselves</span></a><span style="vertical-align: baseline;"> or from their own family.</span></li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Ultimately, LLM judges are not objective arbiters of truth. They are subjective models with their own distinct biases. Treating them as infallible tools that don't need their quality measured might leave your work compromised. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">In this post, we’ll explore what’s holding enterprises back from strong evals, and share an easy three-pillar method you can use to improve your quality. </span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Three helpful ways to audit your LLM judges </span></h3>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 1: Measure internal stability</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Before you can trust an AI judge’s score, you need to know that it isn't just guessing. Because LLMs are probabilistic, they can be inconsistent, giving different scores to the exact same text.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">You can test this internal stability by running multi-pass consistency checks on a single output and calculating the variance. If a judge's scores fluctuate wildly on identical inputs, its rubric is too ambiguous.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 2: Validate human alignment</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">When your evaluation datasets are mostly valid responses, a lenient AI judge can look great on paper. It might align closely with human reviewers overall, but miss a majority of invalid or even unsafe outputs.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To prove your LLM judge is catching real errors, you need chance-corrected metrics. These mathematical models filter out agreements driven purely by leniency or random chance, showing how closely your evaluator actually aligns with the informed decisions of human experts.</span></p>
<p><strong style="vertical-align: baseline;">Pillar 3: Use multi-judge ensembles</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">To avoid the halo effect mentioned earlier, teams should use decomposed prompting. Instead of asking one LLM judge to score an entire response, assign a specialized judge to evaluate a single criteria, like factual accuracy or tone. This isolates traits and forces independent scoring.</span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Second, to track performance in real time without human intervention, the established best practice is ensembling. This means running at least two different models under the hood to evaluate the exact same output simultaneously. </span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">By finding the statistical consensus between these models, you can flag if their evaluations begin to diverge and avoid any scoring quality degradation.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: justify;"><span style="vertical-align: baseline;">Get started</span></h3>
